💾 Phone Memory Prices Jump 263% — and Samsung Raises Galaxy A Series Prices!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Samsung's financial report for the third quarter revealed that the average purchase price of mobile phone memory jumped 263% during the first nine months of 2026 compared with the 2025 average — meaning the price is now more than three and a half times higher. The company's device division spent 7.7 trillion won (about $5.2 billion) on memory bought from outside suppliers, 12.3% of its total raw-material purchases, with the main suppliers being the American company "Micron" and the Japanese company "Kioxia".

The cause is the AI data-center boom: expanding investment in these centers first drove up demand for server memory, and then the price wave spread to phone memory. By contrast, phone processor prices rose only 8% over the same period, and camera module prices stayed almost flat, meaning memory alone is squeezing phone costs. Samsung responded by raising the average selling price of its phones by 13%, focusing on premium models like the "Galaxy S26" and "Galaxy Z Fold 8".

But the pressure showed in profits: although the phone division's revenue rose to 37.3 trillion won in the third quarter, the phone and network divisions together posted an operating loss of 600 billion won. Samsung is preparing to raise the prices of eight models in its mid-range and budget "Galaxy A" series by up to 19% starting January 1, 2027, so the "Galaxy A17", for example, would rise from $269 to $319. Memory now accounts for 54% of the cost of components in cheap phones, up from just 20% a year ago.

Simply put: when AI data centers compete with your phone for the same memory chip, the chip's price goes up — and in the end, the phone buyer pays for it.
