📱 OnePlus 16 may finally reach India — but at a price nearly 60% higher than in China!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

OnePlus launched its flagship OnePlus 16 in China on October 12, and since then the phone has been confined to the Chinese market, after the company halted new phone launches in North America and Europe.

The company confirmed it will not launch the phone in North America and Europe, having wound down operations in those two regions and integrated further with its parent company Oppo — to the point that the OxygenOS system was replaced by Oppo's ColorOS.

India, though, remained a question mark: trusted sources initially said the phone would not launch in the Indian market, and that the company might settle for launching the cheaper OnePlus 16R instead.

Advertisement

But new reports flipped the story on November 6: well-known leaker Abhishek Yadav said the company held a closed meeting in Mumbai about launching the OnePlus 16 in India, and that the phone is being secretly tested inside the country, while another leaker said the company will soon launch two phones in India, one of which is almost certainly the OnePlus 16.

The company has not confirmed any of this officially: no announced launch date, no official price, not even confirmation that the phone is coming to India at all. All of the above is leaks from leakers and the Indian tech press.

The biggest shock, though, is the expected price: leaks suggest the phone could cost in India between 120,000 and 140,000 rupees — about $1,383 to $1,490 — compared with about $852 in China. That is nearly 60% higher, and about 53% higher than the price of the OnePlus 15 in India last year.

Observers attribute this jump to the global memory crisis, which has raised component costs.

The phone's specs themselves are confirmed: a 9,000 mAh battery, 100W wired and 50W wireless charging, a 185Hz display, a Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 processor, up to 16 GB of memory and up to 1 TB of storage.

Simply put: OnePlus's strongest phone may finally reach India, but at a price that turns the "flagship killer" equation upside down. Price alone may decide the phone's fate before its specs do.

Would you buy a flagship phone at a price nearly 60% higher than in China, or wait for another phone?
