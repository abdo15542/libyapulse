🏫 Google to Schools: Accept AI or Lose Search... and the Schools Answer Back!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

On November 3, Google announced that AI tools and the search service will be switched off by default for school student accounts at the start of 2027, and that every school administration must turn them on itself. But the company explained that search and AI are deeply merged, so anyone who refuses AI will lose search along with it.

The decision sparked a wide wave of objections, as three of the four largest school districts in America — New York, Los Angeles, and Chicago — are considering abandoning Google and using other search engines. Los Angeles disabled AI summaries in search, while New York schools are living under a one-year pause on student-facing AI tools, and Chicago has banned tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini since December 2025.

Some districts did not wait for the decision: schools in Fairfax County, Virginia, developed a browser extension that hides AI-generated answers from students' results, and schools in Niles, Michigan, published a similar free extension for everyone. School web filtering companies also announced products that block AI summaries.

Senator Ed Markey sent a letter to the chief executive demanding a reversal, setting November 30 as the deadline for a reply. The mayor of New York described the move as an attempt to force AI on public schools, while education organizations see giving schools an off switch as a step in the right direction.

Simply put: search without AI or no search at all; so does a company have the right to tie a basic service to accepting a new technology, or should the decision stay in the school's hands?
