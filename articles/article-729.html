🚨 AI Discovers 100,000 Flaws in One Year... and the Outages Have 'Only Just Begun'!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A wide analysis published on November 21 drew a worrying conclusion from the week of outages that hit the world: AI now discovers software flaws at a speed the internet has never known, and the race to fix them has itself become a cause of outages. The number of flaws discovered in 2026 passed 100,000, about four times what was recorded in 2022, the last full year before large language models spread, and more than double the total for 2024 and 2025 combined. In October alone, more than 17,000 new flaws were recorded, compared with fewer than 5,000 in October 2025.

During that same week, Microsoft 365 and Outlook services went down, along with Apple services, six American banks, United Airlines flights, and the National Security Agency's classified communications network. Each outage had an ordinary cause: a faulty update, a backup test, or a hidden error, but the common thread was the pressure to fix things fast: Microsoft alone fixed 3,554 flaws in two and a half months before its outage, and PHP reached government websites and shut itself down as a precaution before attackers arrived.

Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince warned that what is coming is harder, saying it will 'look like the sky is falling for two years', because AI tools discover flaws and turn them into attacks within hours, while security teams do not have the same time to fix them. But in the end he sees a positive side: every flaw discovered and fixed makes software safer in the long run, and AI itself may later help defenders.

Simply put: AI finds flaws faster than we can fix them, and a rushed fix itself can stop your service; so do you think two years of outages is an acceptable price for a safer internet, or is the solution somewhere else?
