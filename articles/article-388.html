🏦 'MoonPay' Applies to Create a US Bank — Stablecoins Enter the Banking System!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Digital payments company "MoonPay" has announced it has applied to the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to create the "MoonPay National Trust Bank", in a move making it the latest crypto company seeking a federal banking license. The application is dated November 17, was received by the office on November 18, and was announced on November 20.

The proposed bank, headquartered in Miami Beach, Florida, would be a non-depository trust bank: it would not accept traditional deposits or make loans, and would not seek federal deposit insurance, but would operate as a member of the Federal Reserve System. Its services would include custody of digital assets for customers, issuing and redeeming stablecoins and managing their reserves, and transfer services as an agent.

The move comes before the "GENIUS" Act takes effect on January 18, 2027 — the law that will set federal standards for stablecoin issuers, including reserve and redemption rules. "MoonPay" follows companies that came before it: "Circle" received final approval in July 2026, and "Ripple" in October, while "Coinbase"'s license took effect at the start of the month. The company had obtained a New York "BitLicense" in June 2025 and a state trust charter in November 2025.

Simply put: crypto companies used to work outside banks; now they ask to become banks themselves — but without deposits or loans, instead custodying assets and issuing stablecoins.
