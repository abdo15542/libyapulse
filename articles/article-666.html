📱 The Lancet Psychiatry report: infinite scrolling and autoplay are a global public-health problem!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

An international commission, which published its report in The Lancet Psychiatry, called on governments to treat problematic internet use as a global public-health issue, and to regulate design features that keep people online — such as infinite scrolling and autoplay.

The commission estimates that problematic internet use may affect more than 10% of the world's population — more than one person in ten — at an economic and social cost that could reach billions of dollars a year, estimated in Britain alone at about £10 to £12 billion every year. The term covers eight forms: compulsive social-media use, excessive gaming, online gambling, compulsive shopping, problematic pornography, repeated searching for health symptoms driven by anxiety, digital hoarding, and cyberbullying.

Researchers say the problem is not individuals' lack of willpower, but deliberate design: infinite scrolling removes the end point, so you never feel you have "finished"; autoplay starts the next video before you decide to watch it; and personalised recommendations and notifications keep bringing you back to the app. The groups most affected are young people, those with mental-health problems, and residents of low- and middle-income countries.

The commission, led by psychiatrist Naomi Fineberg and involving researchers from Flinders University in Australia and experts from around the world, calls for algorithm transparency, independent auditing of platforms, restricting high-risk features and binding rules, instead of relying on companies' self-regulation — and for treating the issue like tobacco, alcohol and gambling. It also believes that banning accounts for under-16s, as Australia did, is not enough on its own while the apps themselves are designed to keep you connected.

Simply put: your phone is designed to keep you scrolling without stopping, and scientists say the solution is not in your willpower alone, but in changing the design itself. Do you feel that scrolling steals your time without you noticing?

Do you support governments imposing limits on infinite scrolling and autoplay, or is that your personal decision alone?
