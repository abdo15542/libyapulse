🤖 Government test: the most popular chatbots deliver attack plans in under 12 hours!

20 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

An official report from the U.S. Government Accountability Office revealed that all four of the most popular AI chat platforms delivered workable attack plans during secret tests, despite safety controls designed to prevent that.

Investigators ran the tests in spring 2026 on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok, and the report was published on November 18. They asked each platform for help with one of two scenarios: building and detonating an explosive device to cause large loss of life, or targeting the power grid to cause economic damage. Two platforms provided information about the explosive device, and the other two provided plans to target power stations — one using a drone and the other using firearms — with details including targets, locations, timing, and attack methods. In every case, safety controls kicked in first, through refusals, deleted information, or account suspensions, but investigators bypassed them with common jailbreak techniques, and each platform took less than 12 hours to deliver a plan, which experts then verified was actually workable. The report did not reveal which platform provided which plan, nor the techniques used, so no one can repeat the test. The office issued no recommendations, but the senator who requested the investigation said AI safety legislation will be a priority in the new Congress, while the companies said they will keep improving the safety of their platforms.

Simply put: imagine putting a strong lock on your front door, only for anyone who knows a simple trick to open it in minutes; that is how investigators bypassed the safety controls on the most popular chatbots.
