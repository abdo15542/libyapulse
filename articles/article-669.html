⚖️ Four British regulators warn AI companies: the law applies to your agents before launch!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Four major British regulators sent a joint letter to the government on November 16, calling for tighter oversight of personal AI agents — the systems that act on your behalf, such as an agent that books for you, buys for you or deals with your bank.

The four bodies are the Competition and Markets Authority, the Financial Conduct Authority, the Information Commissioner's Office, and the communications regulator Ofcom, working together within the Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum. The letter was written to Chancellor John Healey, the AI minister and the culture and business ministers, and said that companies preparing to launch new products must understand their legal obligations, assess the risks, and put safeguards in place before launch, not after.

The regulators stress that current laws apply in full to personal agents, including competition, consumer protection, data protection, online safety, financial services, payments and cybersecurity laws, and that the agent's independence does not reduce the responsibility of the company deploying it. Among the risks they identified: fraud and deception, sharing your personal data in ways you don't expect, and misleading recommendations or decisions.

The letter also calls on companies to engage with the regulators early and openly before launching their products, and to explain to the user when and how the agent acts on their behalf, who bears responsibility when something goes wrong, how they get compensation, and what commercial interests influence the agent's recommendations. The letter follows a joint statement on October 30 warning that agents could amplify and accelerate risks.

Simply put: Britain is telling AI companies that your personal agent is not above the law, and that companies must prove their product is safe before it reaches you. Will Arab countries follow suit as agents spread?
