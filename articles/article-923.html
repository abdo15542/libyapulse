🚀 New Record at Vandenberg! One Launch Pad Fires Two Rockets Within 32 Hours — and "NROL-136" Succeeds!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

"SpaceX" has broken its own record for the fastest turnaround of a launch pad, as a "Falcon 9" rocket lifted off on the "NROL-136" mission on Sunday morning, 22 November, just 32 hours and 3 minutes after a previous launch from the same pad.

The new record crushes the previous Vandenberg pad record of 55 hours and 48 minutes, set in June, and even beats the company's overall record of 45 hours from Cape Canaveral in January — meaning the pad is now ready for the next launch within just a day and a half.

The rocket lifted off at 4:23 a.m. Pacific time from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California, carrying a fresh batch of intelligence-gathering satellites for the US National Reconnaissance Office, on a classified mission whose cargo is not disclosed.

The first-stage booster "B1097" returned to land at Landing Zone 4 near the pad about 7 and a half minutes after liftoff, on its fourteenth flight — the 38th landing at this site and the 680th "Falcon" booster landing to date — and a powerful sonic boom was heard in neighbouring counties as it returned.

The cargo itself remains classified, as usual for reconnaissance office missions: neither the number of satellites nor their exact task is announced. But the launch speed itself is the biggest news — turning around a whole launch pad within a day and a half means rockets now launch like repeat flights, not projects needing weeks of preparation.

Simply put: a rocket climbs, and the pad is ready for the next one within a day and a half — is space closer to us than we imagine?
