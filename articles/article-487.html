📉 CoreWeave Falls Below Its IPO Price — Debt Is Eating AI Growth!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

CoreWeave's cloud computing shares fell below their IPO price of $40 on Friday, November 20, in a new sign of investor anxiety about the debts of AI infrastructure companies.

The shares fell about 10% during the session to a daily low of $39.96, then closed at $39.51, down 12.02%, having lost about 79% since their peak of $187 in June 2025.

The fall came despite what the company described as its largest quarterly revenue in its history: sales grew 168% to $3.66 billion in the third quarter, contracted orders reached $132.5 billion, and the company raised its 2026 revenue forecast to between $13 and $13.3 billion.

Advertisement

But the problem is in the balance sheet, not demand; the company is borrowing heavily to buy chips and build data centers, and its debts reached about $47 billion at the end of September. Net interest expenses reached $846 million in the third quarter, about 23% of revenue, and the company expects between $1.05 and $1.15 billion in the fourth quarter, leading to a net loss of $709 million, versus $110 million a year earlier.

The CEO ruled out unsecured debt at current prices, as the yield on the company's unsecured bonds exceeded 15%, while the company expects about $9 billion in customer payments and deposits within three months.

In a sign of confidence, co-founder Brian Venturo bought $30 million of the company's bonds this week, but the three founders have sold shares worth about $3.3 billion since the IPO, weakening the signal's effect.

Simply put: a company whose sales grow 168% but whose debt interest eats a quarter of its revenue; the market does not doubt the demand for AI, but the company's ability to fund expansion without drowning in debt.

A company whose revenue grows 168% but whose debts eat its profits... would you buy its shares or stay away?
