🚕 Fireworks breach leaks data on around 150,000 Uber drivers! License photos in hackers' hands!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Uber has begun notifying around 150,000 drivers that their personal data was stolen after a breach of a technology vendor it works with, in an incident that shows once again how breaching a single vendor can become a leak affecting hundreds of thousands.

The vendor, the AI platform Fireworks, told Uber on October 9 that an unauthorized party had breached internal login credentials of its own and used them to access Uber's account with it, so Uber immediately cut its connections to the platform and began an investigation, which confirmed on October 13 that the attackers had reached the data on October 7.

The stolen data includes names, addresses, dates of birth, driver's license numbers and license photos, in addition to other government ID numbers, and the number of affected people was 149,346 according to a notification filed with the authorities in the US state of Maine.

Notification messages were sent to those affected on November 10, more than a month after the incident was discovered, and similar notifications were filed with the authorities of several US states, while Uber offered free credit monitoring and anti-fraud assistance to those affected.

And most alarmingly, the attackers used valid login credentials belonging to the vendor itself, so Uber's alarm systems set up to monitor its cloud environment did not trigger, since those systems were designed to detect unauthorized access, not access with stolen valid credentials.

The incident traces back to a wider breach of the Fireworks platform in October, attributed to a financially motivated criminal group linked to a series of attacks stealing access tokens to large language models, and the same breach also hit the Cursor and Notion platforms, which reported the theft of data from a limited number of customers.

Simply put: login credentials stolen from one vendor opened the door to the data of 150,000 drivers... do you trust your data with the vendors of big companies?
