🚀 SpaceX Bets $74 Billion on Two AI Cities… So Who Pays the Electricity Bill?!

24 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

SpaceX announced it will invest more than $74 billion in two giant supercomputing and AI cities, one in Texas and one in Arizona, in the company's biggest bet yet outside rockets and satellites.

In Texas, the city will sit on about 444 acres near Starbase, costing more than $24 billion, and will generate its own electricity from a private gas plant, drawing nothing from the public grid. It has received an air permit for thirty-five gas turbines with a capacity of about 750 megawatts.

In Arizona, the cost is about $50 billion for a complex of nine buildings on 747 acres, which could consume up to 6 gigawatts of electricity when complete — enough for millions of homes — with dry cooling that uses no industrial water. The company says it will pass $3.6 billion in grid improvements to local electric cooperatives at no cost to their members.

The company says the two projects will create more than 3,000 construction jobs in Texas and 5,500 in Arizona, plus hundreds of permanent jobs, and that electricity bills for cooperative members in Arizona could fall by up to 20%. But residents and officials are demanding proof that the cost of expanding the grid will fall on the company, not on bill payers.

Both projects are still awaiting local approvals, with decisive hearings set for December 9 in both states. Meanwhile, smart computing has become the company's biggest source of income, passing half of its revenue in the third quarter, with Anthropic as its largest customer, while Google pays $920 million a month for computing capacity.

Simply put: two AI cities the size of full cities… and the question worrying the neighbors: will the company pay for its own electricity, or will residents? Would you agree to an AI city being built near your home?
