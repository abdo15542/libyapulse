🚀 Progress MS-36 Launches to the Space Station! 2,500 Kilograms of Supplies!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The Soyuz-2.1b rocket lifted off today, Tuesday, November 24, at 10:17 GMT from the Baikonur base in Kazakhstan, carrying the Russian cargo spacecraft Progress MS-36 on its way to the International Space Station, with docking scheduled for Thursday, November 26.

The spacecraft carries about 2,500 kilograms of supplies, including 1,400 kilograms of dry cargo such as food, clothing, medicine, and scientific equipment, and 420 kilograms of water, plus the fuel needed to raise the station's orbit, and oxygen and nitrogen.

This is the fourth and final Progress cargo flight to the station in 2026, and the tenth launch of the Soyuz-2.1b rocket this year. After its cargo is unloaded, the spacecraft will move to the Zvezda module, in a new procedure that will become standard on future Progress flights.

The mission is known to NASA as Progress 97, the ninety-seventh spacecraft in a series that began decades ago and remains the station's logistical lifeline.

Simply put: an uncrewed space truck carrying the astronauts' food, water, and station fuel… and it arrives two days after launch, right on schedule.

Can you imagine life on a space station that depends on shipments arriving from Earth?
