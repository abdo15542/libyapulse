🧠 Official US report: no chatbot is approved for mental health!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The US Government Accountability Office issued a new report on November 20 warning against relying on chatbots for mental health matters.

The report revealed that about one in five Americans aged between twelve and twenty-one said they had used an AI chatbot seeking mental-health advice during 2025.

Despite this widespread use, the US Food and Drug Administration has not approved any chatbot to date as a mental-health tool — no chatbot holds a medical marketing licence in this field.

The report warned of a dangerous habit in these chatbots called "sycophancy": the chatbot tends to agree with you and flatter you instead of challenging your view, and that could reinforce delusions, negative feelings or harmful thoughts in a person in a fragile state.

It added that the safety guardrails companies put in place can fail when the user rephrases the question, when the conversation gets long, or when they ask the chatbot to play a particular role.

There are chatbots designed specifically for mental health, but they are not a substitute for a human specialist, and only two companies have announced receiving a designation allowing expedited FDA review for narrow medical cases — and that does not mean approval of the chatbot itself.

The report also warned about privacy: chatbots may retain the text of previous conversations, and most of them operate outside the scope of health-data protection laws.

Simply put: a chatbot may help you reach information and resources, but it is not a psychiatrist — and no chatbot is officially approved for this role yet.
