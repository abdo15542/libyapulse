🛡️ Instagram protects original Reels from theft — and protection starts automatically after you enrol!

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Instagram has launched a new feature to protect original Reels from copying, as part of its “Content Protection” system, so eligible creators can enrol their clips in it — they are then protected automatically and scanned for matching copies across Instagram and Facebook. The feature is based mainly on eligibility: the platform builds eligibility on originality and account-integrity criteria, and eligible creators can check whether the feature has reached them from the “Tools” section in the Home tab, under “Your professional tools”, and they may also receive notifications about it in their activity log.

After enrolment, protection becomes automatic: every new original video published after enrolment enters protection on its own, and the platform keeps scanning it for visual matches across Instagram and Facebook, using the same technology as Meta’s “Rights Manager” system. Older eligible clips published before enrolment are not covered automatically — the creator chooses them one by one to add to protection. Enrolment and archive coverage are two separate steps: the new is automatic, the old needs manual selection.

When a match appears, the system shows its details, including the number of views and the number of followers of the account that posted the matching copy, then the creator chooses between four actions: “Track” — the default — where the match stays visible while it continues to be monitored; or “Hide from everyone”, where the match disappears from Instagram and Facebook without directly penalising the account that posted it; or “Add an attribution link”, which links viewers to the original video or its owner’s account — an option specific to Instagram matches; or “Release the match”, which removes the protection from it. Those who want a match to stay visible while they watch it leave it on “Track”; hiding it changes its availability on both platforms at once.

The platform is also testing a step further: on 17 November it announced a test with a small group that matches the spoken words in a protected original video, even when AI replaces the visuals with other scenes, making it harder for a thief to dodge detection by changing the image while keeping the same audio. This is an important step because AI tools can now regenerate scenes easily, so matching the image alone is no longer enough.

Simply put: the new protection does not prevent theft before it happens — it makes detecting it and acting on it easier, and gives the original creator clear options instead of discovering a copy of their videos by chance weeks later. But it starts with enrolment, not automatically for every account.
