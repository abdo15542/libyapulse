🇪🇺 The European Commission Confirms a Data Theft… Names and Emails of EU Academy Users!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

In a new development in the cyberattack that targeted the European Commission, spokesperson Thomas Regnier confirmed at a press briefing on Monday that attackers had breached the EU Academy online learning platform and stolen its users' personal data.

The confirmed stolen data includes users' names and email addresses, while the Commission has not yet announced the number of those affected or whether other data was exposed.

The Commission suspended public access to the platform as a precaution, and posted a warning on its website alerting users to phishing messages that could exploit the stolen data.

Advertisement

The Commission had revealed last Thursday that attackers breached its systems on 22 October using an AI-powered penetration testing tool called Artex, disrupting its services and the Sysper human resources platform for more than ten days.

The spokesperson confirmed that no ransom demand had arrived, and that attributing the attack to a specific party will wait until the investigation ends, while the European Data Protection Supervisor has been notified of the incident.

The platform will stay closed until 14 January 2027 while the Commission rebuilds it, according to a separate notice on the website.

This is the third breach to hit the Commission in 2026: in January its mobile phone infrastructure was breached, and in March its cloud environment was breached and data from 29 other EU bodies was exposed.

Simply put: an attack that first looked like a service disruption turned out to have stolen real data; the lesson is that a claim of 'no evidence of data theft' can change as the investigation progresses, and any email you receive in the Academy's name deserves suspicion now.

Would you open an email from an organization that suffered a breach before checking the sender?
