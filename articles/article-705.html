⚡ Reports: AMD delays Zen 6 processors until after Computex 2027 — in search of higher performance!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

New reports suggest that AMD has delayed the launch of its Zen 6 processors by several months, from CES next January to after the Computex show in mid-2027. The delay, according to a post on the Chiphell forum, is due to the company's decision to move Zen 6 chips from TSMC's N2P process to the newer N2X process, which gives higher frequencies and tolerates higher voltages, with about 10% higher performance than its predecessor.

AMD was expected to use the N2P process for the compute chips and N3P for the input/output chip, and it is not yet clear whether the process change also covers the input/output chip. The delay may give its rival Intel a chance to pull ahead, as Nova Lake processors are expected to launch in the first quarter of 2027.

AMD had revealed details of the Zen 6 architecture last week, announcing a 27% improvement in performance per clock cycle compared with Zen 5, with 12 cores in each compute group instead of 8, doubling the cores of the Zen 6c version to 32, and a 48MB Level 3 cache.

Simply put: a delay in exchange for higher performance, in a merciless race with Intel. Was AMD's decision to take its time the right one, or will the delay cost it the market lead?
