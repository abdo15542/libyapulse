📱 A startup sues Apple in India — NameDrop stole its patent and it demands $162 million!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Apple faces a lawsuit in India from the American startup GG Technologies, accusing the NameDrop feature in iPhones of infringing its patent, and demanding compensation of 15 billion rupees — about $162 million — plus a ban on using the feature in the country.

The company, owner of the "Stay Touch" app for sharing contacts by bringing two phones close together, says NameDrop uses technology covered by its Indian patent for transferring contacts between nearby devices without manual pairing. The company launched its app in 2019, applied for the Indian patent in 2021, and obtained it in July 2024, while Apple introduced the NameDrop feature with iOS 17 in June 2023.

The company filed the lawsuit before the Bombay High Court last October, after mediation that began in April and ended in September without agreement, asking the court to ban the sale or import of iPhones and Apple Watches equipped with the feature in India. The company also filed a similar lawsuit in the United States last April, and the US International Trade Commission opened an investigation in August.

Simply put: a simple feature of bringing two phones together has become a patent case worth millions of dollars. Do patents really protect startups, or do only the courts decide?
