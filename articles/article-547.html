🔐 Breach Hits an Indian Software Company — a Supplier's Account Opens the Door to Attackers!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Indian company MoEngage, which provides software for communicating with customers via messages, notifications, and email, suffered a cyber breach in which attackers reached a limited part of its cloud infrastructure through a compromised account belonging to one of its external suppliers. The company said it discovered and contained the breach in late October, that fewer than 1% of its customer accounts were affected, and that it notified every affected customer directly.

Despite the company's confirmation that its services never stopped, reports said some customers had difficulty accessing the platform for about seven days, and one customer regained access on November 19 after a full week of disruption. The company says it brought in independent security experts to verify the findings, fixed the vulnerability, and strengthened its security measures.

The company serves more than 1,350 institutions in 75 countries, from its headquarters in Bangalore and San Francisco, and is preparing for a possible listing on the Indian stock exchange. The incident comes amid a wave of breaches hitting suppliers and intermediaries this month, as attackers now target small companies that hold data for many customers, instead of attacking large companies directly.

Simply put: imagine protecting your home with strong locks, but the thief enters through the neighbor's door — the neighbor who holds a copy of your key; that is how the attackers entered the company through an external supplier's account, without ever breaking into its systems directly.
