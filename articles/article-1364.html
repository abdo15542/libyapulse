🧮 Google reveals Cognetic — an AI that writes six full math papers in a single day!

26 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Google Research published six math research papers on November 25, produced entirely by its Cognetic system, a multi-agent system running on the Gemini 4 model, with no human intervention after the initial direction.

The six papers claim progress on problems that have stayed hard for decades, most notably a mathematically proven algorithm for factoring integers faster than the best proven bound known since 1992, as well as results in discrete logarithms, game theory, the log-rank conjecture, and graph reconstruction.

The system works by distributing the research among specialist agents that cooperate to write the complete proof, then publishing the paper with a computer-checkable formalization of the proof. The system was first described in a research paper in September 2026.

But the papers have not yet been peer-reviewed by mathematicians, and they were not published in a scientific journal. Instead, they were placed in a public online repository, and Google promised to publish the verifiable computer versions in a later release.

What stands out is that the guaranteed constant in the new factoring algorithm is very large, so it is a theoretical proof more than a faster practical attack, and it does not threaten the RSA encryption used today. Still, it is an unprecedented step for an AI to write complete mathematical research from a single prompt.

Simply put: an AI wrote six math papers from one instruction, and the hardest of them is the factoring problem that puzzled scientists for decades. Would you believe that a machine now discovers mathematics on its own?

Would you trust a math proof that no human has reviewed yet, or is human review still an indispensable condition?
