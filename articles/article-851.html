🔍 "Li Ming": the man who ran the Nvidia chip smuggling network to China from behind the scenes!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Reports on 22 November revealed the man who ran the smuggling network for advanced AI chips to China from behind the scenes: "Li Ming", a 52-year-old Chinese citizen who today stands outside prison on bail in Singapore, in a luxury villa near the Chinese embassy.

According to informed sources, "Li" was the central operator of a network of companies that smuggled restricted Nvidia chips to China, including Singaporean company Megaspeed, in which Alice Huang, the partner of Nvidia's former founder, was a director. Singaporean authorities charged him and others with fraud in February 2025, in a case linked to alleged re-shipment of Nvidia chips.

Prosecutors allege that "Li" bought servers fitted with Nvidia chips and lied to the American supplier, saying they were for his local company "Luxuriate Your Life", after which they were shipped to Malaysia and from there to an unnamed third destination. "Li" had worked with Alice Huang and her husband Kang Wei for years, and together in 2021 they founded a joint cloud project called "Century Longting", then bought a Singaporean unit of a Chinese gaming company and renamed it Megaspeed.

After Megaspeed told Nvidia that Alibaba was a major client of its own, it joined the company's cloud computing partner programme in November 2023, gaining priority in buying the latest chips. The company provided servers to Malaysia to supply legal computing power for Alibaba, but some servers were sent to a company in Shanghai in violation of export controls. Nvidia placed the company on its internal "do-not-sell" list in June 2025.

The story did not end with Alice Huang's departure in August 2025: the same team now runs a new project called "Novagate Cloud", founded in 2025, which says it is an Nvidia cloud partner and runs at least two data centres with advanced Blackwell chips, and has received at least $300 million of Nvidia-chip hardware in one of its entities in Malaysia during the first five months of this year. Li's criminal case is still ongoing.

Simply put: the public face of the network was a businesswoman, but the real operator was another man, and even after the first company fell, a new company appeared with the same team; can the new export controls really stop these networks?
