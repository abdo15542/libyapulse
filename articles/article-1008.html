🛩️ Zipline brings in an Uber man to lead the biggest drone expansion in Africa!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The American drone company Zipline has appointed Anthony Le Roux, one of Uber's senior managers, as head of its operations in Africa starting in January 2027, at a time when the company is preparing for its largest expansion on the continent.

Le Roux will oversee an ambitious plan to open at least 20 new distribution centers, known as 'nests', within two years, and the company has already agreed nine new centers in Côte d'Ivoire, expecting its expanded services to reach more than 130 million people, serve 20,000 health facilities and delivery sites, and provide more than 800 jobs.

Le Roux spent 13 years at Uber: he joined in 2013 as general manager in Cape Town, then led its business in Eastern Europe from Warsaw, became regional director for the Middle East and Africa from Dubai, and in 2020 built the corporate group transport business that became Uber Group Transportation, which he led until his departure, after five years spent in Amsterdam.

Medical deliveries will remain the backbone of the expansion, as the drones carry blood and essential medicines, and the company integrates its services into public health systems, already working with the governments of Rwanda and Nigeria to connect its infrastructure to health supply chains, while later considering delivering food, groceries and medicines after the infrastructure is running.

The expansion will not be easy: it must get past complex airspace approvals, power cuts off the grid, and sovereign payment risks — but Le Roux believes that building local teams, managing field operations and dealing with regulators are skills he brought with him from Uber, and he has already started hiring three senior managers to lead deployment, service and logistics, with a special focus on talent from the continent itself.

Simply put: drones carrying blood and medicine to distant villages... and a new leader from Uber driving the expansion; will drones become Africa's flying pharmacy?
