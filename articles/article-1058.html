🛰️ Amazon Leo LE-04 Launch Delayed at the Last Minute… A Ground-Equipment Fault Stops the Countdown!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Arianespace announced that the countdown for the launch of the Ariane 6 rocket — which was due to carry 36 Amazon Leo satellite-internet satellites on the morning of Monday 23 November — was halted because of a technical event on the ground equipment used to operate the rocket.

The launch, the tenth flight of the Ariane 6 rocket and the fourth for Amazon Leo satellites, was scheduled from the European spaceport in French Guiana, in a window running from 11:09 to 11:38 a.m. GMT — but the company stopped the sequence before rolling the rocket back from the pad, and announced the launch was delayed by a full day, to the new date of 24 November.

The delay comes just eight days after Amazon lost 40 Leo satellites in the failure of a Vulcan rocket launch on 15 November, making every successful flight a measure of how fast the network recovers — the network, formerly known as Project Kuiper, is planned to include 3,276 satellites.

The flight is called LE-04 and uses the Ariane 64 rocket with four solid boosters, and it was due to place the satellites at an altitude of around 465 kilometres, before the company's operations centre in Redmond raised them to their operational altitude of 630 kilometres after initial health checks.

The flight itself had already been delayed because of a problem with a turbopump on one of the rocket's engines, discovered during ground tests in the summer, and the European Space Agency acknowledged the delays, without either company announcing details of the fault or the repair.

Simply put: a rocket ready and satellites ready, but a fault in equipment on the ground stopped everything... Did you know that most launch delays come not from the rocket itself, but from the ground beneath it?

If a launch carrying the internet of the future is delayed by a day because of a ground fault, do you trust the network will be completed on time?
