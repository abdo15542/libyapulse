🚀 A Russian Humanoid Robot Heads to the Space Station! "Teledroid" Will Work Outside the Station Instead of Astronauts!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The Russian space agency Roscosmos launched the Progress MS-36 cargo spacecraft toward the International Space Station on November 24, carrying a humanoid robot as part of an experiment called "Teledroid".

A Soyuz-2.1b rocket lifted off from Baikonur at 13:17 Moscow time, carrying more than 2.4 tons of cargo, and the spacecraft reached its orbit and deployed its antennas and solar panels as planned.

The spacecraft is scheduled to dock with the station's small research module on November 26 at 15:10 Moscow time, and that's when the robot's real mission begins.

Advertisement

"Teledroid" is a surrogate robot that works outside the station and helps astronauts: the operator — whether inside the station or at mission control on Earth — wears a special suit, and the robot copies their movements. Its tasks include preparing the worksite before a spacewalk, opening covers and installing equipment, and it carries out some tasks on its own.

The main goal is to reduce the time astronauts spend outside the station, because spacewalks are among their most dangerous and exhausting tasks.

The Progress MS-35 spacecraft had already delivered the control equipment to the station in September, and astronauts Pyotr Dubrov and Anna Kikina trained to install the robot outside the station and connect its communications — even practicing underwater.

Simply put: instead of a person stepping out into the cold, dangerous void, they send a robotic stand-in that copies their movements from inside.

Will a day come when robots work outside the space station while astronauts watch from inside?
