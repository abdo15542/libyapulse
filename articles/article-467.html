👓 The Xreal Aura Glasses Finally Arrive — a Giant Screen on Your Face, but Google's System Is Still Half-Ready!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The Xreal Aura mixed-reality glasses have finally reached reviewers' hands — the first smart glasses running Google's Android XR system at a developer-friendly price — but early reviews say the hardware is excellent while the software is still in its infancy.

The glasses are strikingly light: the frame weighs just 95 grams, versus about 730 grams for Apple's Vision Pro, and they feature two micro-OLED screens at 1920×1200 resolution per eye, brightness up to 1,058 nits, and a 70-degree field of view — the widest in the company's history — though still much narrower than large virtual-reality headsets.

The computing lives in a separate unit called the Compute Puck, containing a Snapdragon Reality Elite processor, 12 or 16 GB of memory, and 256 or 512 GB of storage, plus a dedicated spatial processor, and its top surface works as a touchpad for navigation without raising your hand. The glasses include cameras for spatial sensing, hand tracking, and photo and video capture, and lenses that can be dimmed in five levels to block out the world entirely.

But actual use reveals the system's limits: hand tracking is slow and sometimes misjudges the finger's position, apps crash often, the number of mixed-reality apps is very small, and there are no Netflix or Disney Plus services — only HBO Max and Prime Video. The battery also drained from full to 40% after just 90 minutes of watching and browsing, and the Compute Puck heated up until the company warned against putting it in a pocket during heavy work.

Even so, reviewers praise the glasses as a giant screen: watching films on a huge virtual screen is excellent, mirroring a laptop screen alongside a YouTube app works smoothly, and the glasses can be connected directly to a computer to work as ordinary display glasses without needing the puck.

The price places it in the middle: $1,279 for the base version, $1,099 in the launch offer until December, and $1,499 for the stronger version, versus $1,800 for Samsung's Galaxy XR glasses and $3,699 for the Vision Pro. The reviews concluded that it is primarily a developer's pair of glasses — a proof of life for the Android XR system, nothing more.

Simply put: light hardware and a wonderful screen at a reasonable price, but an immature system, few apps, and a battery racing toward empty.

Would you buy mixed-reality glasses today despite the lack of apps, or wait until the system matures?
