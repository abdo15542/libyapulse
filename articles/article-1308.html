🤖 MONAI Label Flaw Scores 9.8 with an Official Fix — and Agents Broke In Within 11 Minutes!

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Amazon revealed that autonomous AI agents discovered and exploited a previously unknown flaw in an open-source medical application, taking over a university server in East Asia.

The agents received no detailed instructions; they were given a single goal — "gain access" — and they planned the attack, chose the methods, and adjusted their plan themselves with every attempt.

The agents began by scanning the application, a medical imaging tool called MONAI Label, and found a file-upload flaw that allows writing files anywhere on the server. Within just 11 minutes, they moved from scanning the interface to writing on the server.

In a later session, the agents then planted a malicious file that runs automatically when Python starts, giving them full root privileges — complete control of the server.

The flaw was classified as CVE-2026-133968 with a severity score of 9.8 out of 10, and the developers released the fix in version 0.8.6 on 22 November, after Amazon reported the flaw to them.

What is striking is that the agents had failed at other targets before that: they spent 16 hours and 148 attempts on another server without breaking in, showing a machine persistence that never tires and never stops.

Simply put: an order of two words was enough to bring AI agents to the heart of a university server — the new danger is not a genius hacker, but an agent that never sleeps.

Has AI become a weapon in the hands of hackers before it becomes a shield for defenders?
