🚀 The first spacecraft ever caught by the tower's arms will never fly again.. SpaceX is keeping it for display!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

SpaceX founder Elon Musk announced on 18 November that the first Starship upper stage ever caught by the launch tower's arms will never fly again — instead it will be preserved for public display, because what it achieved is historic. The vehicle, known as Ship 42, launched on 13 November on the fifteenth test flight, returned from orbit after about 22 hours and 14 orbits around Earth, deployed 26 satellites, then settled gently between the tower's arms at the Starbase base in Texas.

Even though the vehicle came out of the flight in good condition, and Musk himself said it "could probably fly again after a few weeks of maintenance", the company decided not to risk it. It has not yet been announced where it will be displayed, but it will most likely stay at Starbase itself. The company had previously caught the first stage, Super Heavy, three times and relaunched it twice, but catching the upper stage is far harder, because it returns from orbit at a higher speed and endures greater heat.

On the same flight, the attempt to catch the Super Heavy booster failed: it veered off course because of a low-fuel warning and exploded over the Gulf of Mexico. Musk aims for around 10,000 Starship flights a year by 2030, to build the new Starlink satellite network of around 100,000 satellites, as well as a constellation of AI data centres in space ten times bigger than that.

Simply put: when you make something historic, you may choose to preserve it in a museum instead of risking it on another flight — so do you agree with turning the first vehicle to return from orbit into an exhibit, or should it have flown again to prove that reusability really works?
