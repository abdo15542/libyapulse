🚀 After an Eight-Year Journey — the BepiColombo Spacecraft Enters Mercury's Orbit Today!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The BepiColombo spacecraft enters the orbit of the planet Mercury today, Saturday, November 21, after a journey lasting eight years, during which it covered about 10 billion kilometers. The spacecraft was launched in October 2018 and performed nine close flybys — one around Earth, two around Venus, and six around Mercury itself — to slow down gradually until the planet's gravity could capture it.

The mission is a joint effort between the European Space Agency and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency, carrying two scientific orbiters that traveled attached together throughout the journey: the first, European, studies the planet's surface and approaches it to within 480 km, and the second, Japanese, orbits it to measure its magnetic field. The two orbiters will separate from each other on December 9 and 10, then adjust their orbits over the following months, with the science phase set to begin in April 2027.

Arrival had been delayed by a full year due to a propulsion fault that struck the spacecraft in 2024, and the transfer module that pushed it throughout the journey separated last September. If the maneuver succeeds, BepiColombo will become the second spacecraft to orbit Mercury in history, after the American MESSENGER spacecraft, which orbited it between 2011 and 2015, and the first mission to send two orbiters together around the smallest planet in the solar system and the closest to the Sun.
