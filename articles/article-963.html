🔓 'GPT-6 Cyber' finds 35 flaws in Apple devices... and bypasses Lockdown Mode!

23 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A new press report has revealed that the cybersecurity-specialized AI model 'GPT-6 Cyber' discovered about 35 security flaws in Apple products, including iPhones, Mac computers, iPads and Apple Watches, and even Apple TVs and 'Vision Pro' headsets.

These findings came from research conducted by 'OpenAI' together with a cybersecurity company over the past few months, where researchers found flaws in Apple's hardware and software, some of them serious enough to let a hacker take over a device through a malicious website the user visits.

The most dangerous point in the report is that the researchers used the model to develop an exploit that bypasses 'Lockdown Mode' protection, the feature Apple designed to protect people exposed to advanced targeted attacks, by restricting some features and narrowing the ways an attacker can reach the device.

The CEO of the cybersecurity company that partnered in the research said Apple fixed many of the flaws after being told about them, but Apple has not publicly confirmed the full list of all thirty-five flaws and did not respond to a request for comment.

There is partial public evidence of the model's contribution: in its security notes for 'iOS 27.2' and 'iPadOS 27.2' issued on October 26, Apple credited the 'Codex' security team at 'OpenAI' with three flaws in the web engine, two of which could allow arbitrary code execution when processing malicious web content, and also named the company alongside another lab in a kernel flaw.

It is important to distinguish here: Apple's public security notes mention the 'Codex' security team, not the 'GPT-6 Cyber' model itself, so the two names should not be confused, even though the press report links the discoveries to the new model.

The model was launched on November 5 and classified as 'critical' in security readiness, the highest rating, because it can discover flaws and develop exploits for them without human intervention, and the company's researchers had previously used it to find 81 previously unknown high-severity flaws in the Firefox browser, and to help ethical hackers develop exploits for hundreds of flaws in the Linux kernel.

Simply put: an AI designed to protect devices has become able to break their strongest protection... do you trust that Lockdown Mode really protects you?
