🍔 A Breach Hits McDonald's Japan… Purchase Records and Device Locations Exposed!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

McDonald's Japan confirmed that an unauthorized party accessed the database of its official mobile app, in a statement issued last Friday.

Among the data that may have been exposed: product purchase history, IP addresses, mobile device location data, and internal customer management numbers that alone are not enough to identify an email address or membership number.

The company confirmed the breach did not expose names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, or credit card information.

Advertisement

The company did not disclose the number of those affected or when it discovered the breach, but a later investigation found no service disruption and no misuse of the app as a result of the incident.

The company is working to invalidate the credentials used in the unauthorized access, is investigating the affected systems, and has notified Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission.

The company said customers do not need to change their passwords or take any special action at this time.

The incident follows a breach announced by Burger King Japan last month that stole 11.18 million customer records, part of a wave of attacks that hit major Japanese companies this year, including Japan Airlines, Nikkei, and KDDI.

Researcher Yutaka Seiyama counted 288 security incidents affecting Japanese institutions since the start of the year that involved the theft or exposure of personal information.

Simply put: even your purchase history and your phone's location are sensitive data when they fall into strangers' hands; the good news here is that names and cards were untouched, but the incident is a reminder that the apps we use every day know more about us than we think.
