📰 "New York Times": "Alice Huang" was in contact with a Chinese intelligence officer for over a year!

23 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A new news report has revealed that "Alice Huang", the Chinese woman at the heart of the latest "Nvidia" scandal, stayed in contact with an officer in China's Ministry of State Security for more than a year, during her relationship with the company's former CEO Jensen Huang.

According to the report, which draws on people who have seen intelligence reports describing the meetings, the contact began in spring 2025 and continued until this autumn.

Huang previously ran the Singaporean company "Megaspeed", an important "Nvidia" customer that made billions of dollars from chip sales, and US authorities have been investigating the company since 2024 over concerns about smuggling advanced AI chips to China.

But US officials cast doubt on early suggestions that she was a spy, and the US administration has repeatedly stressed that it has not concluded she spied for China; the attorney general said the Justice Department has not yet reached a conclusion, and the director of national intelligence repeated the same thing on Sunday.

The first article about the scandal, on October 29, was what ignited the crisis and led to Huang's resignation two weeks later, along with a sharp fall in the company's shares, while the scandal pushed lawmakers from both parties to ask whether the administration has been lax in enforcing export controls.

Simply put: documented contact with an intelligence officer, but no official verdict yet on whether it was spying... are suspicions enough to condemn someone, or is only the investigation entitled to the answer?
