💼 Microsoft ties Azure employees' pay to performance — stock bonuses for top performers, formal meetings for laggards!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Microsoft confirmed on November 20 that it has updated the pay model in its Azure cloud-computing division to tie pay more closely to performance: top-performing employees receive additional stock bonuses, while those whose results fall short face formal meetings with their managers to set clearer expectations and receive direct feedback.

The change came in an internal memo from Arun Ulagaratchagan, executive vice president of Azure, who wrote that "pay for performance only works if we are equally rigorous on both sides: recognising exceptional impact and being honest when performance is below what is required."

He added that "reaching the required level today means closing past performance gaps and showing a real leap in impact", and that "AI is reshaping how the world works, and competition has never been fiercer."

Advertisement

A company spokesperson said the update includes "meaningful additional rewards for our strongest-performing colleagues", while ensuring clear expectations and direct, timely feedback for all managers.

The company did not disclose the size of the additional stock bonuses, nor did it announce that the new model would be applied outside the Azure division.

The change comes amid a broader tightening: the company, which employs more than 220,000 people worldwide, began a voluntary exit programme last month for about 60,000 employees in the United States, Canada, India and Britain, and the Azure chief asked his senior leaders to identify ways to cut costs by at least 20%. The company also laid off about 4,800 employees in July.

Simply put: in Microsoft's most important cloud division, performance now directly determines pay. Competition over AI has raised the bar, and those who don't reach it face a formal meeting instead of a bonus.
