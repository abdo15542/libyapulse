🎬 An actor licences his face to a short-drama company — and AI films his stand-in!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

In a first that could change the future of acting, actor Aki Avni has licensed his face to the startup Shortical, which specialises in short dramas, to appear in a series made entirely with AI. The series, called "Inevitable", was shown in Los Angeles last week, with the actor playing himself in a story about an actor who licences his face to an AI studio — then his digital copy goes out of control.

The company was founded last January, and offers romantic drama episodes of no more than two minutes, by monthly subscription, with the first 7 to 10 episodes free. Its homepage recently switched to AI-generated content, because it costs far less than live filming, while the potential profits remain higher. The CEO says AI lets small teams produce film-level work, from kingdoms and dragons to superheroes and werewolves.

The company is now promoting a new programme called "License Your Face", inviting interested people to send their social media accounts, so the company can decide whether to offer them a contract to use their features in its content. No contracts have been signed through this platform yet, and the actor's deal was through a direct agreement, in which he became a partner in the project for pay similar to a normal acting role. In China, where this industry began, companies pay people between $15 and $700 to licence their faces.

Simply put: your face could act in series you never stood before a camera for. Will licensing faces open a new source of income for actors, or turn acting into a cheap commodity with no actors?
