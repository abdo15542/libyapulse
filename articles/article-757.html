📉 Nasdaq-100 Enters a Bear Market During Trading... the Worst Week for Stocks Since 2020!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The Nasdaq-100 index - which includes the 100 largest tech companies - entered bear market territory during trading on Friday, November 20, after falling 5.6% in a single day to stand 20% below the record high it set on October 6. A bear market means the index has lost a fifth of its value from its peak, the mark investors consider the start of a long downturn rather than just a passing dip.

As for the broader Nasdaq Composite, it closed at 22,617 points, down 5.28% on the same day, only around 18% below its peak - meaning it has not officially entered a bear market yet, but it has turned negative since the start of the year. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910 points, entering correction territory, while the Dow Jones lost 949 points to 48,147 points.

The weekly toll was harsh: the Nasdaq lost 10.02% in a single week, the S&P 500 8.61%, and the Dow Jones 8.02% - the worst week for the market since April 2025, and the worst for the Dow Jones since March 2020. AI stocks led the fall: Nvidia shares dropped 14%, Anthropic shares 8.5% just two days after its stock market listing, and 71 of the 101 companies in the Nasdaq-100 were down 20% or more from their highs.

Simply put: when a tech index loses a fifth of its value in six weeks, the question becomes about the end of the AI wave, not its beginning. Do you think the drop is a healthy correction after an exaggerated rise, or the start of a long winter for tech stocks?
