⌚ Your Watch Detects Your Breathing Stopping During Sleep — and a Smart Ring Does It for the First Time!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Samsung announced on November 3 an upgraded version of its sleep-apnea detection feature, now available on the Galaxy Watch 9 and Galaxy Watch Ultra 2, and on the Galaxy Ring, after approval from the US Food and Drug Administration in July and from the Korean Ministry of Food and Drug Safety in October. This makes Samsung the first company in the world to bring apnea detection to a smart ring.

The new version, available through the Samsung Health Monitor app, does not merely say there is a potential risk — it analyzes vital signs during sleep in greater depth: monitoring drops in blood oxygen and breathing disturbances, estimating the number of times breathing stops per hour, and classifying the condition as mild, moderate, or severe, to give a clearer picture that helps you talk to your doctor.

The feature requires two nights of sleep of no less than five hours each, within a ten-day period, to deliver a result. It is limited to adults aged 18 and older, and targets detecting signs of moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea. The company stresses that it is not a replacement for specialized medical diagnosis.

The update will later reach the Galaxy Watch 7 and Galaxy Watch 8 through software updates. The original feature had received the first US authorization of its kind for a wearable that detects apnea, in February 2024.

Simply put: imagine a tiny guard on your wrist watching your breathing while you sleep, counting how many times your breathing stops per hour, and telling you whether it is worth visiting a doctor — that is what the new feature does, even in a light ring you barely feel.

Would you sleep comfortably knowing your watch is monitoring your breathing?
