🏛️ America's governors call on Congress for national AI rules! 24 new principles!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

On 22 November, the National Governors Association in America announced new principles for governing AI at the state and federal levels, prepared by a bipartisan council led by Maryland Governor Wes Moore, Indiana Governor Mike Brown, North Carolina Governor Josh Stein, and Utah Governor Spencer Cox.

The principles include 24 principles: 14 directed at the states, 5 at the federal government, and 5 shared between them. They call on the federal government to set a risk-based framework for the most powerful models, with evaluation or auditing by independent experts, and to require their developers to report security breaches and major safety incidents to federal authorities and affected states.

In return, the states keep their primary role in protecting people: protecting consumers, preventing discrimination, protecting workers and children, and addressing AI's effects on jobs. The principles also warn that any attempt to override state laws must be done carefully and with real participation from the states themselves.

The governors said humans must remain in control of decisions, that public trust in AI safety is a condition for its success, and that the aim is to inform the new Congress as it drafts a national AI framework. These principles are recommendations, not law — they carry no binding force on their own.

Simply put: the governors want one set of national rules for the most powerful models, without cancelling their states' laws that protect people — do you think AI needs one law for everyone, or does each state have its own circumstances?
