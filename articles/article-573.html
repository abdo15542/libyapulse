📡 Your phone will connect to satellites soon — AST switches on the service before the end of 2026!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

AST SpaceMobile announced it will switch on intermittent space coverage for AT&T and Verizon customers' phones before the end of 2026, in key markets including the United States, Canada, Japan, Britain and Saudi Arabia, so your ordinary phone works with no modification in places cell towers don't reach.

The service works via the huge BlueBird satellites, which connect directly to your ordinary phone just as ground towers do. At first the coverage will be intermittent — your phone connects only when a satellite passes overhead, then drops until the next satellite passes. Trial tests with customers of both companies begin in early 2027, when the number of satellites reaches about 25.

The company now has only 13 satellites in orbit, while it needs about 45 for continuous, uninterrupted coverage — a goal it pushed to mid-2027 after it had been set for the end of 2026. The BlueBird 14 to 16 satellites wait at Cape Canaveral for their launch via SpaceX, while other satellites launch via Blue Origin in early 2027, and the company plans more than 100 satellites between 2027 and 2028, and its own Black Lift rocket in late 2028.

The company has already proved the idea in practice: on October 21, near the town of Granite Falls in Washington, an ordinary phone kept its call alive while switching from AT&T's ground network to the company's satellite, in cooperation with Ericsson and Nokia. Neither the company nor the two carriers has announced the service price yet, or which plans will include it.

Simply put: imagine your phone connecting to a satellite in the sky just as it connects to a tower in your street, so you never lose touch with the world in the desert, the sea or the mountains. The start is intermittent, but it is a step towards coverage without borders.

Do you want your phone to connect to satellites when the tower signal disappears, or is waiting until coverage returns enough for you?
