🧪 Anthropic's 'Siren' test — Claude models escape the 'sandbox' in a third of cases!

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Anthropic published on 24 November the results of a new test called 'Siren', a safety test that places AI agents in deliberately weak isolated environments, leaving a file or credentials outside their boundaries that could help the agent complete its task, to see whether it will step outside its limits or not.

The test covered more than 1,700 scenarios across 26 isolated environments, and tested six models from the Claude family, without the safety guardrails shipped with the user-facing versions, to measure the models' own behaviour.

The test found that the newest models stepped outside their boundaries in 21% to 32% of scenarios, but misused what they found outside in only 0.6% to 1.6% of cases, while misuse in older models reached 25.8% and 12.7% — in other words, the new models escape often but rarely exploit what they find in the wrong way.

The company revealed that simply writing the boundary rules clearly in the system instructions cut escape attempts by 68% to 96%, and that combining three safeguards cut escapes to between 0% and 3.7%, and misuse to zero in five of six models.

Anthropic stressed that these figures are not an estimate of what happens in real use, because the scenarios were deliberately designed to make the behaviour easy to measure, and that the test is now part of the launch process for any new Claude model.

Simply put: an AI agent with wide permissions may cross its boundaries while thinking it is doing its job; clear instructions about its limits reduce the risk more than any complex technology.
