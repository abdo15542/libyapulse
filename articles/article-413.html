📉 'Intel' Shareholders Sue Its CEO and CFO — After the Stock Crashed 28.6% in One Day!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A proposed class-action lawsuit was filed against "Intel" and its CEO Lip-Bu Tan and CFO David Zinsner, in a federal court in San Jose, California, on November 13. The suit is led by a firefighters' and police officers' pension fund in the city of Southfield, Michigan, on behalf of everyone who bought the company's shares between July 23 and October 29, 2026.

Shareholders accuse the company's management of misleading them about the scale of the decline in personal-computer sales, and hiding that distributor partners' inventory was piling up and that rising component prices were squeezing sales. They say the company had a motive to hide it: in mid-August, in the middle of the same bad third quarter, it raised $23 billion by selling shares at $95 a share. Then on October 29 it issued weak fourth-quarter guidance, and the stock plunged 28.6% the next day, wiping out about $140 billion in market value in a single session — the biggest one-day drop for the stock in more than 50 years.

The context is broader than "Intel" alone: global PC shipments fell 20.1% in the third quarter of the year, to 62.7 million devices, after "DRAM" memory prices jumped fivefold within a year, because memory factories shifted their capacity to higher-profit AI data-center memory. "HP" shipments fell 30.9%, "Dell" 25%, "Lenovo" 22.6%, and even "Apple" fell 11.3%. The CFO warned the slowdown could continue until at least the first half of 2027.

Simply put: a class action means a group of shareholders files one case on behalf of everyone harmed in the same way, instead of each person suing alone.
