🔭 A Mysterious Sodium Cloud Around Jupiter's Moon Europa — What Does It Hide About Its Icy Ocean?

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Astronomers revealed an extended sodium cloud surrounding Jupiter's moon Europa — one of the most intriguing moons in our solar system, because beneath its icy crust a global water ocean is believed to hide. The team, led by Emma Lovett from Boston University, used the Keck telescope in Hawaii and an instrument that measures the distinctive light emitted by sodium atoms, tracking their distribution around the moon.

The cloud extends to a distance equal to about 30 times Europa's radius, stretching east and west. The surprise is that its quantity appears relatively stable across observations spanning more than twenty years, with an archive going back to 1999, despite the highly volatile environment around Jupiter, and despite the volcanoes of the neighboring moon Io constantly pumping material into the surrounding space.

The researchers conclude that the sodium most likely comes from salts on Europa's own surface, not from Io's volcanic emissions. A clever technique helped them: observing the moon as it passed through Jupiter's shadow, when the reflection of sunlight off its icy surface disappears, making the faint sodium glow near the moon clearer.

The study was published in the Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics, and announced on November 20. But the results do not mean scientists have directly observed the hidden ocean, nor do they offer any evidence of life; they improve models of the moon's thin outer atmosphere, and help understand how salts move between the surface, space, and possibly the ocean beneath the ice.

Simply put: imagine a sea covered by a thick icy lid, with salt particles flying off into the air above it, so you know from the smell of salt that the sea exists beneath the lid without seeing it — that is how scientists read the sodium cloud around Europa.
