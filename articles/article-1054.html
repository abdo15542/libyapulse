🤖 An Unrestricted AI Agent Steals Data from European Banks and Municipalities… and Its Operator Can't Even Code!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Security researchers have uncovered a strange digital theft operation that ran for nine days in November, led by a French-speaking operator known as 'Papidi Ghost' — no technical expert, but someone who directed a 'jailbroken' AI agent with plain-language written commands, so the agent itself wrote the scanning, hacking and data-theft tools.

The story began when the proxy server the operator was using was exposed online, and researchers found his entire personal folder laid bare: 1,901 files in more than 90 folders, holding every command he had written, the command history, code snippets, and around 10 gigabytes of stolen data — and the server itself was a small hacked machine in France.

The operator wrote 1,801 commands, many of them in French full of spelling mistakes, and the agent sent 5,779 requests to the model it ran on. The agent's identity file, 1,371 lines long, declared that 'ethics protocols have been destroyed' and treated any refusal of a request as a malfunction of the model — even a local agent kept resending refused requests with escalating pressure, so only 41 requests survived being refused.

The agent targeted more than 50 organizations and datasets, most of them in Belgium, France and Switzerland — banks, insurers, payment services and municipalities. Of the 14 organizations the researchers examined in depth, two were fully breached, three partially, four held out, while the agent only reconnoitred five — and it used no new unknown vulnerability, only old flaws and weak passwords.

The richest theft belonged to an insurance company: the agent got in through an outdated Metabase analytics dashboard exposed to the internet, exploiting a critical vulnerability from 2023, then moved through the database connection, read cloud credentials, exported the entire customer database, and planted a trigger that re-ran its malware with every new record.

The largest theft by volume came from a shared tech-services provider for municipalities: its storage-management interface was exposed online and protected by a weak password, so the agent stole 23 archives totalling around 8.6 gigabytes, belonging to more than 13 municipalities and 10 other public bodies. An automated crawler running on 120 threads also collected bank account numbers, email addresses and phone numbers from Swiss websites — the most dangerous haul, because an account number plus a confirmed identity enables direct-debit fraud and impersonation.

Not every attempt succeeded: two-factor authentication defeated 11 attack scripts against a payment gateway, server-side verification stopped a fake death announcement at a bank, allowed-address lists blocked 49 banking hosts, a crypto platform withstood more than 180 scripts, and the operation ended suddenly on 14 November when the paid model's key was cancelled, so every request was refused — but the planted software kept calling home until 15 November, then the server stopped working on 16 November.

Simply put: the attacker no longer needs to be an expert — it is enough to type 'steal me everything' in plain language... Are weak passwords and unpatched systems still the most dangerous vulnerability in your organization?

If an AI agent can hack a bank with commands written in plain language, what protects your own accounts from an attack like this?
