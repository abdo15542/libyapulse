🚨 US Intelligence Chief Warns: Tech Outages Will Continue! And the Cause Is Fixing AI Vulnerabilities!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

US Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton warned that the wave of tech outages that hit America within a single week may continue, saying Americans should prepare for "bumps in the road" as systems are updated for the age of artificial intelligence.

The warning came after a week of back-to-back outages: a global Microsoft 365 outage covering email, Teams, and the Copilot assistant, then the longest national aviation shutdown in America in 25 years, lasting more than four hours, followed by outages at JPMorgan Chase bank and United Airlines, then disruptions to Apple and Marriott services and an internal network at the National Security Agency.

The US administration and the companies involved said there is no evidence that these outages are connected or caused by a cyberattack, attributing each one to a separate operational cause: a latent flaw in a network fix at Microsoft, a network router at the aviation authority, and a software bug in the bank's systems.

But Clayton revealed a new cause for the National Security Agency outage: a wide fix for vulnerabilities in the PHP programming language discovered by AI last month pushed maintenance teams to update systems, and some systems did not come back working as expected after the update.

In the same context, a startup announced it used AI to discover a flaw in the Java language, so Oracle issued a security update, and the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency issued an alert urging organizations to install it immediately.

Experts said AI is finding vulnerabilities faster than teams can safely fix them, and that current infrastructure was never designed to handle the demands of massive AI services, while Senator Mark Warner called these outages a "wake-up call," because AI is moving faster than our ability to manage its consequences.

Simply put: we fix the vulnerabilities AI discovers, and what we fix breaks — will these outages become a regular thing in the age of AI?

Do you think this wave of outages will become the new normal, or is it just a passing coincidence that will soon end?
