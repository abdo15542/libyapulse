🛰️ The Dragon capsule leaves the International Space Station today — and splashes down off California tonight!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The uncrewed Dragon cargo capsule separates from the International Space Station today, Saturday, November 21, at about 12:05 p.m. US Eastern time — about 7:05 p.m. Libya time.

The capsule splashes down by parachute in the Pacific Ocean off the California coast at about 4:55 p.m. US Eastern time — about 11:55 p.m. Libya time. NASA will not broadcast the splashdown live, but will publish updates on the space station blog.

This is SpaceX's thirty-fifth commercial resupply mission to the station.

The capsule carries more than 6,300 pounds — about 2,858 kilograms — of cargo and scientific samples, after arriving at the station on October 15, two days after its launch aboard a Falcon 9 rocket.

Among the returning samples: a 3D-printed blood vessel for studying blood-vessel ageing and heart disease, frozen tumour samples that could help improve cancer treatments and personalised medicine, and frozen microbe samples for studying antibiotic resistance in space.

A lighting system that spent more than 3 years on the station testing support for astronauts' sleep and wellbeing on long missions also returns.

Simply put: imagine a package returning from space carrying samples that could help treat heart disease and cancer on Earth. That is what the capsule carries tonight.

Did you know that space research helps treat diseases on Earth?
