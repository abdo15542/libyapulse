🚗 Empty Electric Cars Roam Chinese Parking Lots at Night — and the Reason Is a Contest, Not a Glitch!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Videos spread in November of empty electric cars moving in underground parking lots in China, including two Avatr cars performing synchronized parking maneuvers side by side at 6 a.m., and another car said to have attempted to park 868 times in a single night — scenes that looked like the cars were moving on their own in the dark.

But the most likely explanation is not a system malfunction: the clips coincided with the Huawei Qiankun Smart Driving contest, held from October 31 to November 13, in which more than 190,000 owners took part. Its Parking Elite category ranked participants by the number of successful uses of assisted parking features, with the top 100 participants earning Huawei points. Posts said some owners repeated maneuvers deliberately to improve their ranking, and one claimed to have completed a thousand sessions in a single day — a figure not independently verified.

The contest rules state that qualifying activity was only counted between 6 a.m. and 10 p.m. — meaning the nighttime maneuvers earned no extra points at all. Surveillance footage alone also does not prove a software glitch, as possible explanations include owners using the app to move and re-park their cars, or a maneuver interrupted by weak lighting or faded markings. Huawei confirmed that its system is a driving assist, not self-driving, and that it is installed in more than two million vehicles from more than 20 brands.

Simply put: imagine a contest that rewards whoever parks their car with the smart assistant the most times, so some participants spend the whole night parking and re-parking — that is most likely what happened, not haunted cars.
