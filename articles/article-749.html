🏛️ 48 AI Agents 'Discover' a Maya City... But Scientists Beat Them to It by a Year!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

In a personal experiment that sparked wide debate, an AI researcher set 48 smart agents loose on public laser survey data from NASA, and the agents pointed to a location that looked like a Maya settlement hidden under the Mexican jungle. Within less than 72 hours the idea had become an official report and a reply from the Mexican government, before it turned out the site had been known to scientists for a year.

The story began on October 18, when the researcher, while traveling in Peru, wondered what an old explorer would do with today's tools, and directed 48 general-purpose agents to examine images from an airborne laser survey covering around 1,200 square kilometers of jungle, searching for shapes resembling pyramids, platforms, and plazas. The agents spotted a hill with a flat plaza, an oval pyramid between 8 and 12 meters high, a raised courtyard, and possibly a water reservoir, then prepared a report, translated it into Spanish, and sent it to Mexico's National Institute of Anthropology and History, which replied the next day that there was no registered site at that location.

But the reply meant only one thing: that the site was not in the institute's register, not that it was unknown to science as a whole. On November 11, the researcher corrected his account: the same site had appeared in a scientific study published in 2025 on Maya population density, based on laser data, and the agents had missed finding that study, turning the 'discovery' into a 'rediscovery.'

Simply put: the agents found the pattern in the images, but they did not check the literature. Do you think AI has really become able to speed up science, or is human verification still the real difference?
