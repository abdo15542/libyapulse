🔬 Google's quantum processor 'Willow' wins a game impossible to win!

20 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Google's quantum processor 'Willow' has demonstrated a new capability for quantum computing, after winning a mathematical game no traditional computer can win at the same rate, according to a study published in the journal 'Science'.

The game is based on a 3x3 grid in which two players fill rows and columns with positive and negative values under contradictory rules impossible to satisfy together, so the best traditional strategy wins no more than 8 rounds out of every 9 — a rate of 88.9%. But the 'Willow' processor won at a rate of 98.3% across 900,000 rounds, benefiting from a quantum property called 'contextuality', meaning a measurement's outcome changes depending on the other measurements taking place with it at the same moment. The team also measured a contextuality index of 5.59, above the classical limit of 4 and close to the quantum limit of 6, and expanded the experiments to 105 qubits. Most importantly, these tests reveal tiny hardware errors that traditional performance tests do not catch, making them a more accurate measure of quantum processor quality.

Simply put: imagine a game whose rules are designed so it is impossible to always win, and then a player comes along who wins 98 times out of every 100; that is what 'Willow' did with the laws of quantum physics.
