☎️ US Communications Commission Bars California From Protecting Landlines, and 184,000 Homes Face Losing Service!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The US Federal Communications Commission has ordered California to stop enforcing its rules requiring AT&T to provide basic telephone service to any customer in its area, in a decision issued on November 20 that paves the way for ending traditional landline service in the state.

The company wants to stop offering basic telephone service to new customers and cut off service for about 184,000 residential customers and 15,000 business customers on June 1, 2027, replacing the old copper lines with a wireless home phone service over the cellular network, which the company and the commission consider an adequate alternative. The company had already obtained a similar exemption in 20 of the 21 states where it operates, leaving California alone in holding on to the rules, after a federal judge in July rejected the company's attempt to bypass them through the courts.

Commission chairman Brendan Carr said California's rules were written 30 years ago for a bygone era and no longer fit an advanced competitive market, and that the state's requirements obstruct a transition the commission authorized federally. Democratic commissioner Anna Gomez opposed the decision, saying state communications commissions are 'the last line of defense for consumers', and that California's commission is already studying an update to its rules and should have been given time, especially since the company does not intend to cut service before June 2027.

California's rules require fixed-price options for unlimited calls, discounted prices for low-income customers, and a call-relay service for the deaf and hard of hearing, and the state says the wireless alternative is allowed if it meets basic service standards. The state is likely to challenge the decision in court.

Simply put: a 30-year-old copper line stands against a federal decision to end it; so is a wireless phone enough of a replacement for a landline, or do some homes still need the old wire?
