💾 "MemOnes": AI Memory 1,313 Times Smaller! A Million Tokens in 25 Megabytes!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Researchers from Nanjing University and the University of Science and Technology of China presented a new system called "MemOnes" that gives AI models a long memory in a tiny size, instead of storing everything.

The known problem is that a model keeps a huge memory for every word it reads, so it swells with long texts until it fills the memory. The new system, by contrast, stores only a small part of the hidden states of the last layer, then expands them into a full memory when needed, and retrieves from it only what matters for the current question.

The numbers are striking: on the "Qwen3.5-4B" model with a context of one million tokens, memory shrank 1,313 times — from more than 32 gigabytes to just about 25 megabytes — the text-reading stage became 6.9 times faster, throughput rose 3.5 times, and memory-task scores improved by 5.77%.

Full memory retrieval also completes in 64.2 milliseconds — 12 times faster than the "M+" system and 17 times faster than traditional "RAG" pipelines — and the system outperformed traditional models across different sizes and multiple memory benchmarks.

But the paper is preliminary, from a single team, and no independent party has reproduced the results yet, so the numbers remain promising while awaiting confirmation.

Simply put: instead of carrying a whole library in its memory, the model now carries a small card that summarizes it — so it remembers more and consumes less.
