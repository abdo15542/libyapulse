🇪🇺 Germany and Austria Want a 'European TikTok' — €25 Billion to Buy the Platform from Its Chinese Owner!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Germany and Austria are pushing to place TikTok's operations in Europe and its technology under European ownership, in a plan that mirrors the American arrangement that transferred control of the app's US business earlier this year. The two countries intend to present the proposal to EU culture and media ministers when they meet in Brussels on Friday.

German Culture Minister Wolfram Weimer said investors from five countries have shown willingness to support the deal, with an amount that could reach €25 billion — about $27.4 billion — and that 'European TikTok must be prepared to sell 75% to 80% of the shares.' Weimer issued a joint statement with Austrian Digital Affairs chief Alexander Pröll on Wednesday calling for the 'Europeanization' of the platform.

The two countries rely on the app's wide reach, as about a third of the population of each country uses it, giving it major influence in shaping public opinion, along with data protection, after a €530 million fine imposed by the Irish privacy authority last year for transferring European users' data to China. The European Commission is also investigating the app under the Digital Services Act, including infinite scrolling, and has asked it to redesign its recommendation algorithm or face a new heavy fine.

ByteDance, the Chinese parent company, rejected the idea, with a spokesperson describing it as 'nonsense', and there is no official sale process, nor does the app face a ban in Europe. Europe also has no legal mechanism to force the company to sell, unlike the American law issued in 2024 that led to a joint venture completed in January, in which ByteDance still owns 19.9%. German publisher Axel Springer has expressed willingness to join a potential alliance.

Simply put: imagine an app used by a third of your country owned by a foreign company, and your government proposes that local investors buy it so it becomes 'European'; the idea is simple, but there is no legal way to force the seller.
