💰 Binance Hackers Move $594 Million of Stolen Ethereum — Through Swap Protocols!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Wallets linked to the Binance exchange hack moved about $594 million in Ethereum within just two days, in the latest movement of the stolen funds in the hack worth about $6.3 billion on October 29. Blockchain analysis firms tracked the transfers, as about $248 million was moved on Thursday, then 197,460 Ethereum units worth about $346 million on Friday, to new wallets.

Part of the money passed through cross-chain swap protocols, most notably THORChain, which accounted for most of the tracked activity, and Chainflip, through which only about $5 million passed. Daily trading volume on THORChain jumped to $239 million on Friday, a sign that the protocol has become a preferred route for hiding the trail of stolen funds and moving them between networks.

TRM Labs and Chainalysis linked the original hack to the TraderTraitor group linked to North Korea, the same group that previously used THORChain to launder funds from the $1.4 billion Bybit hack in February 2025. After the latest transfers, the hackers' tracked wallets still hold about 224,722 Ethereum units, worth about $394 million.

This movement comes weeks after the original theft, most of which was carried out by social engineering rather than technical hacking, as the attack began with a fake job interview. Investigation teams continue to track the funds across chains, while the use of decentralized swap protocols makes recovering them harder.

Simply put: stolen money does not stay in place; hackers move it between new wallets and swap protocols to mislead tracking, and about $394 million of it is still under watch.
