🔑 Google Launches the 'Pixel Tag' — a Tiny Device That Finds Your Keys and Bag Using a Billion Phones!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Google put its first own tracking device, the Pixel Tag, on sale on November 11 — a direct rival to Apple's AirTag — priced at $29.99 for a single unit, or $99.99 for a pack of four. The device had been announced in August alongside the Pixel 11 phones, and users waited three months before it reached stores.

The device is small and elongated, made of plastic and metal, 5.4 mm thick and weighing 11.8 grams, and comes in the silver 'Mist' color. For nearby tracking it relies on ultra-wideband technology and distance measurement via Bluetooth 6.0, guiding you with an arrow and a distance on screen toward the lost item, while remote tracking works through the Find Hub network, which includes more than a billion Android devices worldwide.

Its advantages include a replaceable coin battery lasting up to a full year, water and dust resistance rated IP67, a speaker that rings the device when searching for it, a button on the device itself that rings your phone if it is lost, and 'left behind' alerts when you move away from the tagged item. The device's location can be shared with up to ten people — double what its rival from Apple allows.

But there are caveats: there is no hole for attaching the device to a keychain, so it needs a case bought separately, and precise arrow guidance requires a phone that supports ultra-wideband or Bluetooth 6.0 with Android 16 — only recent phones — while the basic device works with any Android 9 phone or newer.

Simply put: imagine every Android phone in the world as eyes searching for your lost keys — if one of them passes near them, it tells you where they are; that is the Find Hub network the new device relies on.

Would you buy a tracker for your keys, or do you never lose them in the first place?
