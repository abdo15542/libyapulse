🚀 Starship Could Leave NASA With No Ride: Falcon 9 Retires and Dragon Ends in 2028!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A recent report has warned that the rise of the giant Starship rocket could leave NASA with no way to carry its astronauts to the International Space Station, because the company plans to phase out the Falcon 9 rocket as Starship accelerates, while Falcon 9 carries the Dragon capsule, currently the only American vehicle able to carry NASA astronauts to the station and bring them back.

According to a NASA announcement in September, the agency contracted the company for only three additional flights to the station, enough until 2028 if they fly every six months - two years before the station itself retires in 2030. The company's founder said shifting scarce engineering and production resources to Starship makes sense when the new rocket flies steadily several times a week, reaching several flights a day, which means phasing out Falcon gradually. The company's president, Gwynne Shotwell, said the Dragon capsule on Falcon 9 resembles a 'small family car,' and that the company wants to move from the old technology to the new.

The company's figures reveal the reason for the shift: of its total revenues until September this year, launches brought in only around $2.1 billion, compared with around $12.8 billion from the Starlink satellite internet service and around $10.9 billion from the fast-growing AI business. Starship already carries the new third-generation Starlink satellites, and the company's founder later hopes it will carry data centers into space.

But the gap worries everyone: a recent analysis estimates the commercial market could face a shortfall of around 150 launches between now and 2030, while the US Department of Defense is looking for flights for its security satellites, and US Space Command has secured Falcon 9 flights only until 2028. As for Boeing's Starliner capsule, the supposed alternative, it is still struggling: its first crewed attempt in 2024 turned from an eight-day trip into a nine-month stay, and the two astronauts returned on a SpaceX vehicle in March 2025. It is scheduled to be tested again this winter, with a crewed flight in around 2028 - the same year the Dragon contract ends.

The company has not set any official date for retiring Falcon 9, and did not respond to a request for comment, while NASA confirmed that the agency and the company continue to work closely together, and that technologies and services will evolve as the commercial space industry evolves.

Simply put: the company that saved NASA's crewed flights could leave the scene before the replacement finds its way, leaving the agency with a counted number of flights until 2028. Do you think Starship will mature in time to replace Falcon 9, or will NASA pay the price of rushing?
