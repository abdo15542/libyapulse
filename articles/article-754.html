🤖 Axion 1.0: A Fully Open AI Agent... With a Three-Step Training Recipe!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A team from ByteDance and American universities published the Axion 1.0 model on November 2, an AI model that works as an 'agent' - meaning it does not just answer your question, but plans, searches, uses tools, and carries out long tasks step by step until it delivers finished work.

The model is available in two versions: the first with 35 billion parameters, of which only around 3 billion are activated at each token, and the second with 122 billion parameters, of which around 10 billion are activated, both mixture-of-experts types that wake only a small part of the model for each task instead of running all of it.

But the real surprise is not the size; it is the simplicity of the recipe: the model was trained in just three stages - two light fine-tuning stages followed by reinforcement learning - without the complex stages that have become the standard in training agents, such as mid-training, DPO, or distillation. Instead, the team relied on data quality: only around 18,000 training examples, carefully made and automatically tested before use.

As for the figures, they come from the team itself and have not yet been verified by an independent party: the large version scored 59.0 points on the difficult Humanities Last Exam test, 87.0 points on the Frontier Science Olympiad, and 66.7 points in the research section that requires gathering information from multiple scientific papers - results that rival leading commercial models. Its training also needed only around 1,000 hours on graphics cards, a small number compared with commercial training runs.

'Open' here goes beyond the weights: the team published the complete recipe - data building, filtering, and verification, the prompts, and the training code - under the Apache 2.0 license, so any team can rebuild the model from scratch and develop it.

Simply put: when an open model reaches the level of commercial models with a short recipe and little data, the question becomes who owns the recipe, not who owns the huge computers. Do you think open agents will break the monopoly of the big companies, or does real superiority still need resources only the big players have?
