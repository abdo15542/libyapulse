🏭 The World's Data Center Capital Halts Construction — 33 Projects Frozen!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The Loudoun County board in the US state of Virginia voted on November 17 to stop considering new data center and power station applications for up to 12 months, by a majority of 7 votes to 1, with one member abstaining, in a decision affecting the world's data center capital.

Loudoun is known as the largest data center cluster on Earth, with 245 centers in operation and development, and the county's 2027 budget expects $1.3 billion in revenue from data centers — 40% of its total budget. The freeze affects 33 active applications now: 10 for data centers, 12 for power stations, one for a grid reliability station, and 10 for joint projects.

The decision is retroactive from September 15, and its aim is to give the county time to complete a comprehensive review of data center policies and locations, including restrictions on heights, noise, and on-site power generation, with the review to finish in summer 2027 and the freeze ending when the board adopts the new standards.

Developers can submit new applications during the freeze, but the board will not vote on them finally until the review ends, while 15 to 17 exempted older projects can proceed. The freeze also does not cover administrative applications or properties in the Route 28 tax district. County staff warn that clearing the accumulated backlog could take months after the freeze ends in September 2027.

Simply put: imagine a city living off the rent of huge computer buildings, suddenly deciding to stop building any new building for a full year to write clearer rules; the money matters, but residents want rules that protect their city.
