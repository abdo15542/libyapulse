🏛️ A Third of the US Chips Approved for China Went to Military Companies — and an Order Linked to a Spy Network!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

US government records revealed that about a third of the AI chips Washington approved for sale to China went to companies the US Department of Defense classifies as Chinese military companies — a shocking detail hidden inside the aggregate numbers.

Of 879,868 approved chips, the companies on the Pentagon's list received about 272,000 chips — nearly a third — including 150,000 for Tencent, 75,000 for Alibaba, 43,000 for Baidu, and 4,000 for the car company BYD. The approved chips include Nvidia's H200 chips and AMD's MI325X.

Among the controversial requests was Nvidia's request to sell more than 40,000 H200 chips to a data company linked to the Megaspeed network — the network linked to alleged spy Alice Huang — and committee chairman Brian Mast described the companies Maginfra and New Matrix as shell companies, hinting at the possible use of fronts to evade restrictions. Nvidia responded that these chips 'have not arrived and will not arrive' at that destination.

Notably, every request submitted by Nvidia and AMD described the risk of diverting the chips to military uses as 'low', even though the companies themselves requested to buy 3.44 million chips between December 2025 and May 2026, and the ministry approved 879,868 of them through 21 requests, without any of them actually being shipped until May 11.

The committee chairman disclosed these records publicly using his powers under the Export Control Reform Act, describing the disclosure as the most detailed in the law's history, after considering the ministry's withholding of this information as 'against the national interest'. The committee plans to hold hearings next month with the Commerce Secretary and his deputy in charge of the licensing file.

Nvidia said the approvals were subject to strict conditions and limited quantities, and confirmed that it has since stopped shipping H200 chips to China, while AMD confirmed its commitment to export-control laws.

Simply put: America, which tightens restrictions on AI chips, secretly approved selling hundreds of thousands of them to companies it itself accuses of being military — and some requests passed through companies Congress described as shell companies.
