⚛️ Pelagos from IBM and AMD: A Free Tool That Tests Quantum Computer Error Correctors at Record Speed!

23 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

On November 16, IBM and AMD announced the launch of a new open-source tool called Pelagos, which helps researchers test quantum computer error-correction software at large scale.

Quantum computers are error-prone by nature, so they need special software called decoders that detect where an error occurred and correct it without revealing the information itself — and testing these decoders requires simulating billions of error patterns.

The new tool uses graphics cards to accelerate these tests, reaching speeds up to 97 times faster than a single CPU core — the equivalent of hundreds of traditional CPU cores.

Advertisement

The tool was built with the Triton programming framework, so it runs on both AMD and Nvidia cards, and its tests showed speed-ups of up to 7.5 times when work was spread across eight cards of the Instinct MI300X and MI355X models.

The tool was unveiled at the SC26 supercomputing conference in Chicago, alongside a new research paper on scaling tests across multiple cards and a broad scientific survey on the future of quantum error-decoding systems.

Pelagos complements a joint platform launched by Oak Ridge, IBM and AMD laboratories on November 5, combining quantum processors with supercomputers to develop the correction techniques future quantum computers will need.

Simply put: a quantum computer cannot work without a strong error corrector, and Pelagos makes testing these correctors faster and cheaper for everyone... Does this tool bring us a step closer to an error-free quantum computer?

If quantum error-correction tests become 97 times faster, how many years separate us from a reliable quantum computer in our daily lives?
