🔍 New Spy Group 'Volant Manticore' Hides in a File-Sharing Network — Targeting Airlines and Hospitals!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Security researchers have uncovered a previously undocumented Iranian cyber-espionage group called 'Volant Manticore,' believed to be made up of former operators of the well-known 'MuddyWater' group who broke away and now operate independently. It has been active since late August 2025.

The group targets telecom and aviation companies and critical infrastructure in Cyprus, then in September 2026 suddenly turned its attention to healthcare institutions in Saudi Arabia, at a time coinciding with rising tensions in the region. Among its targets is a Cypriot airline operating flights between Cyprus and Israel.

Its attack relies on a new backdoor called 'Kubo Shell' that uses the decentralized file-sharing system IPFS as its control channel: the infected device registers itself using its network card address, then checks a command file on Pinata every 60 seconds and executes whatever it contains through a hidden PowerShell window, making it hard to trace because it never contacts an attacker's server directly.

The attacks usually begin with AI-generated fake CVs inviting the victim to download the full résumé from a link that hides a malicious file, or with compressed files carrying health-themed titles like 'AI in Healthcare Symposium,' some of which were sent from a compromised account belonging to a private Saudi hospital.

Alongside the backdoor, the group deploys the 'Huma' post-exploitation framework and legitimate remote-management tools such as 'MeshCentral' to stay inside the network, as well as campaigns to steal email login credentials and corporate accounts.

Simply put: when a public file-sharing network becomes an attacker's command channel, malicious traffic hides in plain sight among ordinary traffic; and a fake CV can be more dangerous than any suspicious attachment.
