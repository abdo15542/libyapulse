📉 US phone market shrinks 9% — and Apple alone grows to take 57%!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Smartphone shipments in the United States fell 9% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, between July and September, according to the latest data from market research firm Counterpoint.

The firm blamed the decline on global constraints on memory supplies and rising component and manufacturing costs, which hit Android phone shipments — especially the cheap ones — alongside weak demand for low-priced phones.

Apple was the only company whose shipments did not fall in the US market during the quarter: its share jumped to 57%, up from 50% in the same quarter last year and from 54% in the second quarter of this year.

Advertisement

Samsung came second with a 25% share. Its shipments grew 1% year on year, but its share fell 5 percentage points compared with the previous quarter, when it was 30%. Its dual strategy of flagship phones and the budget "A" series helped it weather the storm better than its rivals.

Motorola came third, but its share fell 4 percentage points year on year to 8%, hit by memory-cost pressure on its cheap phones, while Google's share reached about 6%, boosted by sales of the Pixel 11 series.

Simply put: the memory crisis pushes phone prices up and hits the cheapest phones first. When component costs rise, the buyer pays the difference or delays the purchase.

Will you pay more for your next phone because of the memory crisis, or wait until prices settle?
