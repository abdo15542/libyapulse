💰 IMF Warns: AI Pushes Global Inflation to 4.7% — and Interest Rates May Rise!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

International Monetary Fund Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva warned at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 19 that two shocks are pushing global inflation to 4.7% this year: an energy-supply shock and an AI-demand shock. She called for tighter monetary policy in many economies — raising interest rates or keeping them high.

Georgieva said 2026 is the first year in which AI actually affects global growth, as investment in it offset the energy shock, helping lift growth to 3.1%. But the problem is timing: money is being spent now on chips, data centers, electricity, and skilled labor, while the productivity gains that could lower prices come later. The Fund's October forecasts expect inflation to reach 4.1% in 2025, then 4.7% in 2026, then return to 4.1% in 2027.

The Fund attributes most of the increase to energy, but it sees the AI investment boom as a major demand shock revealing shortages in chips, data centers, energy, and skilled labor, which could add 0.4 percentage points to US inflation by 2028 if the shortages persist. AI growth expectations, along with huge government and corporate bond issuances, are also pushing bond yields in many advanced economies to their highest levels in decades.

Simply put: imagine the whole world building AI factories at the same time, so the price of everything rises — from electricity to labor — before these factories start producing what lowers prices; that is the price the IMF is warning about.
