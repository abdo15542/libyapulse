🌋 Rocks hotter than an oven: Earth's underground heat could power AI data centers!

20 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Startups are racing to reach the deepest, hottest rocks underground, because the winner could supply AI data centers with electricity. The new idea is called 'superhot rock': at great depths, heat and pressure rise until water turns into a supercritical fluid — a state between gas and liquid carrying enormous energy — and a single well could generate more than 15 megawatts of electricity.

News came in quick succession this week: Google signed an agreement with 'Quaise Energy' paying more than $500 million for 50 megawatts from a project in Oregon that drills to a depth of three miles. Quaise uses technology that sounds like science fiction: a 'gyrotron' device that bombards rock with microwaves until it turns to ash, instead of a metal drill bit that wears out quickly in extreme heat.

In the same direction, 'Mazama Energy', backed by Khosla, announced its 'Carbon Chill' technology, which replaces drilling mud with carbon dioxide gas to cool the drill head, drilling its second well 80% faster than the first, with gas cooling cutting drilling time by a further 20%. 'XGS Energy' received a government grant to develop materials and well designs that withstand these depths. Experts estimate America's capacity from this energy reaches 57 terawatts — far more than the coming wave of data centers needs.

Simply put: imagine the Earth's interior as a giant oven that never goes out; if we can drill deep enough without the drill bit melting, we get clean electricity working day and night, with no sun and no wind.
