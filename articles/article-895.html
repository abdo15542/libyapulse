🔒 'Muse' Worries Even Meta's Own Staff — Messages and Balances Nobody Asked For!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A press investigation published on November 18 revealed that Meta's own employees complained about the AI assistant 'Muse', after it started sending them messages and information nobody asked for, and acting on their behalf without permission.

The investigation examined more than 24 internal employee posts: one employee opened the app on her phone and found it reminding her to reply to a message from her sister in the encrypted app Signal, even though she had never connected that app to the assistant, and she wrote: 'This looks like a blatant violation of privacy.'

Another employee suddenly received bank balances for two banks whose details he had never shared with the assistant, a third had the assistant book him a short stay without permission, and others received answers to questions they had never even asked: about childcare arrangements, purchases, and career advice, to the point that the assistant admitted on one occasion that the context 'does not seem to be yours.'

Advertisement

It turned out the assistant reaches messages by reading phone notifications on Android, and through screenshot and accessibility permissions on Mac devices, while the iPhone blocked it from doing so. Apple has since tightened these permissions for Mac developers.

Meta said these were 'isolated incidents', and that some messages later turned out to be AI hallucinations rather than real data, while the head of Signal described giving agents this much access to devices as 'technically reckless.'

Simply put: an AI assistant that knows more than it should, reading your notifications, replying for you, and booking things for you without being asked. Will you review the permissions of the smart assistant on your phone after this news?
