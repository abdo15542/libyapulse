📱 TikTok Unveils New AI Tools... and Stronger Parental Controls!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

TikTok announced at its third Safety and Innovation Summit, held in London, a package of new transparency tools for AI-generated content, along with wide updates to parental control tools and resources for schools.

The platform launched a new transparency tool that lets anyone upload a photo or video to check whether it was created or edited using TikTok's AI tools, even without having an account on the platform, as the tool examines the content's digital watermarks, and it is available now through the Transparency Center.

The platform also began rolling out its 'Likeness Detection' tool globally, an optional tool that lets eligible creators and public figures verify their likeness through a trusted verification partner, so the system matches incoming images and alerts them when content appears using their likeness without their consent, letting them report it directly to the safety teams.

Advertisement

The platform redesigned the 'Family Pairing' experience to give parents a clearer view of how their teenage children use the platform and who they communicate with, now showing more data about what their children post, plus a new element letting parents see what is shown to their children, through a sample of content from verified creators the teenager follows.

The platform made the screen-time element clearer, so parents know how much time their children spend on the app and when they are active during the day, and it is working with experts on a new alert that informs parents if a teenager repeatedly searches for known terms linked to self-harm, with expert-backed guidance on how to check on them and start a conversation.

The platform also launched a new guide for teachers offering extra resources to help them steer teenagers toward mindful use of social media, at a time when it says more than 5 billion AI-generated clips have been labeled so far worldwide, including 220 million clips in Europe between January and September 2026, and that more than 98% of content violating its AI policies is removed proactively before any report.

Simply put: the question is no longer how to tell whether a clip was made by AI, but how to protect your face and your child in a world filling up with manufactured clips... Are these tools enough to reassure parents?

If an app let you check any photo or video to find out whether it was made by AI, would you use it before believing what you see online?
