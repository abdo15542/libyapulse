🧩 A fake extension posing as Google Chrome steals passwords and spies through the webcam!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Security researchers have uncovered a malicious browser extension impersonating Google Chrome itself, stealing login data, hijacking sessions and taking photos from the webcam — as part of voice-phishing and extortion campaigns targeting financial, healthcare and legal organisations in the United States, Britain and Australia.

The extension is distributed from a site controlled by the attacker, not from the Chrome Web Store, and the victim has to enable developer mode and install it manually. Its deceptive name makes the browser's warning "Google Chrome started debugging this browser" look less suspicious while it runs.

The extension steals keystrokes on web pages, cookies for Microsoft and Google accounts, local storage, session storage and in-browser databases, and it scans QR codes visible on screen that may carry two-factor login secrets.

Worse, it hijacks Chrome's debugging interface to take full control of tabs: it captures screenshots, injects keystrokes and mouse movements, navigates between sites and reaches clipboard contents — turning into a remote-access tool that works inside the user's own authenticated sessions.

It reaches the webcam through the media-capture interface, taking a JPEG photo every 400 milliseconds and uploading it to the attacker's server. Audio capture is disabled in the examined version, and no bypass of the browser's or operating system's permission controls has been documented.

The extension is attributed with medium confidence to the group Helix, whose activity overlaps Google's tracking of the group UNC6671. Two builds of it carry the version numbers 1.6.111 and 1.6.112 and connect to the same server with a fixed hardcoded key, and researchers recommend extension allowlists, stronger vetting of support staff and phishing-resistant authentication.

Simply put: the fake extension doesn't steal one password — it lives inside your browser and uses your own sessions. Never install any extension from outside the official store, and never enable developer mode because a phone caller asked you to.

Do you check the extensions installed in your browser before installing a new one?
