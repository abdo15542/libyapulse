🛢️ Equinor stops the cyberattack after 3 days — and a second Norwegian energy firm faces a hacking attempt!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Norwegian energy company Equinor, Europe's largest gas supplier, announced on Friday, November 20, that the cyberattack on its systems had stopped, three days after it was discovered. The company confirmed that production is running normally, that there is no evidence of data being stolen or altered, and that the incident was reported to the police.

The company had revealed on Tuesday, November 17, an ongoing attack on some of its IT systems, confirming from the start that oil and gas production was running normally and that there was no evidence of data being stolen or altered.

Norway's National Security Authority said the attack does not appear, at this stage, to be part of a wider campaign to destabilise Norway's energy sector, and that it is currently mapping the scope of the attack and any other parties that may have been affected. The authority raised the country's cyber risk level to "high", the second-highest level, on October 27, saying successful cyber operations against Norwegian organisations had tripled in recent months.

The day after Equinor's disclosure, regional energy company Skagerak Energi announced on Wednesday, November 18, that it had spotted suspicious activity in some of its systems, describing it as an attempted cyberattack, with no impact on its operations or production and no evidence of any information leaking. It notified the relevant authorities. Unlike Equinor, it has not yet announced that the attack has stopped; on Thursday it said it had taken the necessary measures and was still dealing with the incident.

Equinor has not yet disclosed the technical details of how the attackers got in, and there is no evidence so far that the two attacks are linked, even though the two companies' announcements came on two consecutive days in similar wording.

Simply put: an attack that stops without revealing how the attackers got in leaves the most important question unanswered — how did they get in at all?

Do you feel safe when a major energy company announces that an attack has "stopped" without revealing how the attackers got in?
