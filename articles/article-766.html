🌐 10 Countries Sign the San Francisco Declaration to Protect AI Supply Chains!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

On November 15, the United States and nine partner countries signed the San Francisco Declaration to protect the sensitive technologies and infrastructure that AI depends on. The signatories are Australia, India, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Qatar, Singapore, the UAE, and Britain, alongside the United States, and Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands, and the European Commission are expected to join later.

The declaration comes within the Pax Silica initiative, launched last December to coordinate AI supply chains among allies. It commits the ten countries to treat protecting these technologies and infrastructure as a shared responsibility, covering software, materials, knowledge, and components essential to AI supply chains, and anything whose theft or illicit transfer to a hostile party could give it a military, intelligence, or economic advantage.

The protection also covers critical infrastructure: ports, stations, transport, communications, and energy networks, and IT systems they depend on. The countries will form five joint working groups on AI policy, adoption, supply chains, security centers, and resilience, aiming to reduce dependence on adversaries for critical minerals, AI models, and semiconductor chips.

The US official in charge of the file says the chip's design, the machine that makes it, and the port that ships it may be in three different countries, so no single country can protect the chain alone. Observers note China's absence from the signatories, making the declaration a new step in the technology race between Washington and Beijing.

Simply put: when a chip crosses three countries before it reaches your phone, protecting it becomes an international agreement, not a local decision; do you think alliances like this will make AI supply chains safer, or will they split the tech world into two camps?
