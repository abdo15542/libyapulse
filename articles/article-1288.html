⚖️ US Jury Convicts Huawei on 10 of 12 Counts — Fraud and Theft of Trade Secrets!

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A jury in a federal court in Brooklyn convicted Chinese company Huawei on November 24, after a trial lasting more than two months, finding it guilty on 10 of 12 criminal counts, including bank fraud, wire fraud, sanctions violations, and conspiracy to steal trade secrets.

The jury acquitted the company of the two most serious counts: conspiracy to commit racketeering, which described the company as a criminal enterprise, and conspiracy to obstruct justice — which the company's lawyer described as a fundamental rejection of the government's account of a systematic culture of fraud inside the company.

The case dates back to 2018, when prosecutors accused Huawei of misleading the banks HSBC, Standard Chartered, and BNP Paribas about its business in Iran, by presenting its subsidiary Skycom as an independent company, so it could move dollars to and from Iran despite US sanctions.

During the trial, prosecutors called more than 60 witnesses and tried to prove that the company stole the source code for Cisco routers, antenna technology from Quintel, and a circuit board from semiconductor company CNEX through a university professor, and showed a video of an employee hiding a piece of a T-Mobile test robot in his bag.

The company faces fines that could reach billions of dollars when the sentence is handed down in September 2027, and it said it will appeal the convictions, while its lawyers stressed that the company succeeded through hard work and investment in research and development, not theft.

Simply put: after seven years of investigations and a two-month trial, Huawei has become the first major tech company to be criminally convicted in the US of stealing trade secrets — but its acquittal on the racketeering count means the legal battle is not over yet.
