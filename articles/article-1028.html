🏥 AI detects bladder cancer 5 years early! It reads your medical record and finds hidden signals!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Researchers at the University of Plymouth have developed a new AI system that could help doctors identify people at risk of bladder cancer years before they are diagnosed, and in some cases the signals appeared five full years earlier.

The team, led by Professor Chang-Ming Chu, studied electronic medical records of around 70,000 patients over 25 years, from 1995 to 2020, and the model named PRECISE-AGZ examined more than 48,000 potential health indicators, from smoking habits and medicines to medical conditions, to select 38 features that were most useful in estimating risk.

The model correctly identified 85% of patients with bladder cancer, and correctly classified 91% of those without it, outperforming current referral guidelines, and it also showed the ability to detect cases around 12 months before the official diagnosis.

Notably, the system found links that were not clear before: patients with Parkinson's and dementia appeared to have a lower risk, while long-term use of the breast cancer drug tamoxifen was linked to a higher risk, with the researchers stressing that these are only statistical associations and do not mean that one causes the other.

The system also divides patients into three groups by estimated risk: low, below 7%, uncertain, between 7% and 55%, and high, above 55%, which could help doctors decide who needs urgent screening and who only needs follow-up.

But the researchers clearly warn: the system is not yet ready for widespread clinical use, as all the data came from a single database in Wales, and it must first be tested on other populations and health systems.

Simply put: an AI that reads your full medical history finds what the doctor misses.. so do you trust a system that predicts your illness years ahead?
