🔐 Secretly modified crypto wallets steal recovery phrases in Berlin and Dubai!

20 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Ledger, the maker of crypto wallets, has confirmed the existence of secretly modified wallets in Berlin, and that it is investigating two suspected units in Dubai, in an extension of an investigation that began in Southeast Asia last October.

The company said the new cases are part of the same incident, and that every confirmed device carries the same modification designed to capture the recovery phrase during wallet setup. It confirmed 'there is no flaw in Ledger's secure chip or its operating system, and no confirmed case of a device bought directly from Ledger's website'. An affected unit of its newest model, the 'Nano Gen 5', was also found in Southeast Asia. The company asked anyone who bought a device in Berlin since January 2026 to contact its support team, and is working with distribution partners in Dubai to find out how the two suspected units entered circulation, and its security team examined the suspected devices and asked sellers to stop sales where checks are still ongoing. It advised anyone who suspects their device to stop using it and not open it themselves, move their funds to a new wallet on a device bought directly from Ledger's website without reusing the old recovery phrase, report to local authorities, and offered free physical X-ray checks at its factory in France for a limited period. The trick works by planting a small chip, a SIM chip, and a hidden cellular unit inside the device, which reads the 24-word recovery phrase from the screen during setup and sends it over the phone network, and the authenticity check does not detect it because the original chip is intact. One blockchain-analysis firm estimated the theft of about $1.68 million from 16 wallets in Dubai and Berlin, after about $102 million in Southeast Asia.

Simply put: imagine buying a new safe from a store, but someone planted a hidden camera in it that copies your key before you use it; that is how these devices stole the recovery phrase without their owner noticing anything.
