🌐 A single malicious webpage is enough to hack the "Muse" assistant and steal your private conversations!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Security researchers revealed that a single malicious webpage is enough to hack the AI assistant "Muse", extracting the user's private conversations and saved memories, without any consent window appearing to them.

Muse runs inside a private cloud computer for each user, and its protection assumes that the assistant itself may be attacked, so a monitoring system was designed to control everything the assistant sends out, keeping passwords and tokens of connected accounts out of its reach.

But the researchers showed that the malicious page, when the user asks the assistant to save it as a document, exploits a flaw in its built-in browser, then chains it with a flaw in the Linux kernel, taking full control of the user's private cloud computer.

Once in full control, the attacker reaches the credential store, saved files and the monitoring system itself — the very parts designed to be out of the assistant's reach.

The experiment was in the lab only, and did not go beyond the private cloud computer of a single user, nor reach shared servers or other users' devices.

The researchers said they informed the Muse team of the findings, and that all the flaws were fixed before publication, while the company has not publicly confirmed the fix or commented on the research so far.

Simply put: even an assistant designed to withstand attack can be fooled by an ordinary webpage — so don't ask an AI assistant to open pages you don't know or save them.

Do you let an AI assistant open webpages on your behalf? And what limits of trust do you give it?
