🥔 Potatoes on the Moon? Scientists grow them in lunar soil!

20 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Researchers at Oregon State University have succeeded in growing potatoes in soil simulating lunar soil, with a yield close to that of ordinary Earth soil, in NASA-funded research published in a specialized scientific journal. The secret ingredient was worm compost: organic fertilizer produced by earthworms breaking down waste.

Lunar soil, called 'regolith', lacks organic matter and essential nutrients, and harmful minerals in it may hinder plant growth. The team tried five types of simulated lunar soil, representing the Moon's volcanic plains and highland regions. Without compost, the potatoes were stunted with deformed roots; with 15% compost they improved, and with 30% growth and yield matched ordinary soil. Nutritional value also stayed close, and natural toxins in the potatoes barely rose, with slightly higher accumulation of some minerals in some tubers.

The result opens an important door for the 'Artemis' program, which aims for a permanent human presence on the Moon: potatoes provide many calories for a small area, and astronauts could one day recycle plant and food waste into compost. Researchers are now testing ten potato varieties to find the best suited to harsh conditions, while noting that weak gravity, radiation, and extreme temperatures on the real Moon make it far harder than a greenhouse experiment on Earth.
