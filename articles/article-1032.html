🤖 A Chinese robot maker returns to the Hong Kong stock exchange! A $74.6 million offering after a previous withdrawal!

23 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Chinese robot maker "Changzhou Microintelligence" is seeking to raise up to HK$584.9 million (about US$74.62 million) from an initial public offering on the Hong Kong stock exchange, to fund research and development, according to a filing with the exchange today, Monday.

The company set an indicative price range of HK$12.07 to HK$15.69 per share; the new offering covers 37.3 million shares, about 33.5 million of them going to international investors, with trading expected to begin on December 4.

The company had withdrawn a previous offering in October that aimed to raise up to HK$799.9 million, citing market conditions, amid a global sell-off in tech shares sparked by concerns about the sustainability of AI spending and its returns.

The company will direct more than 60% of the offering's proceeds to developing its core technologies, with a focus on intelligent embodied industrial robots — robots that combine AI with a physical body to work in factories.

The new offering prospectus includes a mechanism to cut the price to HK$10.87 per share if market conditions worsen, a sign of the company's caution about current market volatility.

Simply put: a robot company pulls back from an offering, then returns at a lower price... does that reflect investor caution about an AI bubble?
