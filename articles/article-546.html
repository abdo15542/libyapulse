🎓 Huge Study Across 19 Universities: AI Tools Designed by Professors Actually Raise Students' Grades!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A large randomized trial, whose research paper was published on November 14, showed that AI tools designed by professors themselves actually improve student learning. The trial covered 7,200 students in 55 classrooms across 19 universities, over three academic semesters, and its plan was registered in advance before data collection, reducing the likelihood of bias in the results.

Instead of giving every classroom the same tool, the researchers encouraged each professor to design what they saw as most suitable for their students: automatic essay grading, generating practice problems, interactive simulations, or a personal tutor. Learning was then measured with proctored in-person exams where AI could not help, and the researchers found that students who used their professors' tools scored about 0.32 standard deviations higher than their peers, while their engagement with the platform and their evaluation of the courses did not change.

The result runs counter to earlier studies that found general chatbots can harm learning, because students rely on them instead of thinking. The difference here is that the tool was designed by someone who knows their students and their subject, so it became an aid to understanding rather than a replacement for it. Still, the paper remains a preliminary study not yet reviewed by independent specialists, it did not follow students over long periods, and it did not test whether the same benefit appears in fully online courses.

Simply put: imagine a teacher crafting an AI tool tailored to their students, so they understand the lesson better and excel in the exam where no one helps them — that is what the trial proved, provided the tool is designed by someone who knows the classroom, not someone selling the software.
