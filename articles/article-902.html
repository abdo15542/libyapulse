🔫 Seven laser beams compete in an American desert.. and the winner gets an instant purchase order!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The Pentagon has chosen seven high-energy laser systems to compete in live-fire trials against drones at the Dugway Proving Ground in Utah, starting in mid-December 2026.

The seven systems were selected from 13 proposals submitted by companies, and include systems from major defense firms and startups: "Bullfrog" from Allen Control Systems, the portable "Archimedes" from Aurelius Systems, "Locust X1" from AeroVironment in cooperation with BlueHalo, "Firefly" from Cord Technologies, "Crossbow" from Lockheed Martin in cooperation with IPG Photonics, the lightweight "Laser Armor" from Thor Dynamics, plus "Talos" from Erica, a drone-mounted laser system that chases threats from above.

The selection is led by two bodies: the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering, and Joint Task Force 401, led by Brigadier General Matthew Ross, a force the Pentagon formed to speed up counter-small-drone capabilities across all military branches.

What makes this competition different is speed: the trials were designed so the winning systems move straight to purchase orders, with no lengthy procurement procedures after the trials, so production lines start immediately and the systems enter field service quickly.

The laser's strength lies in its cost: while intercepting a cheap drone with a conventional missile costs hundreds of thousands of dollars, a laser costs only the price of electricity, and hits its target at the speed of light — but it faces a tough physical challenge: dust, terrain, and buildings can block the beam near the horizon, which is why the Pentagon is testing an airborne system to overcome this obstacle.

Before live firing begins, all seven teams must complete laser safety certifications and integrate their systems into military command-and-control networks, then they will face targets representing hostile drones above and below the horizon, in scenarios that mimic real threats.

Simply put: the Pentagon is testing seven laser beams to shoot down drones, and the winner will be bought immediately with no waiting — will lasers end the era of missiles for shooting down drones?
