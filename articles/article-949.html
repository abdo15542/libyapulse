⚖️ Study: AI models are harsher on men! 10 models judge the same mistake in two ways!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A new scientific study sparked wide debate this week, after its summary spread on the 'X' platform on November 21: the study found that 10 leading AI models judge the same mistake with different severity, depending only on the gender of the person who committed it.

The research was prepared by a team from the University of Science and Technology of China and the Eastern Institute of Technology in Ningbo, and they built a test called 'GAMA-Bench' with 1,298 identical scenarios: a neutral description of misconduct, then two versions of it that differ only in the gender of the actor, once a man and once a woman, with everything else unchanged, from the context and severity to the style.

The scenarios included emotional conflicts such as financial control, reading a partner's messages, and emotional blackmail, and public conflicts such as false accusations, stealing credit at work, and public disputes. The team then measured the answers of 10 models, including 'GPT-5.4', 'GPT-5.2', 'Gemini 2.5 Pro', 'Gemini 3 Pro', 'Qwen3', 'DeepSeek V4 Pro', and 'Kimi 2.5', and an independent model evaluated their answers, with human review of two hundred pairs of answers.

The result was consistent across all the models: in emotional conflicts, the models assigned the man full responsibility for the mistake at a rate about 23 percentage points higher on average than the woman, with the gap ranging from 7.5 points for the 'Kimi 2.5' model to 37.4 points for the 'Doubao' model, with harsher punitive language, a higher assessment of severity, and less empathy, while the woman received therapeutic wording and greater empathy. In public conflicts, the gap was smaller: about 9% more accusatory instructions and 6% more full assignment of responsibility against the man.

But the research itself is a preliminary paper that has not yet undergone peer review, an AI model evaluated the answers, and the team itself admits that automated evaluation may carry measurement noise, that the study does not cover all gender identities or social contexts, and that the companies owning the models have not commented on the results.

Simply put: the same mistake, the same person, the same words... but the AI's judgment changes with a single word: gender... Is this a bias that must be fixed?
