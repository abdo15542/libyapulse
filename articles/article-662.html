📚 A major publisher pulls a book cover after discovering it was secretly drawn by AI!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Publisher Tor pulled the cover of a new fantasy novel by authors Ilona Andrews, titled "This Crown Will Not Rule Me", after discovering that generative AI had been used to draw the cover without the publisher or the authors knowing.

Readers were the first to notice signs of AI in the cover when it was revealed, then two artists pointed it out in comments on Instagram, and the authors asked the publisher to prove the work was human — before it became clear that AI had indeed been involved.

Strikingly, the authors' contract and the publisher's artists' contracts explicitly ban the use of AI, yet the work still reached an announced cover. The publisher apologised, took full responsibility, and said it would strengthen its internal review processes and share a new cover soon.

The authors announced they may request the original working files for every illustration drawn for them in future, to make sure it is human, and they also ended a contract with another company after discovering the level of its AI use in its productions.

Simply put: even the biggest publishers, with clauses banning AI, could not stop it slipping onto their book cover — and the readers were the ones who exposed it. Are clauses alone enough, or has checking every work become essential?
