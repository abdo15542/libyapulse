💾 The 'RAM Cartel' Case Goes to Trial: Judge Rejects Samsung, Micron and SK Hynix's Request!

22 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A U.S. federal judge on 14 November rejected a request by Samsung, Micron and SK Hynix to dismiss a class action accusing them of conspiring to restrict DRAM memory supplies and raise prices, sending the case to trial. The lawsuit was filed in June 2026 by seventeen consumers and small companies from five states, accusing the three companies — which control more than 90% of the global memory market — of coordinating production cuts since October 2022 and shifting capacity to AI memory, while shutting down older memory lines at the same time.

The plaintiffs point to a staggering price surge: memory prices in stores rose about 500% between October 2025 and June 2026, and they calculate the cumulative increase reached about 697% over 18 months. Judge Noel Wise found that the three companies taking identical steps at the same time — including similar customer screening questions in January 2026 — is hard to explain without coordination, and she described the claims as resembling 'hawks flying in formation'.

Simply put: three companies control the entire memory market, their prices jumped several times over, and the courts want to hear their explanation — so will memory prices fall if collusion is proven?
