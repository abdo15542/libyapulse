🚀 Creditors stop SpaceX from launching its chips into space — "They will stay on Earth"!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

In a strange detail from the biggest AI financing deal this year, the creditors who financed SpaceX's purchase of AI chips demanded contractual guarantees preventing the company from launching these chips into space, because recovering them from an orbit moving at 17,500 miles per hour is practically impossible.

The bonds were sold on Thursday, November 19, through an independent entity managed by "Apollo" called the "Saturn Financing Fund", which buys the chips and leases them to SpaceX, keeping the debt off the company's balance sheet. The largest tranche reached about $25.2 billion at a yield of 8.259% — far higher than the 5.95% average for good corporate bonds on the same day — and the deal was led by "Goldman Sachs" and "Apollo", with "BlackRock" and "KKR" as major investors.

The financing, which exceeds $40 billion with bank loans, covers the purchase of equipment worth about $47.3 billion, mostly the new "Rubin" chips from Nvidia, starting to arrive in January 2027, and leased to SpaceX for about five years. The bonds received an "A3" rating from "Moody's", but whether the "stay on Earth" condition entered the final documents or not remains unknown.

The figures reveal the scale of the bet: revenues of SpaceX's AI unit grew from $2.6 billion in the second quarter to $7.6 billion in the third quarter, but it spent $16.4 billion on equipment in the same quarter. The company expects to spend an additional $35 to $40 billion in the fourth quarter alone.

Simply put: imagine borrowing the price of a huge fleet of trucks, and the bank requiring you not to travel with them to a place it cannot reach to recover them. SpaceX borrows to buy chips, and the creditors want them on Earth, not in orbit.
