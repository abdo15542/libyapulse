💳 Europe gives you the right to challenge a loan decision made by a computer!

20 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

New European rules to protect borrowers have taken effect today, expanding protection to loans of up to 100,000 euros, and covering for the first time many 'buy now, pay later' agreements spreading in online stores. The rules require lenders to show standardized information about the cost, display the annual interest rate and the total amount you will pay, verify your ability to repay, and generally give you 14 days to withdraw from the contract.

The most important point concerns AI: if a lender uses automated software to analyze your data and decide your loan eligibility, you have the right to request human intervention and a review of the decision, challenge incorrect data, and explain circumstances the automated assessment did not capture. The rules also require greater clarity in advertising, and generally ban misleading practices and forcing extra products with the loan.

Simply put: before, the computer alone decided whether you deserved a loan, without you knowing why your application was rejected; now you have the right to ask a human to review the decision, and to know the full cost before you sign.

Do you think the right to human review will really protect borrowers, or will automated decisions remain the decisive ones?
