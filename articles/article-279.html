💰 Fake crypto platforms steal $37 million from 1,500 investors!

20 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission has accused eight companies of running fake investment and crypto platforms that stole more than $37 million from more than 1,500 American investors, through a scheme lasting about two years.

The scheme worked through four successive pairs of an 'investment advisor' and a 'trading platform', each pair appearing for a few months then disappearing, replaced by a new pair under different names, including 'Tech Brokerage Asset Alliance', 'Idea Crypto', 'Real Simple Crypto', and 'Essential Crypto'. The scammers attracted victims with social media ads leading them to chat groups, where people claiming to be advisors gave stock-trading tips, then convinced them to transfer their money to a crypto trading platform. The platforms' apps showed fake profits, while no cryptocurrency was ever bought, and neither the advisors nor the platforms had any real existence. To add credibility, the scammers sent forged certificates from the commission, and referred skeptics to real regulatory filings that imply no official approval. The commission says the operators likely work from outside the United States, and is seeking permanent court orders, recovery of funds, and fines.

Simply put: imagine a store displaying luxurious goods in its window, but completely empty inside; that is how the platforms were: a beautiful facade, fake profit numbers, and nothing real behind them.

Before investing your money in any platform, do you verify its official license yourself?
