🚀 Starship returns from orbit and is caught by giant steel arms — for the first time in history!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

On 14 November, SpaceX succeeded in catching the upper stage of its giant Starship rocket as it returned from Earth's orbit, in a historic step towards fully reusable rockets. The rocket, which is 124 metres long, had launched on its fifteenth test flight from Starbase in Texas on 13 November, and completed a journey of about 22 and a half hours, during which it circled the Earth 14 times and deployed 26 Starlink satellites.

After completing its mission, the rocket returned to the launch site, where it was caught by a giant pair of robotic arms resembling chopsticks, in what a company spokesperson described as "the missing link" and "the holy grail of spaceflight". Catching the upper stage was harder than catching the lower booster, because it flies higher and faster, while the NASA director congratulated the company on the achievement, describing it as "like magic".

Not everything went as planned: the lower booster, Super Heavy, was also supposed to be caught, but a fuel shortage forced the company to divert it to a landing in the Gulf of Mexico, and it is now investigating the cause of the problem. Starship still faces another major challenge: proving in-orbit refuelling, something that has never been done before, before it carries astronauts to the Moon on the Artemis 3 mission, scheduled for mid-2027.

Simply put: a rocket flies to space and comes back to be caught in mid-air instead of being thrown into the sea — will space travel soon become as cheap as air travel?
