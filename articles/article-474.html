💾 SK Hynix Becomes the Largest Shareholder in Rival Kioxia — 14.1% of the Storage Giant!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

South Korean company SK Hynix announced that it will become the largest shareholder in its Japanese rival Kioxia after converting convertible bonds into shares during December.

The company will convert bonds worth 129 billion yen, or about $810 million, into 129,000 ordinary shares, in addition to buying 1,001 extra shares from Bain Capital, bringing its indirect stake in Kioxia to about 14.1%, ahead of Toshiba, which holds 10.46% and ranks second.

The company confirmed it has no current plans to buy additional shares or sell them, and that its participation in Kioxia's management will remain limited after the deal completes, even though it has become its largest shareholder.

The move comes at a time of strong demand for NAND storage chips, used in everything from smartphones to AI data center servers. SK Hynix is the world's second-largest maker of NAND chips with a 19% share, while Kioxia ranks fourth with a 14% share.

SK Hynix invested about 395 billion yen in 2018 in the Bain Capital-led alliance that bought Toshiba Memory, which later became Kioxia and was listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in December 2024.

Simply put: a Korean memory company will become the largest owner in its Japanese rival, not by acquiring the whole company, but by converting old debts into shares, at a time when demand for storage chips is intensifying because of AI.
