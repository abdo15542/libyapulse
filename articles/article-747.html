📦 Amazon Warns New York: A New Law Could Add $5 to Every Order!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Amazon has begun warning its customers in New York City that a bill before the City Council could raise the cost of every delivery order by up to $5, in a rare public campaign that puts the company in direct confrontation with the Council and the city's mayor.

The bill, called the 'Delivery Protection Act,' requires companies that operate last-mile delivery stations to obtain a license from the city, and to employ drivers and parcel sorters directly instead of relying on contracted companies, including part-time drivers who work more than eight delivery hours within 30 days. The city's mayor supports the bill, while Amazon says it threatens more than 40 local delivery companies it works with, employing more than 5,000 people, and that it may have to move its stations outside the city, meaning slower delivery and higher prices.

The company bases its $5 figure on a consulting study estimating the law could raise city households' costs by $409 to $664 a year, even though the bill's text itself includes no extra fees on orders - the possible increase is the company's estimate, not an imposed fee. The company attached its warning in the app and on the website to a ready-made form that sends a message to the local council member objecting to the bill, used by tens of thousands of customers within a day and a half.

Simply put: a battle over who pays the price of protecting workers: the company or the customer. Would you support employing delivery drivers directly even if your order price rises, or do you prefer prices to stay as they are?
