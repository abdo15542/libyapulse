🔒 Backblaze may sell your data for ads.. and opting out is in your hands before 1 December!

22 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Cloud backup company Backblaze has worried its customers after updating its privacy policy, which takes effect on 1 December 2026. The new text says the company's use of tracking technologies "may be considered a sale or sharing" of personal information with third parties for behavioural advertising purposes, and the categories covered include identifiers such as name, email and internet address, commercial information, internet and network activity, and geographic location.

What is striking is that the previous policy, updated in April 2026, stated explicitly that the company would not rent, exchange or share customers' addresses and emails with other companies for marketing purposes without consent; that promise has disappeared from the new text, replaced by an admission that these categories are actually being sold or shared for advertising purposes over the past twelve months.

There is an important distinction to make clear: the policy talks about personal information about the customer, not the files they upload for backup; the company's frequently asked questions state explicitly that stored files are not personal information and are not sold or shared. As for anyone who wants to opt out of the sale or sharing linked to tracking, they can do so from the "Privacy Choices" link at the bottom of the company's website, or by turning on the global privacy signal in their browser.

Simply put: a company that keeps your files is opening the door to selling your personal data for ads, while the files themselves are outside the sale — will you review your privacy settings before the decision takes effect?
