🕊️ Trump and Xi Agree: No Military Decisions by AI Without Humans!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping have agreed that humans must always be involved in military decisions that use AI technologies, even as the two countries compete for AI supremacy, according to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Friday, after returning from Trump's meeting with Xi in Shenzhen, China — describing the agreement as one of the major achievements of Trump's Asian trip.

Bessent said: "I think it was very important that President Trump and President Xi agreed that humans must always be involved in any military decisions made by superintelligence, and that superintelligence should not be allowed to be autonomous in major military operations" — referring to the Trump administration's preferred term for the technology, "superintelligence". He added that the US-China agreement on military use of the technology, which requires human involvement and other safeguards, will not be affected by the two countries' competition for AI supremacy, and that American AI labs must maintain their technical lead as the best defense against "dangerous" Chinese AI models used in cyberattacks.

Bessent added: "Competition has nothing to do with saying both sides agree that no major military operation should be launched without human involvement." He said the espionage allegations concerning former "Nvidia" CEO Jensen Huang and a Chinese citizen he was in a relationship with were not raised in the bilateral meeting between Trump and Xi.

Bessent also said that Nvidia's "H200" chip, the second-tier AI chip, remains the most advanced chip China can buy with licenses, after Trump took what he described as a "very firm line". He added: "I think what we've seen is a scarcity of Chinese requests for H200 chips, while they try to build their own chip stack, and I think they're probably many years behind." Bessent downplayed disagreements he showed with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over AI chip restrictions, saying Trump administration officials are "all committed to following President Trump's instructions". The Commerce Department had taken steps on Monday to tighten China's access to advanced AI chips, including those used in offshore data centers where Chinese entities can rent computing power.

Simply put: the agreement means the decision for a major war stays in human hands, not a machine's — even if AI is the one analyzing the data and proposing the plans.
