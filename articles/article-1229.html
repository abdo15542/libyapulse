🇰🇪 Claims of a Breach of Kenya's eCitizen Platform… 740 GB of Identity Data Under Investigation!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Kenyan authorities are investigating claims of a major breach of the government eCitizen platform that could expose hundreds of gigabytes of personal identity records and government databases.

A hacking group calling itself ByteToBreach claims it breached the platform's infrastructure and stole about 740 GB: 600 GB of KYC documents, 100 GB of database content, and 40 GB of source code.

Cyber intelligence firm VCERT Analyzer spotted the claim in an alert published on Monday, and said the attackers exploited a vulnerability in the platform's file-upload system to execute malicious code through what is known as a web shell.

Advertisement

The group also claims the breach extended to the OAuth single sign-on systems, which could affect other institutions that rely on the platform for identity verification.

But the firm itself warned against treating the claim as established fact: neither the Kenyan government nor the institutions mentioned have officially confirmed the breach or the size of the stolen data yet.

Among the bodies that could be affected: the Social Health Authority, the Directorate of Criminal Investigations, telecom company Safaricom, and the National Police Service, because they rely on the platform's systems for identity verification.

Simply put: a serious claim of huge scale, but still a claim with no public evidence; the difference between 'a group claims it stole data' and 'a confirmed theft' is what separates real news from a rumor, and the ongoing government investigation is what will settle it.

Would you believe hackers' claims of stealing data before the affected party confirms them?
