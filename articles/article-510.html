👁️ A Tiny Chip That Restores Sight to the Blind — and $400 Million to Bring It to America!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Company Science Corporation raised $400 million in a new funding round announced on November 17, taking its market value to $3.5 billion and bringing its total raised since its founding in 2021 to more than $900 million.

The company is led by Max Hodak, former president of Neuralink, and its first product is the Prima system: a tiny 2x2 mm chip implanted under the retina, with 378 pixels, working with glasses fitted with a camera that projects the scene onto the chip, turning light into electrical signals that stimulate the retina in place of the cells destroyed by disease.

The system targets patients with age-related dry macular degeneration, one of the most common causes of vision loss in older adults. It has received the European CE mark and is available in Germany, and is now also being tested for Stargardt disease, which causes blindness.

A clinical trial showed that 80% of patients had significantly improved visual acuity after 12 months, and 84% of them could read letters, numbers, and words at home with the system's help — an average improvement of 25 letters on the eye chart.

The company will use the new funding to reach the US market through the FDA's Humanitarian Device Exemption pathway, hoping to make it available to American patients early next year, while working in the long term on a bio-hybrid sensor that connects implanted nerve cells directly to electronic circuits.

Simply put: imagine your retina has lost its light-sensitive cells, and a tiny chip arrives to replace them and restore your ability to read letters — that is Prima.
