💰 Oracle Says Its Contract with OpenAI Is Fully Funded — $300 Billion and 4.5 GW of New Capacity!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Oracle announced on Friday that the cloud infrastructure contract it signed with OpenAI is now fully funded, after completing its latest funding round earlier this month — ending months of investor doubts about its ability to pay the expansion bill.

The company had raised about $20 billion in that latest round, split almost evenly between $10 billion from selling shares and $10 billion from debt — the amount that completes the funding plan it presented on investor day, including supplier financing. The company's shares jumped about 3.3% in evening trading after the announcement.

The contract concerns a five-year computing agreement worth about $300 billion, representing a large part of Oracle's future commitments. Under it, the two companies plan to build 4.5 GW of additional data center capacity in the United States, above the first Stargate site in the city of Abilene, Texas.

The company said the Abilene complex, where the GPT-6 Astra model was trained, is nearing completion, and that its next large-scale site in Shackelford County, Texas, has received all permits, and that delivery of Nvidia's new Vera Rubin racks is proceeding as planned over the coming weeks.

Simply put: Oracle was borrowing billions of dollars to build huge data centers to run OpenAI's models, and investors worried the money would not be enough — so the company came out to say the funding is complete and construction is proceeding as planned.
