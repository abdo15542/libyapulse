🧠 Study: Heavy Screen Time Is Linked to Changes in Teen Brains — and Memory Is Affected First!

22 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A new study from the University of Eastern Finland, published on 19 November in the journal NeuroImage: Reports, found that long screen time among teenagers is linked to clear differences in brain structure. Researchers scanned 44 healthy teenagers aged 16 to 19 with MRI and found that those who spend more time in front of screens and smart devices have a smaller volume of grey matter in the medial temporal lobe, the brain's centre responsible for memory and learning. In contrast, heavy screen use was linked to a larger volume of grey matter in areas of the frontal and occipital lobes responsible for attention, planning and image processing.

The researchers explain this cautiously: the teenage brain normally goes through a natural 'pruning' process in which neural networks shed excess connections to become faster and more efficient, so a larger remaining volume of grey matter may mean this process is delayed in heavy screen users, or it may simply reflect the demands of apps and games. The study also found that better physical fitness is linked to a larger volume in the parietal lobe, responsible for attention and spatial awareness. Lead researcher Hanna Mari Skog stresses that the study is small and captures a single moment, that it proves an association rather than causation, and that understanding the long-term effect needs more research.

Simply put: screens do not 'damage' the brain as is often claimed, but they do leave a measurable mark on memory and attention areas — so will you cut back your children's daily screen hours after this finding?
