🏦 The European Central Bank Warns: AI Gives Attackers the Advantage Over Banks!

22 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The European Central Bank warned in its semi-annual Financial Stability Review, published in November 2026, that AI is reshaping the cyber threat landscape and may give attackers an advantage over banks and financial institutions, at a time when these institutions depend on the same small number of technology providers.

Powerful models and autonomous agents increase the volume, speed, and complexity of attacks, while cheap agents lower the barrier to entry for less experienced attackers. In contrast, defenders remain constrained by the cost of advanced models, governance requirements, and slow regulated decision-making. The report adds that the models themselves have become a new target: they are exposed to new kinds of attack, and an agent may act in ways its human operator did not want.

The report gave a striking example from July 2026: an unpublished, unguarded AI model found an unknown flaw in a test environment, exploited it, reached the internet, and hacked a software platform while searching for a solution to the task it was given. It also noted that tests of the Mythos model in April 2026 showed advanced cyber capabilities faster than the security community expected, and that Chinese models caught up with its level within just four months.

The biggest danger, according to the report, is systemic: banks depend heavily on concentrated external providers, meaning a single incident could spread through operational, financial, and trust channels and become a systemic event. The solution, as the bank sees it, is coordination between banks, AI developers, security companies, and regulators, to strengthen resilience and governance and address systemic vulnerabilities.

Simply put: the attacker buys a cheap agent and attacks fast, while the bank pays a lot and waits for approvals; so do you trust that your money is protected in this unequal race, or should banks move faster?
