💰 Day One Halts Its $5 Billion US Listing... Are Investors Starting to Doubt the AI Data Center Bubble?

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Singapore-based data center company Day One has halted plans for its US initial public offering, which aimed to raise up to $5 billion in the final quarter of the year, at a valuation of around $20 billion.

The company had filed to list on Nasdaq under the ticker DODC on October 5, with banks including Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citigroup and BNP Paribas, but it began reconsidering its options as data center and AI stocks swung, weighing a delay or a smaller offering, while a 2027 listing remains possible if market conditions improve.

The problem lies in customer concentration: its largest customer, ByteDance, the owner of TikTok according to reports, accounts for 69.2% of revenue in the first half of 2026, with Oracle second at around 15%, leaving the company dependent on a single customer for most of its income.

The numbers tell a story of rocketing growth that has not yet turned into profit: revenue jumped from $178.1 million in 2024 to $484.3 million in 2025, then reached $512 million in the first half of 2026 alone, but the company recorded a net loss of $367.1 million in 2025, mostly stock compensation, and it operates 962 megawatts of working capacity, with bookings of around 2.3 gigawatts as of September 20.

Day One is not an isolated case: it is the third data center listing to stumble this autumn, after Australian firm Firmus Grid withdrew an offering of around $5 billion, and ByteDance-linked AirTrunk delayed a property listing in Singapore to next year.

Pressure increased with a new US rule issued on November 16 requiring individual export licenses for Blackwell-class chips and above headed to data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil — nearly all of the company's Asian markets.

Simply put: demand for data centers has not faded, but investors have started asking a new question: will huge bookings turn into real money on time?... Will the public market become the first real judge of the AI boom?

If capital markets stop funding data center construction without limit, who pays the bill for the next expansion?
