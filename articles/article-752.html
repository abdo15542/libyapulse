🏭 Samsung Makes the Memory Hearts for AMD and Nvidia in Texas!

22 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A report on November 3 revealed that Samsung will use its second chip factory in Taylor, Texas, in the United States to manufacture HBM memory base dies for AMD and Nvidia, in a step that brings the Korean company back strongly into the advanced chip manufacturing race after years of struggle.

The memory base die is the 'logic heart' that controls high-bandwidth memory: Samsung manufactures these base dies in Texas and then connects them to memory chips produced in its factories in South Korea, to deliver complete memory units ready for AI accelerators. Most of the capacity booked in the new factory is allocated to AMD, according to the report.

The new factory is around 2.7 million square feet in size, about one and a half times larger than the first Taylor factory, which operates under a contract with Tesla, and it will run on 2-nanometer technology. Construction is scheduled to begin officially during November, with equipment installation starting in February 2029 and mass production beginning in 2030.

The deal comes at a time when Samsung is focusing all its efforts on stabilizing 2-nanometer production, while it has delayed its mass production target for 1.4-nanometer technology to 2029, behind its rival TSMC, which targets mass production of 1.4-nanometer technology in 2028, and Intel, which also targets mass production of 1.4-nanometer technology in 2028.

Simply put: whoever manufactures the memory base die holds the key to AI accelerators, and Samsung chose to start from Texas. Do you think this deal will bring the company back into real competition with TSMC, or is it a small step in a long race?
