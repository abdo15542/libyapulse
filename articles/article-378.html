🧊 An Abu Dhabi Court Freezes $6.5 Billion Worldwide After the Biggest Crypto Theft Ever!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

"Binance" has obtained a worldwide freezing order worth $6.5 billion from the courts of the Abu Dhabi Global Market, against unknown persons behind the theft of about $6.34 billion from the platform's wallets on October 29 — the largest crypto theft in history. The order prevents those involved from disposing of assets up to that ceiling, and requires them to disclose their worldwide assets exceeding $50,000 before November 30.

A separate order directs 18 companies, including "Circle" and "BitGo", to keep the stolen assets frozen without lending, swapping, or mixing them, and the court will decide on December 1 whether to extend the order and whether the companies must hand over what they froze. About $2.85 billion of the stolen funds have been frozen or intercepted by third parties so far, while "Binance" says it has already compensated customers from its own funds.

US authorities say the FBI attributes the theft to the "TraderTraitor" group linked to North Korea. Notably, the freezing order can be served via the blockchain itself, so legal notices reach the wallet addresses linked to the stolen funds directly. "Tether", which froze more than $1.56 billion of its stablecoin, is working with "Binance" directly outside the scope of the order.

Simply put: $6.5 billion is not recovered money, but a legal ceiling that stops the thieves from moving what they stole — and freezing alone doesn't return the money to its owners.
