📱 Hack shuts down Colorado's digital ID app — and 150,000 people are left without a digital driving licence!

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The US state of Colorado has suspended its official app "myColorado" after a cyber incident discovered on the evening of 18 November, which may have put the data of between 50,000 and 150,000 of its 2.1 million users in the attackers' hands.

The app stores digital copies of driving licences, identity cards, vehicle registrations, and insurance cards, and residents use it to prove their identity and access government services, so shutting it down before the Thanksgiving holiday, the biggest travel season in the US, hit travellers directly.

The state announced the incident on 23 November, five days after discovering it, saying the app will stay down until the investigation into the cause and scope is finished, the flaw is fixed, and it is determined what data was accessed, with no set date for the service to return.

Advertisement

Authorities have not yet revealed how the attacker got in, what data was copied exactly, or who was affected, but they advised residents to carry a physical driving licence or paper identity card to prove their identity, especially at airports, adding that anyone who wants a digital ID on their phone can add it to another approved digital wallet.

The state said there is no evidence the incident is part of a wider attack, and that it will notify everyone whose data was affected directly once the investigation ends, while experts warned of fake messages claiming to help restore the digital ID, because they could be a new trap.

Simply put: a digital ID app for an entire state stopped overnight, and hundreds of thousands of residents went back to the paper card. Do you rely entirely on your phone to prove your identity, or do you always keep a paper copy?

Do you trust that your digital identity data is protected, or is a card in your pocket still the safest option?
