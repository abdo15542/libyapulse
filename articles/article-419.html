📺 Netflix's Chief Admits: The App May Bury What You're Looking For Under What You Don't Want!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

"Netflix" co-CEO Ted Sarandos admitted that the app sometimes makes mistakes in how it presents new content, burying what the viewer is looking for under new titles they didn't come for. The admission came in an interview with "Bloomberg" published on November 18, as part of "The Circuit" program with Emily Chang.

Sarandos said the platform, with more than 325 million subscribers, faces growing pressure to deliver successful works constantly, and that the hit rate has become more important than ever, especially in the United States. He pointed to AI as a tool to improve content discovery, as well as helping in production stages, stressing that "Netflix" focuses on storytelling ambition instead of chasing everything people watch for a few seconds or relying on AI-generated content, in a world he said is drowning in low-quality content. The platform already uses a generative-AI workflow in hundreds of titles, like creating crowds and historical battle scenes, which saved money while its content spending rose about 10% this year.

He added that the "streaming wars" are still ongoing, and that half of TV engagement in the United States is still traditional television, even though streaming accounted for 49.6% of TV viewing in August versus 36.4% for broadcast and cable combined. He also revealed that "Netflix" has become more open to cinema releases, after trying to buy "Warner Bros. Discovery" studios and then withdrawing from the deal, extending the cinema run of the film "Narnia: The Magician's Nephew" from two weeks to seven weeks.

Simply put: a huge library means more choices, but it also means what you want may get lost among hundreds of titles you don't want.

Do you find what you're looking for easily on Netflix, or do you spend more time browsing than watching?
