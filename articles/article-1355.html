🧠 U.S. approval for the first pivotal brain-chip trial.. Neuralink tests thought control in ALS patients!

26 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Neuralink has received approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration to launch Link, the country's first pivotal trial of an implanted brain-computer interface, to test its technology in adults living with severe paralysis caused by amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS).

The trial aims to assess the safety and effectiveness of the system in enabling participants to control digital devices with their thoughts, focusing on practical outcomes that improve their independence in daily life, with follow-up extending up to 24 months after implantation.

The system relies on the N1 chip, which is surgically implanted in the skull and inserts fine flexible threads into the motor cortex of the brain using a surgical robot, reading neural signals and turning them into commands to control computers and phones.

The trial builds on earlier work involving more than 30 participants who have received the implant so far, including the first ALS patient who last year managed to communicate using only his brain signals, and enrollment in the new trial is now open at multiple sites across the United States.

It is important to distinguish between two things: the approval covers conducting the trial only, and does not mean the chip is approved for general medical use, as the device is still experimental and available only within approved clinical research.

Simply put: a chip in the brain that turns the intention to move into a tap on the screen, and now the decisive trial begins that could open the road to the market; would you implant a chip in your brain if it gave you back your independence?

If you lost the ability to move, would you accept trying to control your device with your thoughts?
