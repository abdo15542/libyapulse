🔢 The Lean and Mathlib Communities Endorse a Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.. Has the Most Famous Problem in Mathematics Finally Been Solved?

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Just one day after a mathematician working at Anthropic announced that he had solved the Riemann Hypothesis with the help of the Claude chatbot, the endorsement everyone was waiting for arrived: the Lean and Mathlib communities — the two communities that support the Lean mathematical proof language and its main library — announced on 21 November that they fully trust the proof's correctness, saying in a joint statement: 'We endorse that the statement and proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct.'

The proof was published on 20 November as a huge software library of about 17 million lines in the Lean language, spread across more than 175,000 files. What the community did within a single day was independently verify every step: researchers at MIT and elsewhere rebuilt the entire library without errors, confirmed that the proof proves the correct statement of the hypothesis itself as recorded in the Mathlib library, not a different version of it, and that several independent checking programs written in different languages accepted the proof, and that it did not rely on any extra axiom beyond the three standard axioms.

But the story is not over yet: the Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the hypothesis among its unsolved problems, and its $1 million prize is conditional on publication in a peer-reviewed journal and surviving two years of scrutiny, while no human has yet understood how the proof actually works — the entire logic is buried inside millions of lines of code. As for the competing human proof published on the same day, researchers reported problems in it that are still being addressed.

Simply put: a machine proved the hardest problem in mathematics, and mathematicians themselves believe it without yet understanding it; do you trust a proof no human can read?
