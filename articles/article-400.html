🛡️ The 'PHP2Shell' Flaw — AI Hacks Before the Fix Arrives!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Last October, two dangerous flaws were revealed in the "PHP" language that powers about 70% of websites, and exploitation attempts began four full days before they were officially announced, on October 21. "Qualys" counted 167 million copies of the language on its customers' systems, and just twelve hours after the announcement, about one in every thousand had updated their system, while between 2.6 and 4.4 million copies remained exposed outside hosting companies' protection — most of them on versions that will never receive a fix.

And more alarming is that the speed of hacking itself has changed: on August 31, a single operator used AI agents against a print server, building a test lab and writing an exploit for two flaws announced just four days earlier — from an empty workspace to the first breach took less than four hours, then 11 organizations fell in 26 seconds, and the count eventually reached 395 organizations in 48 countries. Building an exploit took professionals 22 days on average in 2017; today it's built in minutes or hours.

The fix, meanwhile, remains as slow as ever: the average time to fully fix a flaw is 43 days, and only 26% of known exploited flaws get fully fixed, and last month alone saw 13,000 classified flaws. Even an unpublished "Claude" model produced a remote exploit for a Linux kernel flaw in under an hour, while two famous distributions were still without a fix.

Simply put: the hacker now reaches your flaw within hours, while fixing it takes weeks — and the race is no longer between humans, but between an AI that attacks and one that defends.

Do you update your software as soon as fixes are released, or delay them until it's too late?
