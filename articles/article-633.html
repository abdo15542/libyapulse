🎬 FFmpeg 9.1 arrives with quantum-resistant signatures!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The FFmpeg project released version 9.1, codenamed "Gauss", on November 20, in an update that makes the world's most famous media tool more secure and faster.

The highlight of the release is that its files now carry quantum-resistant digital signatures, using the ML-DSA and SLH-DSA algorithms, under the new RFC 9980 standard.

The idea is that future quantum computers may one day break traditional signatures, so users can now verify that the file they downloaded is genuine and untampered — even in a post-quantum world.

The release adds improvements in AAC audio encoding, a new APV video encoder that works via Vulkan, and Nvidia's frame-interpolation technology, which generates intermediate frames to make motion smoother.

It also supports encoding and decoding ASTC images, splitting H.264 data, and a new graph-based data-filtering interface.

FFmpeg is a free, open-source command-line tool that powers millions of applications and services, from video conversion to live streaming and audio editing — so much so that most of the platforms you watch video on depend on it in the background.

The release comes about three months after version 9.0, in a regular update rhythm that keeps the tool in step with the latest audio and video technologies.

Simply put: the tool that converts every video you watch now signs its files with a technique that future computers cannot break.

Did you know that a single free tool sits behind most of the video on the internet?
