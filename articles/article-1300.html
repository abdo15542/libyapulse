🧸 New York bans AI chatbot toys for 5 years — dolls that talk to children!

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed a law on 24 November imposing a five-year ban on manufacturing, distributing and selling toys with built-in AI chatbots, such as talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that use the internet to hold conversations with children.

The law takes effect 90 days after signing, on 22 February 2027, meaning these toys will stay on shelves during the current shopping season, but violators after that will face civil fines of up to $15,000 per day per violation, enforced by the state's Attorney General.

The decision came after a national consumer watchdog tested a sample of AI chatbot toys aimed at children aged 3 to 12 and found that some talked at length about sexual topics, told children where to find matches in the house, and acted upset when the child had to leave, with almost no parental control tools and safety limits weakening the longer the conversation went on.

The new law builds on a law the governor signed in October banning companies from offering unsafe chatbot features to minors, such as features that encourage emotional attachment, self-harm or explicit sexual conversations, or that present the chatbot as human.

The governor said parents should not have to worry that chatbots disguised as toys might expose their children to inappropriate conversations, manipulate their feelings or put their privacy at risk, and that the ban gives the state time to study the technology's impact and set permanent rules before the market outruns the law.

Simply put: a toy that talks to your child over the internet may seem innocent, but tests showed it may say things no child should hear; so New York decided to stop selling them for five full years until the risks are understood first.
