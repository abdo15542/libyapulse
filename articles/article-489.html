🌼 Daisy Rose-27B Doubles an Open Model's Abilities — With Smart Training, Not More Data!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Radix Arc launched on November 18 its first model, Daisy Rose-27B, an open-weight model for coding and agents, built on the Qwen 3.8-27B model and trained with reinforcement learning only, without any supervised fine-tuning or distillation.

The model was trained on 1,200 carefully selected agent tasks, with sessions extending up to one million tokens, over 18 days on 144 Nvidia H200 cards, using the open-source reinforcement learning framework Miles, in which the agent and the trainer work at the same time without waiting for each other.

The standout result: the model's score in the Terminal Bench 4.1 test for terminal tasks jumped from 5.6% to 12.0%, more than double, and it also improved in every other test, such as Deep SWE from 42.2% to 46.2%, SWE-bench Pro from 91.3% to 94.7%, and GPQA Diamond from 89.2% to 90.7%.

The model keeps a context window of 256,000 tokens, runs on a single graphics card, and its weights are available under the Apache 2.0 license in three formats. The training also achieved high efficiency: useful compute utilization reached 37% versus 16% in a similar open-source trainer, meaning a 57% lower training cost, and a speed of 16,800 tokens per second across the 144 cards.

Simply put: instead of training a bigger model on more data, the company took an existing open model and trained it smartly on long, hard tasks, doubling its ability to complete real coding tasks.

A small open model beats its base with smart training... is the future for open models or giant closed ones?
