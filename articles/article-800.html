🌏 13 Countries Unite Against Online Scam Centers, With No Sanctions or Arrests Yet!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Thirteen countries issued a joint statement of principles to confront online fraud centers in Southeast Asia, East Asia, and the Pacific, in a wide coordination step against scam networks targeting victims around the world.

The statement was issued on November 19 by the foreign ministries of Singapore and Australia, and was signed alongside them by the United States, Canada, Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, New Zealand, the Philippines, South Korea, Thailand, the United Kingdom, and Vietnam. The statement contains ten principles, including pursuing the leaders of criminal networks, exchanging evidence, notifying countries in advance of operations, coordinating through Interpol, and protecting victims of human trafficking.

But the statement remains only a declaration of intent: no new sanctions, no new money, and no arrests. The countries limited themselves to saying they would 'consider' targeted economic sanctions and entry bans on those involved, and that they 'intend' to return recovered funds to victims wherever local laws allow.

The countries also pledged to strengthen cooperation with the private sector, including cryptocurrency platforms, financial institutions, and telecom companies, and to treat people forced to work in scam centers as victims rather than criminals, and not to punish them for acts that were a direct result of their exploitation.

Simply put: 13 countries agreed on one language against fraud, but an agreement alone does not close a single center; so are good intentions enough when the networks move faster than the laws?
