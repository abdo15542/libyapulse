🇪🇸 Spain Arrests the First 'Real Cell' of a Russian Hacking Group… Weapons, Ammunition and Bitcoin Wallets in the House!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Spain's National Police announced they have arrested the man they say was leading the first real on-the-ground cell of the pro-Russian hacking group 'NoName057(16)' in Spain — a development that moves the group from computer screens into the real world for the first time.

Police arrested the man, a 51-year-old Spaniard, in the city of Burgos on Tuesday 17 November, saying he was carrying out propaganda and recruitment for the group, posting calls on social media to attack state institutions cybernetically and confront the police, and acting as a middleman who gathered new supporters and sent them to the group's recruitment system — as well as distributing stickers bearing its logo and symbols linking to its official channels in eight cities, including Burgos, Valladolid, Zaragoza and Madrid.

The suspect did not take part in carrying out the attacks himself, but encouraged and promoted them, and police say his arrest foiled the start of violent acts the group was planning, as its activity in Spain had so far been limited to cyberattacks. He is accused of belonging to a cyber-terrorist organization and promoting terrorism, and the court released him under precautionary conditions while the investigation continues — the charges have not yet been proven.

When his home was searched, police found more than just computer equipment: a semi-automatic rifle with a folding stock, several short rifles and hunting rifles, high-capacity ammunition magazines, and more than 5,000 rounds, including military ammunition, as well as homemade spiked clubs, detachable double-edged spears, a knuckle duster, unlicensed pepper spray, containers of chemicals, 10 gas canisters, and three hardware wallets for digital currencies holding Bitcoin whose source police are still verifying.

The group is blamed for at least 447 cyberattacks in Spain since 2024, targeting institutions, banks and transport companies. It formed in March 2022, shortly after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, and targets NATO, the European Union and Western allies — and the arrest came just one day after the EU Council imposed sanctions on the group on 16 November, freezing its funds and banning the provision of any resources to it.

Simply put: a group that lived only inside the browser suddenly has a home address and an arsenal of weapons... Are financial sanctions enough to stop a hacking group, or does the real danger begin when it moves from the internet to the street?

If a hacking group can recruit supporters and build a cell in your city without anyone knowing, how do you tell online propaganda from a real danger?
