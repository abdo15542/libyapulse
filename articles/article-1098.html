🛡️ German Army Tests Suicide Drones and Target-Picking AI in Lithuania!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The German Army began on Sunday its first field tests of suicide drones and an AI program that identifies targets, at the Rūdninkai training ground in Lithuania, near the border with Belarus, as part of the "Freedom Shield" exercises.

Suicide drones — also called loitering munitions — are small unmanned aircraft that hover in the air until they find their target, then dive into it and explode — a weapon used only once. German soldiers tested drones from Helsing and Stark, fitted with non-explosive warheads, and they hit tanks placed as targets.

The soldiers also tested Helsing's "Clarity" program, an AI system that gathers information from satellites, reconnaissance drones and ground sensors, analyzes it faster than a human, and suggests likely targets and appropriate actions to commanders. The program only suggests — the decision to attack stays in human hands.

German Army chief Lieutenant General Christian Freuding watched the trials and described attack drones and AI targeting systems as essential technologies for modern warfare.

The exercises run between 6 November and 3 December, with soldiers from eight NATO countries taking part, and they support the deployment of a permanent German armored brigade in Lithuania of 5,000 soldiers by the end of 2027 — the first permanent deployment of the German Army outside its own country since World War II.

Simply put: Germany is testing, for the first time, drones that destroy themselves on their target and an AI program that helps commanders make decisions... so does AI make wars more precise or more dangerous?

If AI suggests the target and a human presses the button, who bears responsibility for a mistake?
