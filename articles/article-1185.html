🧠 “Nary Q” Squeezes a Huge AI Model Into a 10 GB File! Without Losing Its Smarts!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Researchers published a paper on Wednesday, November 19, about a new method called “Nary Q,” which compresses the open AI model Qwen 3.8-27B to about 3 bits per parameter, in a file of just 10.1 GB, while preserving its full performance.

The problem the method solves is an old one: standard compression methods are trained on short texts from the web, so they succeed in short tests, then collapse when the model works as an agent calling tools across hundreds of steps.

In the published results, models compressed to 3 bits or less solved only 72% of the long DeepSWE tasks compared with the full version, even though they matched it in short tests like AIME.

Nary Q, by contrast, is trained on tokens generated by the full model itself during real agentic work sessions, then compresses the weights using “lattice coding,” matching the full version on the two long agent tests DeepSWE and Terminal-Bench, and on AIME and RealWorldQA.

In fact, the 3-bit version even outperformed the full version on the two agent tests: it solved 47 tasks versus 44 on DeepSWE, and scored 63.8 versus 62.6 on Terminal-Bench.

The files were published for free on Hugging Face on November 20 under the Apache 2.0 license, along with a modified version of llama.cpp to run them, because the standard builds do not support its new format yet, and it needs a modern graphics card.

Simply put: a powerful AI model with 27 billion parameters now runs from a small file on your own device, without forgetting how to be an agent that gets long tasks done.
