💰 “Surveillance Pricing”! You Pay More Than Your Neighbor for the Same Item — and New York Is Moving to Stop It!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A new analysis has revealed that some companies now set the price of an item for you personally, not for the whole market, based on what they know about you: your browsing history, your apps, your smart devices, your location, your age, and your purchase history.

This practice is called “surveillance pricing”: algorithms work out the highest price you are willing to pay, and show you a higher price than the one your neighbor sees for the same item.

The nonprofit EPIC center explains that the goal is “inferring the highest price the customer is willing to pay, based on their personal data and other market data,” so you pay more simply because your data says you will.

Advertisement

Among the documented examples: Instacart practiced this pricing, and Consumer Reports found in 2025 that price differences could cost a family of four around $1,200 extra per year, and the company has since removed these tools.

In another example, the Target app was accused of charging $100 extra for a television if the customer bought it while standing inside the store, while Orbitz in 2012 was found showing more expensive hotels to Mac computer users.

This is different from the well-known “dynamic pricing,” like an Uber ride costing more at peak time, which depends on supply and demand in the market, while “surveillance pricing” depends on you.

The New York City Council voted this week by 44 votes to 5 to ban stores and services from setting prices based on personal data, while Seattle, the Federal Trade Commission, and regulators in other countries are moving in the same direction.

Simply put: the price is no longer the same for everyone; your data could make you pay more for the same item, and major cities have started to put a stop to it.

Have you ever noticed the price of an item change when you browsed it from your phone or from a different place?
