🎵 Tidal deletes 29 million AI-generated songs — and shuts the door on them for good!

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Music streaming platform Tidal announced on 24 November that it will start on 9 December deleting all fully AI-generated songs from its library, in an operation expected to wipe about 29 million songs before the end of the year, making it the first major streaming platform to ban this kind of music entirely instead of just labelling it.

The platform's CEO said more than 43% of the songs uploaded to the platform in the past 90 days were AI-generated, and that about 27 million songs — 6.3% of the whole library — were generated entirely by machine, with 96% of them never listened to even once, leaving real music buried under a flood of manufactured songs.

Under the new rules, the platform will reject about 80,000 to 85,000 AI-generated songs every day, prevent distributors from delivering them in the first place, and reject any song whose metadata indicates it was made entirely by AI, relying on an audio detection tool to sort new uploads.

The platform will still accept songs made with the help of AI, not entirely by it. It had started in June labelling fully AI-generated songs and denying them royalties, but that was not enough to stop the flood, so it decided to move to full deletion.

Simply put: when nearly half of what is uploaded daily is music made by a machine alone, and almost no one has listened to it, deletion turns from a punishment into a cleanup; the question now is whether the big platforms will follow Tidal's step or settle for labels.
