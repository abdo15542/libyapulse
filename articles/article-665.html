🚀 Falcon 9 launches at dawn on Sunday with US spy satellites — and the booster returns to land!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

SpaceX is preparing to launch a Falcon 9 rocket from Vandenberg base in California at dawn on Sunday, in a mission called "NROL-136" for the US National Reconnaissance Office, carrying a new batch of intelligence-gathering satellites into low Earth orbit.

The launch window opens at 4:16 a.m. Pacific time — 12:16 p.m. Greenwich time, and 2:16 p.m. Libya time. The mission will add an undisclosed number of satellites to the office's constellation, which it calls the "proliferated architecture" — the fourth launch of its kind this year and the fifteenth so far.

The mission will be carried by booster B1097 on its fourteenth flight, after previously launching missions including NROL-172, USSF-259 and Transporter-17, plus eight batches of Starlink satellites. About seven and a half minutes after liftoff, the booster will attempt to land at Landing Zone 4, next to the launch pad, instead of landing on a ship in the ocean.

If the landing succeeds, it will be the 38th landing at this site and the 680th Falcon booster landing to date. The mission's payload itself remains secret, as is customary for reconnaissance-office missions — neither the number of satellites nor their precise mission is announced.

Simply put: a rocket climbs into space with satellites whose number we don't know, then its booster returns to land on the ground as if it were an ordinary aircraft. That is how military launches have become an almost weekly routine.
