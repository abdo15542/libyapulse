📚 'Internet Archive' Staff Vote to Form a Union — to Protect the World's Largest Digital Library!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Employees of the "Internet Archive", the nonprofit that runs the famous "Wayback Machine", have announced their intention to form a union with the Communications Workers of America. More than 80 of 124 eligible US employees voted in favor of the union — a large majority.

The organizing committee asked the archive's founder, Brewster Kahle, for voluntary recognition of the union, and to join "CWA" Local 9415 in Oakland, California. At the same time, organizers filed a request for a union-certification election with the National Labor Relations Board, which they will withdraw if the archive voluntarily recognizes the union.

The proposed unit includes about 120 workers — librarians, engineers, technicians, project managers, media specialists, and facilities workers — working in the archive's building, a former church in San Francisco. Organizing began in February 2025, and organizers say their goals are job security, benefits, and living wages, and they see the union as a way to preserve the archive's mission: "universal access to all knowledge".

This move comes after Wikimedia Foundation employees won recognition of their union in September, and after organizers drew inspiration from the experiences of the Free Software Foundation and the Electronic Frontier Foundation. The archive, founded in 1996, claims to have archived more than a trillion web pages.

Simply put: the workers at the world's largest digital library want guarantees that protect them and protect the archive itself, so it stays available for future generations.
