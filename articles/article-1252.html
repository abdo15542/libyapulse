📺 European Police Shut Down 57 Illegal Streaming Services — and Trace €26 Million in Crypto!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

An international police operation has disabled 57 illegal TV streaming services that sold access to channels, matches and films without permission from the rights holders, racking up millions of visits a year.

The operation, coordinated from the Spanish city of Seville between November 16 and 20, involved law enforcement from 11 countries, including European states and the United States, alongside Europol, Interpol and the European Union Intellectual Property Office.

It led to the identification of 321 sites offering pirated content, with more than 7.3 million annual visits, as well as the tracing of cryptocurrency transactions worth over €26 million, with help from experts at Binance, Coinbase and Chainalysis.

Advertisement

These services typically work through cheap monthly subscriptions offering premium channels, live sports and films, collecting payments in cryptocurrency to hide the money trail and move it across borders easily.

Estimates from the European audiovisual anti-piracy association put legitimate providers' losses at around €4.83 billion a year, with about 60 million people in Europe using illegal platforms to watch content.

The operation is part of a series of international campaigns against pirated streaming, following earlier operations that shut down huge networks in 2024 and January 2026, though investigators admit operators can rebuild their infrastructure quickly.

Simply put: pirated streaming is not 'free TV' but a black market worth billions of euros; when you pay a subscription to an unknown operator in crypto, you are funding a criminal network — and your payment details could become the next target.
