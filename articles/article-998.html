❄️ A secret operation saves tens of thousands of fridges in Britain from hackers who were about to spoil the food!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Reports on November 20 revealed a secret operation that lasted days in Britain, saving tens of thousands of fridges and freezers in supermarkets, stadiums, warehouses, restaurants and petrol stations from a possible cyberattack.

The story began in late October, when an ethical security researcher working for a company specialized in measuring fridge temperatures discovered a major flaw in the most widespread refrigeration control units in Britain, made by the company RDM, and another security company confirmed the flaw and alerted the authorities.

Teams from the National Cyber Security Centre, government ministries and agencies, and the retail trade association moved with the supermarkets, taking thousands of devices off the open internet — some remotely, some by physically pulling cables — working day and night and over weekends, and the manufacturer issued an update that closes the flaw.

Advertisement

The operation came after a real attack in mid-October on hundreds of French stores, where hackers raised the temperatures of internet-connected refrigeration systems and ruined stocks of frozen food, before the attack spread to Belgium, the Netherlands, Denmark, Sweden and Finland.

Although the flaw has been closed in most major chains, experts warn that thousands of devices at small stores and smaller cold-chain operators may remain exposed, and no British store has reported spoiled food because of this flaw.

Simply put: your fridge in the store is connected to the internet... and a flaw in it was enough to spoil a whole city's food; did you ever think a fridge needs cyber protection?
