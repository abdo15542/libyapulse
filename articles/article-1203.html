🚀 Software glitch lengthens BepiColombo's journey around Mercury.. but the mission goes on

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

On 21 November, the BepiColombo spacecraft fired its four engines to enter orbit around the planet Mercury, in a manoeuvre that was supposed to last 37 minutes — but a software glitch stopped the burn before it finished.

The burn began at 18:24 GMT, while the spacecraft was about 635 kilometres above the planet's surface, then suddenly stopped because of a software problem related to steering the spacecraft and controlling the engines.

The spacecraft then entered "safe mode", a protective state in which all non-essential activities stop until the control team understands what happened.

Although the burn stopped early, the manoeuvre achieved about 77% of the required change in speed, which was enough to capture the spacecraft into Mercury orbit — but it will now circle the planet once every 75 hours instead of 58 hours as planned.

The European Space Agency confirmed that the mission is still on the right track, and that the remaining orbit-entry manoeuvres have been adapted to the new trajectory without needing any extra burns.

The separation of the two science orbiters — the European agency's orbiter and the Japanese space agency's "Mio" — is still scheduled for 9 December, with the European orbiter set to reach its final orbit by March 2027 and science operations to begin in April 2027.

Simply put: a small software error cut the braking burn short, so the spacecraft's orbit is longer than planned — but it is still in a safe orbit around Mercury, and the team adjusted the rest of the plan without losing the mission.
