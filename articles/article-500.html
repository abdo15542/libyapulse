🏛️ A Cyber Incident Paralyzes a US State Legislature's Website — Investigation Ongoing, No One Has Claimed Responsibility!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The legislature of the US state of West Virginia announced it was hit by a cyber incident affecting its technical systems, which began or was discovered on Thursday afternoon, November 19, while its official website was down on Friday during the investigation.

Ann Ali, deputy chief of staff of the House of Delegates and its communications director, said in a statement issued on Friday, November 20, that the specialist teams moved immediately to contain the incident and began an investigation with the help of cybersecurity experts from outside the legislature.

She added that access to some of the legislature's technical systems was temporarily disabled as part of the investigation, and that the incident appears so far limited to the legislature's own systems, with no evidence that any other government body in the state was affected.

Advertisement

Authorities have not yet revealed who is responsible, nor the nature of the attack, nor whether any data was stolen, and have not confirmed that the incident is a ransomware attack, and no hacking group has claimed responsibility for it as of Friday.

The investigation is still ongoing with no announced date for the website's return to service, while the spokesperson promised to share any important findings as soon as they are available.

The legislature's website is the gateway state residents usually rely on to review bills, learn about their representatives, and read state laws, so its downtime affected everyone who needs these services among citizens and local officials.

Simply put: even a whole state legislature's website can stop for days because of a single cyber incident; government systems are an attractive target, and the investigation takes time before knowing what actually happened.
