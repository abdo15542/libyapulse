🧠 Stanford Study: Two Weeks Without "ChatGPT" Reduce Feelings of Loneliness!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A new study from Stanford, Oxford, and Carnegie Mellon universities found that stopping AI use for just two weeks reduced feelings of loneliness among heavy users — and the effect lasted even after they went back to it.

The experiment included 625 active ChatGPT users in the United States, who use it at least 15 minutes a day. They were split at random: 306 of them stopped using AI for a full 14 days, while 319 others stopped for just one day as a comparison group, and more than 30 AI tools were blocked for them through a browser extension, in exchange for $40 a week per participant.

After the two weeks, feelings of loneliness fell more in the stop group than in the comparison group, and the drop was still there two weeks after they returned to using AI. They also met more people face to face, spent more time in social interaction, and started asking people for help instead of delegating tasks to AI.

The surprise is that 84.7% of participants use AI mainly for productivity and work, not for companionship and conversation — meaning the effect is not about an "AI friend", but about everyday ordinary use.

But the study is a preliminary paper that has not yet been peer-reviewed, the size of the effect is small, and loneliness was measured from the participants' own reports, not by an objective measure.

Simply put: two weeks of rest from AI pushed people to meet each other more, so they felt less lonely — even after they went back to it.
