⚖️ OpenAI Tried to Bring In an AI Law Firm's Team — and Got Turned Down!

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The website 'Artificial Lawyer' revealed on 24 November that OpenAI held talks with an American law firm working with a new AI-based model, aiming to bring in several members of its core team — a move that reveals the company's expansion into the world of law.

The targeted firm works mostly with startups and is one of about 15 firms of this kind in the market, but it turned down OpenAI's offer and chose to bet on its own independent growth, while the website declined to name the firm, and OpenAI had not responded to a request for comment by publication time.

The report suggests the initiative did not come from Jason Baumig, who leads OpenAI's 'Astra for Law' offering, but from other executives — pointing to a broader push into the startup ecosystem, where small companies need legal work such as incorporation documents.

In practical terms, OpenAI could have brought in just the team without acquiring the regulated firm itself, in what is known as an 'acqui-hire' deal, gaining the expertise, intellectual property and contract data — law being a trillion-dollar sector with huge unmet needs because of the high cost of legal services.

OpenAI is not alone in this race: Anthropic, Google and Microsoft have launched their own legal channels, while Palantir works with the firm Kirkland, and Perplexity has developed its own legal capabilities.

Simply put: the AI giants want the lawyers themselves, not just their clients — whoever owns legal expertise holds the key to a trillion-dollar sector.
