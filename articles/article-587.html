⚖️ A writer sues xAI after Grok turned her photo into fake images without her consent!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Writer and activist Amani Al-Khatahtbeh, founder of the "Muslim Girl" website, filed a federal lawsuit on Wednesday, November 18, in a court in Northern California against the companies X and xAI.

The lawsuit accuses the AI tool Grok, built into the X platform, of generating sexual fake images of her without her consent, after digitally removing her hijab and turning her photo into different poses.

The story began on January 1, when Al-Khatahtbeh published a photo of herself wearing her hijab from the inauguration of New York City's first Muslim mayor, and some users used Grok to edit the photo and publish dozens of altered copies of it.

Al-Khatahtbeh discovered the images on January 2 and reported them to the platform, but the lawsuit says some images were hidden from her while remaining visible to other users.

The lawsuit accuses the companies of knowingly distributing sexual images without consent, of a design defect in the tool, of negligence, and of intentionally causing emotional distress, and demands compensation that could reach $250,000 for each violation under California civil law.

The lawsuit relies on an analysis that found Grok generated about 3 million sexual images in just 11 days between December 29, 2025 and January 8, 2026, including about 23,000 images of children.

The lawsuit notes that after a change in the tool's policy in March 2026, image generation became limited to subscribers of paid plans, but that did not remove the images created before, nor stop paid subscribers.

Al-Khatahtbeh is not only seeking financial compensation — she wants the court to force the company to install safeguards preventing Grok from generating sexual images of real people without their consent.

A day before filing the lawsuit, Al-Khatahtbeh took part on November 17 in a Congress hearing supporting the "Defiance" bill, which gives victims of sexual fake images the right to sue whoever creates or publishes them.

Simply put: imagine someone publishing your photo, and AI turning it within seconds into images you never took, spreading among thousands of people. This lawsuit asks: who is responsible — the user or the maker of the tool?
