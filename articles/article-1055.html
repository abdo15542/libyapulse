🚗 Dutch Court Bans Sale of the BYD T7 in Europe… Because It Looks Like a Land Rover Defender!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A district court in The Hague, in the Netherlands, issued a court order on Friday 20 November banning the sale of the BYD T7 large SUV in the European Union, because it infringes the design rights of the famous Land Rover Defender — a rare ruling between major carmakers in Europe.

Jaguar Land Rover, owned by India's Tata Motors group, had filed the case earlier this year, after BYD announced on its UK website that its seven-seat T7 was available to order, with customer deliveries due to start in January 2027 — while the car had launched in China last year under the name Fang Cheng Bao T7, with global sales passing 300,000 cars by October 2026.

The court said that 'the distinctive and prominent design elements are reflected in the T7', and among the similarities noted by specialist media: the boxy shape and flat roof, the vertical proportions, the rear-door design, the squared wheel arches, the body-coloured solid panel behind the rear door, and the wraparound glazing around the cabin.

The ban takes effect a week after the ruling, because BYD currently has no concrete plans to launch the car in the EU market. The ruling also barred the company from producing the car at its new factory in Hungary, which was due to start mass production early next year — and it did not cover the UK, because the court said it had no jurisdiction over the company's British branch.

The ruling is temporary, issued in summary proceedings, and Jaguar Land Rover must file a full lawsuit within 6 months or the ban may lapse. Fines for breaching the order reach €100,000 a day, up to a maximum of €10 million, while BYD said it 'strongly disagrees' with the ruling and will appeal, insisting its car is 'the product of independent design work' and infringes no right of the British company.

The ruling comes as BYD expands rapidly in Europe, with its sales more than doubling to over 280,000 cars by September 2026, overtaking Ford, Tesla and Nissan — and it is rare for major carmakers to go to court over design rights, despite old complaints from traditional companies that Chinese firms copy their designs, with little of it ever proven in court.

Simply put: two cars so alike that the judge could not tell them apart from a distance... Is similarity in shape alone enough to ban a car from an entire market?
