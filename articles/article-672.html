🛰️ Satellites now outnumber the stars above your city — 710 satellites in one town's sky!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A new analysis has found that satellites now outnumber the stars you can see with your own eyes above most cities and towns: above an average British town, about 710 satellites cross the sky at any moment of the night, while in a light-polluted city your eye sees only a few dozen stars.

The figures come from the "Night Sky" report prepared by a British engineering company using tracking data from the US Space Force, after tracking 15,833 active satellites in low Earth orbit below 2,000 kilometres, over several nights in October. Over a full night, about 12,891 different satellites pass above a single town, and the numbers are close across most of Europe and the United States and the regions between latitudes 40 and 50 degrees, while the sky calms near the equator with about 490 satellites.

But the important detail is that most of these satellites are not visible to the eye, because satellites do not shine by themselves — sunlight must reach them while the Earth below them is in darkness. Of the 710 satellites, only about 105 are lit and shining like stars at any moment, rising to about 300 in the first hour after sunset, then falling to fewer than 20 around midnight, before returning to 300 before dawn.

The analysis itself has not been independently peer-reviewed, and it covers October only, so the numbers change with the length of the night through the year. But a peer-reviewed astronomy study warned that current plans for mega-constellations could raise the number to thousands of visible satellites every night, threatening astronomers' observations and the night sky itself.

Simply put: your sky is now more crowded with satellites than stars, even if you cannot see most of them. Can you still see the stars from your city?
