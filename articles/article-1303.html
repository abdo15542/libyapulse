🎓 A fifth of Japanese companies are cutting graduate hiring — AI is taking office jobs!

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A new survey in Japan found that about a fifth of companies that hire new graduates for office and administrative jobs plan to reduce the number of those graduates, as AI use spreads, in the first clear sign that AI has started to change the job market at its entry point.

Human resources services company Mynavi conducted the survey between September and October, with 1,667 companies from various sectors taking part, including 114 listed companies. Among companies hiring graduates for office and administrative jobs for the fiscal year starting next April, 19.6% said they will cut the number, while 79.2% expect the number to stay the same and only 1.2% plan to increase it.

The reason cited by companies expecting a decline is the advance of AI and its suitability for entry-level tasks, such as writing standard documents and entering data — the tasks new graduates usually started their careers with.

The impact was not the same in every job: in planning and marketing jobs, 11.7% of companies expect to cut hiring, while in information technology jobs 10.3% of companies plan to increase hiring, and in manual work 8.5% said they will increase hiring compared with only 1.5% who will cut it — meaning AI is squeezing routine office work, while demand grows for those who build and run the systems.

Experts warned that AI taking over routine tasks, such as writing meeting minutes, reduces young people's chances of gaining experience through traditional routes, and that learning information from its source directly, and knowing what AI cannot replace, have become more important than ever for young workers.

Simply put: the first job after graduation was always the gateway into the job market; if AI takes over its routine tasks, new graduates must think more carefully about which career to choose, and companies must redesign the start of the career path.
