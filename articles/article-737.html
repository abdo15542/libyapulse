📉 Goldman Sachs Warns: The AI Stock Selloff Has Become a Broader Decline, and Its Basket Is Down 32%!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Goldman Sachs warned that the selling wave that hit AI stocks has turned into a broader decline across the whole market, after the S&P 500 index closed in correction territory, down 3.59% to 6,918.45 points, in a seventh consecutive loss, its longest losing streak since February 2020, while the Nasdaq fell 5.28% and is now down more than 2% since the start of the year.

The basket of AI stocks the bank tracks has fallen 32% since October 6, and the selling spread beyond AI stocks to growth stocks in general, so much so that the S&P 500 without AI stocks is now 6% below its highest level, and cyclical stocks lagged defensive stocks by five percentage points over the past ten days.

The bank identified three signals that could indicate the market has reached its bottom: news that boosts confidence in AI earnings, a signal from the Federal Reserve toward easing tightening, and a large drop in investor positions. But the signals are still distant: the chances of a rate hike in December are about 95%, and the bank's economists expect two more hikes in December and March, raising interest rates to between 4.25% and 4.5%, while the yield on ten-year Treasury bonds is approaching 5%.

Adding to the concern, the price war between Anthropic and OpenAI is squeezing both companies, as their quarterly revenue growth fell from 75% in the second quarter to 48% in the third quarter, while their combined losses rose from $7.9 billion to about $8.4 billion. The bank also expects growth in global corporate earnings to fall by almost half next year as investment in AI slows.

Simply put: when AI stocks fall 32%, the loss does not stay inside one sector, it drags the whole market down with it; so do you see what is happening as a healthy correction after a rapid rise, or the start of a longer decline for tech stocks?
