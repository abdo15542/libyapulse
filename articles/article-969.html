🚀 Japan hands its 'H3' rocket to 'Mitsubishi'! A step toward privatizing space launches!

23 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Last week, the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency 'JAXA' announced an agreement with 'Mitsubishi Heavy Industries' under which the company will take over the operation of launches of its main 'H3' rocket in some configurations, a step that moves space launches from the hands of the state to the hands of the private sector.

Under the agreement, 'Mitsubishi' will handle launches of the rocket using two liquid-fuel 'LE-9' engines with two solid 'SRB-3' boosters, while the agency continues to operate launches that use different or experimental configurations, until it is sure of the new systems' reliability.

The agency plans to hand over more launches to private-sector operators over time, once it is convinced of the systems' reliability, in a model similar to what other space agencies did when they handed routine launches to companies and devoted themselves to research and development.

The 'H3' rocket is Japan's main rocket in space, and handing its operation to a major industrial company means Japanese space launches are entering a new phase, becoming a regular commercial operation rather than an exceptional government mission each time.

Simply put: Japan is handing the keys to its most important rocket to a private company, to devote itself to research... will privatization succeed in making space cheaper and faster?
