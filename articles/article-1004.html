🛰️ Satellite images reveal China's biggest AI center... and electricity is not its problem!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A research institute analyzed images taken by European and American satellites of the largest AI data center belonging to the company Huawei in the city of Horingor in Inner Mongolia, estimating its capacity at about 286 megawatts spread across four densely cooled buildings, three of which are already operating.

By the institute's calculation, the site holds about 105,000 Ascend 910C chips and 8,000 Ascend 910B chips, equivalent to about 81,000 Nvidia H100 cards — but this capacity is only about 7% of the capacity of Colossus 2 in the US state of Tennessee, the world's largest data center, which exceeds it by about 14 times, with a new building planned by 2028 adding more than 70%.

The analysis did not rely on official figures, but on counting the cooling units and measuring their ability to dissipate heat, then estimating the electrical power from that, and the result matched two other checks: a power substation with a capacity of 304 megawatts and backup generators with a capacity of 230 megawatts.

The images show that China's problem is not electricity but chips: while the West suffers from a shortage of electricity for its data centers, China suffers from a shortage of advanced chips, since the latest Nvidia chips are about two and a half times more efficient than their rivals — meaning a Chinese site with the same electrical power as an American site delivers only 40% of its computing capacity, and the Ascend 910C chip reaches about 76% of the H100's performance according to the company's own reports.

The institute believes the gap is narrowing but will not close within this decade, while a sister site in the city of Wuhu targets about 151,000 equivalent cards by December 2027, and the Horingor site belongs to a government program that moves computing work to regions in western China.

Simply put: China has the electricity but lacks the chips... are satellite images enough to measure the AI race?
