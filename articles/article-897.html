🏦 'Project Lantern': How Anthropic Ran Its IPO in Secrecy That Angered Wall Street Bankers!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A report published on November 22 revealed the behind-the-scenes story of Anthropic's record IPO, which the company internally called 'Project Lantern', and how it imposed unprecedented conditions on Wall Street bankers that angered them despite the huge profits.

The company asked some of the 22 underwriting banks to limit the working team to just four senior bankers, fearing information leaks, and it also asked senior bank officials to sign documents barring them from working with rival companies — including OpenAI, Cursor, DeepSeek, and Hugging Face — for six months after the offering.

The conditions did not stop there: the company shared its documents through a private platform that records who read each page and watermarks every banker's documents with their name, without allowing copies to be saved, and it sent the final version of the IPO prospectus at 2 a.m. New York time, giving the banks only a few hours to approve it.

Despite all that, the bankers earned more than half a billion dollars in fees, as demand for the offered shares exceeded supply several times over, and the stock jumped 17% on its first day before falling 8.5% the next day amid a sell-off in AI stocks. The company also negotiated to pay no commission to the banks on the extra shares sold through the overallotment option.

Experts believe that the major AI companies now hold a negotiating power the market has never seen before: they dictate their terms to the world's biggest banks, and the banks have no choice but to agree.

Simply put: a record IPO with record conditions: only four bankers, a six-month ban on working with rivals, and documents that cannot be saved. Do you think AI companies have become stronger than Wall Street itself?

Would you buy shares in a company that imposes all this secrecy on its own bankers?
