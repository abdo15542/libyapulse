🎮 One Game Paralyzes a Quarter of the Internet — GTA 6 Shatters Network Records!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

GTA 6 did not stop at breaking sales records — it broke the internet's records too: Comcast, America's largest terrestrial internet provider, announced that November 18 was the busiest day in its network's entire history, with peak traffic 31% above an average day and 9.6% above the previous record.

The story began days before launch: since preloading opened on November 10, the event became the largest single event in the network's history, and the first 48 hours recorded two of the network's busiest days ever. At peak demand, the game alone represented up to 24% of all Comcast network traffic — a quarter of the internet was a single game. Five of the network's ten busiest days ever came from the game's launch period.

Demand continued on November 19, the official launch day on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S, making it the network's second-busiest day, coinciding with a weekly American football game. The data transferred across both platforms between November 10 and 18 was enough for a million homes to watch a film in 4K quality continuously for about two days. Twitch traffic rose about 50% on November 18.

It was not only Comcast: gaming traffic on the Spectrum network jumped 49% during the preload period, in the largest download event in its history, and could exceed one exabyte by the end of the month, while the UK's Virgin Media O2 usage jumped 51.6% in the first hour of launch. The game itself is huge: about 212 GB, forcing millions of players to free up space on their devices before downloading.

Simply put: imagine a highway carrying a quarter of a city's cars all at once because a single event draws everyone — that is what one game did to the American internet network in a single day.
