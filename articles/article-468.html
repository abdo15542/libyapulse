📷 The iPhone Camera Software Chief Leaves After 8 Years — and Without a Successor!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

John McCormack, Apple's vice president of camera and photos software engineering, retired at the end of last October after more than eight years at the company, according to a Bloomberg report — a rare leadership change affecting the iPhone's most important feature.

McCormack oversaw the Camera and Photos apps and the core technologies that determine how the phone captures, processes, and edits a photo — everything that happens between pressing the shutter button and the final image you see on your screen. He joined Apple in 2018 and became a central figure in developing computational photography at the company.

Apple has not yet appointed a new vice president to replace him; instead, it distributed his duties among his former deputies, who work under other executives, while software engineering chief Craig Federighi oversees the transition. The company has not officially announced the departure, and a spokesperson declined to comment, while McCormack himself shared the retirement news on his Instagram account — an indirect confirmation of its accuracy.

Notably, McCormack — a photographer and environmentalist — was known for his strong opposition to using generative AI to create or edit images, at a time when Apple is moving toward AI tools inside the Photos app. But the report stressed that his retirement has nothing to do with any disagreement over the company's AI strategy.

Simply put: the man who built the iPhone's photography experience for eight years leaves quietly, and Apple leaves his post vacant at a moment when cameras are being changed by AI.
