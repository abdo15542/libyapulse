🏭 Why Have Data Centers Ended Up at the Heart of the Anger at Big Tech Companies?

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

In a new interview, journalist Paris Marx, author of the book "Hyperscale" about data centers, explains how these huge buildings have become the most controversial issue in tech, even becoming one of the drivers of a broad political wave in the recent US elections.

Marx says data centers are a concrete issue, not an abstract one: when a data center is built in your area, you feel its impact in your electricity bill, in your access to water, and in the noise of the air-conditioning units on the roofs, whatever your political affiliation.

And the numbers explain the anger: in Ireland, data centers consume more than 20% of the country's electricity; in the US city of The Dalles, Google revealed it uses 29% of the city's water; and in Chile, residents protested over the pressure data centers put on the capital's water.

Advertisement

The big companies responded to the anger with new promises: Microsoft, Amazon, and OpenAI pledged not to use secrecy agreements in their negotiations with local governments, and promised to pay for more of the electricity infrastructure and to switch to closed cooling systems that use less water.

But Marx believes the anger goes beyond data centers themselves — it also includes the surveillance cameras spread across the streets, smart glasses, and self-driving cars: everything that makes technology tangible in people's daily lives.

Simply put: data centers have become a symbol of the price of AI that people pay from their electricity and water... is it worth it?
