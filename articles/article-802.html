🏦 Britain Consults Banks on Facing Cyberattacks, as Incidents Hit Their Highest Level in 5 Years!

22 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Three British regulators have opened a joint consultation on how financial services firms manage cyber risks and recover from incidents, at a time when the country is seeing a wave of incidents at its highest level in years.

The consultation was announced on November 19 by the Bank of England, the Prudential Regulation Authority, and the Financial Conduct Authority, under the documents CP17/26 and GC26/9. They are proposed guidelines, not new binding rules, covering governance, cataloguing critical assets, protection, monitoring, response, and recovery. The comment period ends on February 26, 2027, with the final guidelines to be issued next year.

The Financial Conduct Authority revealed that October 2026 saw the second-highest monthly number of reported cyber incidents in five years, and the highest in the last 18 months, up 52% on the previous record in May 2025.

The regulators also published separate papers on cyber risks linked to AI and quantum computing, which are not part of the formal consultation. A survey showed that 88% of firms expect advanced AI capabilities to have a medium or large impact on their risks within two years, and that 64% said managing vulnerabilities and patches saw the largest increase in management attention.

Simply put: the regulators are not imposing new rules, but asking the sector what good defense looks like; so are guidelines enough when attacks are multiplying, or is it time for binding rules?
