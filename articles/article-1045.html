💻 AMD Completes $2.9 Billion Purchase of Unconventional... a Chip That Turns the AI Model Itself into Silicon!

23 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

AMD announced it has completed the purchase of Canadian AI inference chip company Unconventional, in a deal worth about $2.9 billion in cash and shares, including up to $1.4 billion conditional on undisclosed performance targets.

The two companies had agreed the deal on August 6 without announcing the price, then the value appeared in the company's quarterly report on November 4, before the deal's closing was announced on November 17.

What makes Unconventional different is that it does not make a general-purpose chip that runs any model; instead it etches a single specific model into the silicon itself: the model's weights, its structure and the way data flows through it become part of the circuit, so there is no longer a memory bottleneck slowing down the answer.

The company's first experimental chip, the HC1, carries the Llama 3.1-8B model on a 6-nanometre chip with an area of 815 square millimetres, and the company says it generates about 17,000 tokens per second for a single user — about 48 times what it measured on the rival B200 chip — and claims it can turn any new model into a custom chip within just two months.

Unconventional was founded in Toronto in 2023 by Ljubisa Bajic, a co-founder of Tenstorrent and a former AMD employee, and raised about $220 million in funding before the sale, while employing only about 24 people.

AMD said it will integrate Unconventional's technology into its accelerator roadmap, alongside Instinct cards, to offer complete system solutions, and that the deal expands its engineering presence in Canada.

This is the second deal the company has closed in a single week, after buying World Labs for $8.2 billion the previous day, in a frantic race to control the inference market — the stage where a trained model answers users' questions.

Simply put: instead of one chip doing everything slowly, the idea is now for every AI model to become a chip designed just for it... Will custom chips become the fastest route to instant answers?

If any model can be turned into a chip within two months, will the dominance of general-purpose cards over the AI market come to an end?
