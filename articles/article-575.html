🔧 Apple goes on trial in Paris over iPhone repairs — and the fine could reach 5% of its revenue!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Apple will stand before the criminal court in Paris in June 2027, accused of obstructing repairs of iPhones outside its authorised channels, in a case that could become the first of its kind in the world.

The case goes back to a complaint filed in December 2022 by a French association against planned obsolescence, after a four-year investigation. The case covers 24 iPhone models, from the XS to the 15 series and the 2022 SE, over practices between November 2021 and July 2024.

The charges centre on what is known as parts pairing: every part in the phone carries a serial number linked to the device itself, and when the screen, battery or camera is replaced outside Apple's authorised network, the phone may malfunction or lose features such as True Tone, Face ID and the battery-health indicator — even if the replacement part is completely genuine.

The specific charge is obstructing repair and refurbishment under French consumer-protection law — the first time this provision has been used since it was issued in 2021. If convicted, the company could face a fine of up to 5% of its revenue, plus compensation for affected consumers. A hearing to set the timetable is scheduled for February 25, 2027, followed by four days of arguments starting June 17.

Apple denies the charges entirely, saying parts pairing protects users' privacy, security and safety. It also points out that it has expanded repair options since the complaint, allowing the use of genuine used parts from April 2024, and restoring True Tone support with replacement screens. The association acknowledges these changes, but says their long-term effectiveness still needs proving.

Simply put: imagine buying a car, then the manufacturer stops you from repairing it anywhere but its own workshops — even with a genuine part from another car. That is the case in short, and the French court will decide whether this is user protection or a repair monopoly.
