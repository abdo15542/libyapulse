🧠 A global memory crisis threatens iPhone and Mac prices — even Apple's long-term agreements may not be enough!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The world faces a shortage of memory chips — a shortage pressing on Apple's supply chain, which may be reflected in iPhone and Mac prices.

Even the long-term agreements linking Apple with major suppliers may not be enough to contain the costs, if the shortage continues and prices rise further.

"Memory" here means the chips that store your data and run your apps, which go into almost every phone and computer. If their supply drops, their price rises; if their price rises, the device's price rises.

Advertisement

The "supply chain" is the network of factories and suppliers through which every part passes before reaching your device, and any fault in one link reaches the whole market.

With global demand for memory rising, costs on manufacturers rise, and the increase may eventually pass on to the price of the device you buy.

Simply put: imagine an essential material in every device becoming scarce, so its price rises. Either companies pay the difference from their own pocket, or they raise the price of your device.

Do you buy your phone now before any possible increase, or do you wait?
