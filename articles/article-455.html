🔓 Libreboot Backs Down from the AI Ban After Just One Month — 'The Promise Was Unenforceable'!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The Libreboot open-source firmware project backed down from its ban on AI-written contributions, just one month after imposing it. The project's maintainer, Leah Rowe, deleted the contribution rules file that contained the ban, on November 20, announcing she will no longer enforce it.

The project had added a strict anti-AI policy last October, but Rowe said in the edit message that the policy is unenforceable, because the projects Libreboot depends on, foremost Coreboot, do not reject AI-written contributions at all. She added that she does not want to promise what she cannot deliver, and that she prefers deleting an unreliable promise to being a hypocrite.

Instead of a full ban, the project published a new policy called the 'Slop Reduction Policy', based on resisting poor AI-generated contributions wherever practically possible: reverting weak fixes or rewriting them, and removing some features instead of shipping an AI-generated fix. The policy gave a real example: removing support for the 'ThinkPad ACPI' component because a fix in it was AI-generated, even though that weakened some battery functions.

Rowe confirmed she still personally opposes language model technology, but the project will not promise to remove all AI-generated code from dependencies it does not own. New contributions are allowed again, with closer human review of sensitive paths such as power management and security drivers.

Simply put: a small project discovered that banning AI is impossible when it depends on bigger projects that do not ban it, so it replaced the big promise with honest, realistic review.
