💻 A 10-out-of-10 Flaw in ASUS's PC Management Tool — a Fixed Key Gives the Attacker Admin Powers!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

ASUS released an urgent update for its Control Center Express tool, which IT administrators use to monitor and manage fleets of company computers from a single control panel, after three critical vulnerabilities were discovered in it.

The most dangerous vulnerability carries the number CVE-2026-114202, and received the maximum score of 10 out of 10 on the severity scale, because its cause is a fixed encryption key written inside the program itself, so any attacker who reaches the control panel over the network can forge a login token and gain full admin powers, without an account and without any user interaction.

As for the second vulnerability, it carries the number CVE-2026-114204 and a score of 9.0, and lets an attacker with low privileges forge a session token for an account that recently logged in, while the third, CVE-2026-114205 with a score of 9.4, lets a user with low privileges create a new admin account.

The company advises installing version 1.7.29 immediately, as all versions older than it are at risk, then updating the agent software on every computer the control panel manages, and checking their versions one by one.

No actual attacks exploiting these vulnerabilities have been recorded yet, but their maximum severity means the window is open to any attacker who discovers a control panel exposed on the internet.

The company also released on the same day updates for its larger platform Control Center Enterprise and for the Armoury Crate tool, including a critical flaw allowing a user with only a viewer role to change an admin's password and seize their account.

Simply put: the fixed key is like a master key hidden under the door that everyone knows about; whoever finds it enters without knocking, and controls every computer the control panel manages.
