🌙 Earth Is Hosting a Temporary Mini-Moon... a Car-Sized Rock That Will Leave in December!

22 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Earth is currently hosting a second, small, temporary moon: a tiny asteroid only about two meters across, captured by Earth's gravity last April, and expected to leave its domain in early December to continue its journey around the Sun. The Minor Planet Center of the International Astronomical Union named it 2026 QQ8 in late October.

Hungarian astronomer Krisztian Sarneczky discovered the object last August, and astronomers needed weeks to confirm it was a natural rock and not a defunct satellite or rocket debris, since its small size and faint brightness make it hard to distinguish. Observatories in Arizona and Hawaii helped confirm its nature, after studying the effect of sunlight on its motion.

The director of the Minor Planet Center says having a temporary companion like this around Earth is relatively common: on average, there is one at any given time, and these objects stay for weeks or months and then leave. There is no danger from 2026 QQ8: even if it entered the atmosphere, it would break apart without harm, and it cannot be seen with the naked eye, requiring a large telescope.

Simply put: Earth has a temporary mini-moon the size of a car, which will leave in December; did you know our planet has temporary moons that come and go?
