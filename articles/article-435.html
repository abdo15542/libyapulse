⚖️ 'YouTube' Nears a $3 Billion Payment to US Schools — Over Addiction!

21 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

"YouTube" has reached a preliminary agreement to pay about $3 billion to thousands of school districts in the United States, which accused the platform of designing itself to be addictive in ways that harm students' mental health. The agreement also includes adding new teen protections inside the product.

The school districts say features like recommendation algorithms, autoplay, and infinite scrolling were designed to make young users addicted, and contributed to depression, anxiety, and eating disorders. The districts range from big cities like New York and Los Angeles to smaller communities, and say they were forced to spend money on enforcing phone bans and hiring mental-health specialists.

The agreement is conditional on approval by a majority of the school districts, and could take a few months to finalize formally, and also needs the approval of a federal judge in Oakland overseeing the case. If completed, it would be the largest settlement any company has ever reached with US school districts.

The settlement comes while "Meta", "TikTok", and "Snap" remain defendants in the federal trial scheduled for February, noting that "Meta" agreed in August to pay up to $18 billion to settle similar claims from US states. "YouTube" had paid just over $2 million in May in an earlier settlement with a school district in Kentucky.

Simply put: the schools say the platform was designed to steal students' attention and health, and the platform is paying billions to end the case instead of a long court battle, with a promise of changes that protect teens.
