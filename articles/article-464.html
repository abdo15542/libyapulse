🧠 OpenAI's Models Design Chips Themselves — a 14% Improvement in AMD's Flagship Processor!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

OpenAI revealed that its models have become able to design and improve chips at a level approaching human design experts, after achieving tangible improvements in its own chips and in the giant AMD's chips.

In its first inference chip, named Jalapeño, the model-proposed modifications reduced standard cell area by about 13% in the vector unit and 10% in the matrix unit — improvements actually tested in silicon. One redesigned multiplier saved about 15% of area and cut execution time from two cycles to one cycle.

The bigger surprise came with AMD: the models rewrote the code describing the circuits of the company's flagship graphics processor, achieving an improvement of up to 14% in combined power, performance, and area metrics, compared with designs human engineers had refined for years. The company described this as the equivalent of a full year of progress achieved in less than five months, and AMD has actually incorporated some of these modifications into a design sent for manufacturing.

Nor was it limited to graphics processors: the company also verified single-digit improvements in the main parts of its upcoming flagship central processor, using designs written by the models.

Alongside this, the company measured the evolution of its models' capabilities across successive generations on two open RISC-V processor cores, with its latest model, GPT-6.2 Astra, outperforming all previous models, and it is working with Synopsys on training a new model called GPT-Synopsys that uses professional chip-design tools as expert engineers do.

Simply put: AI no longer just writes programs — it now designs the hardware it runs on, sometimes surpassing years of human expertise.
