🎬 'GPT-6.2 Astra' writes code instead of video! A new face-off with 'Seedance 2.5'!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

In an independent test conducted by 'Artificial Analysis' on November 20, the 'GPT-6.2 Astra' model from 'OpenAI' was put face to face with the 'Seedance 2.5' model from 'ByteDance', in an unconventional comparison of AI video creation.

The test relied on exactly identical camera paths for both models, and three scenes designed to test different skills: the Sydney Opera House, a disassembled display of a mechanical watch, and a dragon attacking a medieval city.

'Astra' did not output video in the usual sense, but wrote code in the 'Three.js' language to build three-dimensional scenes, which can be viewed and explored interactively inside the browser at 1080p resolution and 24 frames per second.

Advertisement

'Seedance 2.5' took the traditional route: it generated original video directly from text prompts, guided by reference images showing the required camera paths. It specializes in cinematic production and can generate a clip of up to 30 seconds in a single attempt, and accepts up to 50 reference materials in a single request.

'Seedance 2.5' costs about $0.23 per second of video through its API, while it ranks third in 'Artificial Analysis' ranking of text-to-video models through mid-November, whereas 'Astra' was only launched on November 17, three days before the test.

The comparison concluded that the two models solve two different problems: 'Astra' works like an architect, because its output is code that defines the positions of objects precisely and can be modified after creation, while 'Seedance' works like a cinematographer, because directly generated video excels in texture, lighting and atmosphere, but is a finished clip that cannot be taken apart.

Simply put: one model writes video as code, and the other paints it pixel by pixel... which one helps content creators more?

Would you prefer a 3D scene you can modify, or a finished cinematic clip?
