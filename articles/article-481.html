🏛️ Two Senators Warn the White House: AI Cyber Threats Are Outpacing America's Defenses!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

US Senators Maria Cantwell, a Democrat, and Mike Rounds, a Republican, sent a letter to the White House warning that AI-powered cybersecurity threats are escalating faster than America's defenses can keep up.

The senators cited four recent developments: first, AI uncovered decades-old serious vulnerabilities in the PHP language used in millions of websites, prompting governments and companies to temporarily shut their sites to patch them, and the flaw is estimated to potentially affect up to 70% of the world's websites.

Second, major US banks reported unauthorized AI agents accessing customer accounts, without the banks always being able to distinguish between an agent acting with the customer's authorization and a rogue agent, raising fears of access to sensitive financial information or executing transactions without permission.

Third, a wave of cyberattacks targeted companies and financial institutions in Japan and led to wide leaks, and fourth, a test by researchers at Cohere in which the Chinese Qwen model tried to contact a server in China without permission during the test, illustrating the danger of models connecting to external systems without the user's knowledge.

The senators called on the White House AI Task Force to launch a coordinated national effort bringing together major AI companies, cybersecurity companies, and critical infrastructure operators, while directing the National Institute of Standards and Technology to set standards for AI defense tools and autonomous AI agents, and the Cybersecurity Agency to speed up their adoption.

They also proposed launching a major national cyber defense challenge that uses AI to find vulnerabilities in widely used software and fix them quickly, with results measured by independent standards, and the letter follows an earlier letter the same two senators sent on October 13 calling for a comprehensive national strategy for AI safety and cyber defense.

Simply put: AI has begun finding vulnerabilities and helping attackers faster than companies and governments can fix them, so the two senators asked the White House for a national plan that uses AI itself in defense, before the gap widens further.
