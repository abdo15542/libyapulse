✍️ Musk Signs a Petition with 200,000 People to Bring Huang Back to 'Nvidia' — and the Board Refuses!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Elon Musk likened the dismissal of Jensen Huang from "Nvidia"'s leadership to "kicking Michael Jordan off your team", declaring his full support for the return of the company's founder, at the "DealBook" summit on Wednesday. Musk said: "Jensen is an old friend of mine. I consider him Michael Jordan at his peak, in terms of what he means to the team" — adding that he wrote on the "X" platform that he would hire Huang, describing him as "one of the most talented entrepreneurial CEOs in history".

Musk signed a petition, along with more than 200,000 people, calling on "Nvidia" to bring back its former CEO, while the board confirmed it does not plan to bring him back. Musk said of the board's decision: "It doesn't seem like a smart decision to me. There is really no one who can replace Jensen" — adding that Huang "built long-term relationships over decades, understands the technology extremely well, his team loves him, and many of them are there because of him, having turned down higher offers to go elsewhere out of personal loyalty to him".

Palmer Luckey, CEO of "Anduril", also joined the call, saying "Nvidia" is making a "wrong decision" by not bringing Huang back, adding: "China wants Jensen out, so I want him to stay. They are happy he's out. There's a reason for losing a trillion dollars of Nvidia's market value. They know that removing him makes America weaker." Musk showed no concern about the implications of Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese spy for US national security, saying: "I don't think he's disloyal to the United States. It's like kicking Michael Jordan off your team while you're on a winning streak, and that doesn't seem like a good thing for shareholders."

In contrast, other industry leaders, like "Microsoft" CEO Satya Nadella and "OpenAI" CEO Sam Altman, affirmed their confidence in "Nvidia"'s ability to move forward without its founder, who led it for 33 years. Nadella said at the summit: "It's Nvidia's board decision, but I'll say one thing: Colette Kress, the interim CEO, worked at Microsoft for a long time. I've known her for a long time. She's a wonderful person, and I have great confidence in the Nvidia team and its ability to keep doing good work."

Simply put: a petition is an open letter thousands of people sign to demand a company's board make a decision — here the signatories demand Huang's return "while an independent investigation establishes the facts", but the petition doesn't bind the board to anything.

Do you agree with Musk that Huang is irreplaceable, or is the board right in its decision?
