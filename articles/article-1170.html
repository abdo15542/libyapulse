🚨 APT29 Hackers Break Into Android Phones Through Two Flaws in Chrome and Qualcomm!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A report from security firm Sequoia TDR revealed a spy campaign targeting Android and iPhone users through compromised websites, attributed to the state-backed Russian hacking group APT29.

The attackers exploited a flaw in the Chrome browser targeting versions 139 to 145, and another flaw in Qualcomm's Adreno graphics-processor driver, to break into Android devices. iPhone users were targeted through the same compromised websites.

The campaign relied on the "watering hole" technique: ordinary websites the victims visit daily were compromised to distribute the malware, including a Ukrainian web-analytics service called "Blardy SEO Tracker" that was used in a supply-chain attack.

The practical message is clear: update Chrome and your phone's system updates as soon as they arrive, and don't trust a website just because you've visited it hundreds of times before — even trusted sites can turn into traps.

Simply put: the hackers didn't send you a suspicious link… they planted the trap in websites you visit every day, and waited for you to walk over it.

Do you update your phone's browser and system as soon as updates are released?
