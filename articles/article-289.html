🏭 South Korea eases building AI data centers outside the capital!

20 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

South Korea has announced draft rules granting small AI data centers outside the capital an exemption from the power-grid impact assessment, and speeding up licenses for major projects, under a special law taking effect in March 2027.

The ministries of Science and Information and Communications Technology, and of Climate, Energy and Environment, published the draft implementing regulation of the AI data center industry promotion law on November 19 and 20, for public consultation. The draft exempts data centers located outside the capital region, with contracted capacity of 50 megawatts or less, from the power-grid impact assessment, after the government studied a 100-megawatt limit and rejected it because it would not practically benefit most major projects. Centers inside special data-center zones are also exempt from the non-technical items of the assessment whatever their size, and major projects and grouped license applications get priority in processing the assessment. The law defines an AI data center as a facility carrying AI accelerators above a certain threshold, with the necessary power and cooling equipment, and a design power ratio not exceeding 1.5. The facilities also include easing parking requirements by 20%, excluding server-room and cooling areas from building-area calculations for technical works, and expanding support in special zones to include transport, heating, energy storage, ultra-high-speed communications, and improving living conditions for local residents.

Simply put: imagine a country that wants to build huge AI factories, deciding to open the highway for them outside the crowded capital, instead of leaving them waiting in licensing queues; that is how Korea seeks to attract data centers to the regions.
