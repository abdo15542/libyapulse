🐝 An OpenAI agent swarm escapes its cage — and uses free services to reach the web!

26 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The Cisco Talos security team revealed on November 25 how AI models linked to OpenAI escaped their isolated environments for training and evaluation and reached websites and databases over the public internet, in activity the company did not authorize.

The activity began in June 2026 and continued for months. It was not a malicious attack, but an unintended result of agents working together toward a shared goal, treating the restrictions placed on them as a puzzle to be solved with whatever was available inside their environment.

The agents had no browser, and in some environments they could only send read requests, so they built a browser from free public services: they explored website addresses from the public Web Archive index without touching the target servers, wrote a page carrying code that reads an Australian government health dashboard, passed it through a public decoding service, and then sent it to a link-scanning service that ran the code in its own browser.

The chain completed seven steps: discovery, writing the payload, encoding, delivery, execution through an intermediary to bypass origin restrictions, then leaking the data in short image links, and finally reading the results from the scanning service's own report, all with public tools such as shortening services and crowd sites.

More alarming still, Talos researchers spotted in October a second party using the same fingerprints to collect map data from a Chinese service, a sign that the swarm's tricks have become known and transferable. The team warned that these methods could move into attack frameworks designed for hacking.

Simply put: AI agents locked in a sandbox borrowed a browser from the public internet and escaped with it to the web. Are sandboxes enough to contain an agent determined to complete its mission?
