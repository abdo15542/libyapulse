🌕 China reveals its most detailed moon-landing plan before 2030... two rockets, three astronauts and a rover!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

China has revealed its most detailed roadmap yet for landing astronauts on the Moon before 2030, presented by the deputy chief designer of the Chinese human spaceflight program at a specialist conference in Beijing on October 31, attended by more than 800 experts.

The plan calls for three uncrewed missions before any crewed landing attempt: the first, in early 2027, sends the Mengzhou spacecraft uncrewed to the Tiangong space station aboard the single-core Long March 10A rocket; the second, around 2028, sends the Lanyue lander uncrewed to the Moon to test landing and liftoff; and the third, around 2029, a full robotic rehearsal of the journey with two rockets.

The crewed flight uses two Long March 10 rockets, one carrying the Mengzhou spacecraft with three astronauts, and the other carrying the Lanyue lander, meeting and docking in lunar orbit — then two astronauts descend to the surface while the third stays in orbit for support and monitoring, and the two spend three days on the surface with activities outside the spacecraft every day, before ascending and docking again with the crew spacecraft to return to Earth.

Unlike the American Apollo program, the first Chinese landing flight will carry a rover called Tansu, weighing about 200 kilograms, carrying two astronauts for up to 10 kilometers around the landing site to collect samples and run experiments, while the Americans first used a lunar rover only in the later missions of the Apollo program.

The plan rests on two principles: testing everything done in lunar orbit first in near-Earth orbit using the Tiangong station, and testing everything involving the crew uncrewed first — and the landing could come in 2029, coinciding with the 80th anniversary of the founding of the People's Republic of China, while the 'Rimae Bode' site near the Moon's equator remains the leading candidate among 14 sites under study.

The Tiangong station is at the same time preparing to receive its next crew on November 25, with the first Pakistani astronaut to visit the station, on a visit lasting about a week.

Simply put: two rockets meeting around the Moon and two astronauts walking on its surface... will China beat America back to the Moon?
