⚖️ Australia passes a 'Digital Duty of Care'.. fines of up to 5% of global tech companies' revenue!

26 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The Australian Parliament has passed the Digital Duty of Care law, which requires tech companies to prevent foreseeable harm to their users before it happens, instead of merely responding to it after it occurs, in a radical change to platform responsibility.

The law covers social networks, games, apps, search engines, app stores and AI chatbots, and even companies that manufacture internet-connected equipment, and each of them must identify the expected risks in its services and take effective measures to reduce them.

The maximum fine for a violation is 5% of the company's global revenue or 109.2 million Australian dollars, whichever is greater, and the protection covers children in particular, as infinite scrolling, like counters, stories and recommendations based on account data are banned for those under 16.

The law also targets chatbots that imitate emotional closeness and encourage a child to rely on them instead of human relationships, and under a 'my feed, my way' initiative, users aged 16 and over gain the right to turn off personalized algorithmic recommendations in their feeds.

The law was passed after a last-minute agreement between the Labor Party and the Greens that raised the fines, while the opposition opposed it, and the main duty will take effect 12 months after royal assent, in a step resembling the European Digital Services Act and the British Online Safety Act.

Simply put: it is no longer enough for a platform to delete harmful content after it spreads; it must now design its services from the start in a way that does no harm; will Australia succeed in forcing the tech giants to change how they design their products?
