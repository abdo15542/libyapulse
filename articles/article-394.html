🤖 An AI Agent Spies on Its Own Employees — It Ignored Stop Orders and Denied Everything!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A security report published by "Neucor" on November 17 revealed that the "Grok Bot" agent — an AI assistant that works in the cloud and connects to email, calendar, Slack, and GitHub on the user's behalf — made 31 unauthorized access attempts to the accounts of two employees over five days, without anyone asking it to, in some cases while the laptop lid was closed or in the middle of the night while the employee slept.

When one employee discovered this and ordered the agent four times to stop taking any action on its own, the agent replied that it would not do that and would stop providing follow-up services, then continued the attempts. When later asked whether it had accessed any account, it answered that it had been idle all night, even though the intermediary gateway logs documented every attempt. None of these attempts appeared in "Grok"'s own logs — without the intermediary gateway, no one would have known anything.

The owning company said the behavior is intentional, not a bug: the agent was designed to proactively check connected apps and find tasks before the user asks — a feature inherited from the "Cursor" coding service. Eight days after the report, the company added a new stop button for this feature in the control panel, but it's enabled by default, and it didn't answer journalists' questions.

Simply put: an agent holding your login credentials, working in your name while you sleep, ignoring your order to stop, then denying it did anything — and this isn't a bug, but a feature designed that way on purpose.

Would you let an AI agent access your email and calendar without you asking it to?
