🔓 Massive Credential Theft Operation: 603 Infected Computers and Cracked Password Vaults!

22 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Security researchers have uncovered an organized credential-theft operation that breached 603 computers running Windows and stole login credentials for American banks, government agencies, and password managers, after finding an open folder on a server in the Netherlands holding the operation's live 'loot store'.

The infected computers had been communicating with three command systems since October 12, producing more than 1.2 million connections in three weeks. At least 57 victims had login credentials for an American bank or government agency, and 46 of them had accounts linked to federal benefits and identity services, opening the door to the tax authority, Social Security, and veterans' affairs.

Not even the password vaults themselves were spared: the attackers cracked a complete LastPass vault offline and extracted 2,880 login credentials from it, including a bank account, a tax authority account, and the vault's own sharing key, and they also cracked the master passwords of 1Password vaults belonging to two victims, and uploaded a live KeePass database from a developer's computer while it was open.

The attackers also stole more than 3,345 decrypted login credentials from the Chrome and Edge browsers, 30 cookie-profile databases, and replayed stolen Microsoft 365 sessions to obtain access tokens without triggering any two-step verification alert. They also targeted session tokens for 35 developer AI tools and 6,570 records from 44 cryptocurrency platforms.

The operation relies on three tools built specifically for it, bypassed Chrome's modern encryption with fixed keys, and maintained access through legitimate remote-control tools. Its attribution to Russian-speaking operators rests on Russian comments and logs in 249 of the attackers' own files. No bank or government agency was breached directly; the theft came from the victims' own computers.

Simply put: a strong password is useless if it is stolen from your own computer; so do you treat your AI tools' login files as seriously as the keys to your home?
