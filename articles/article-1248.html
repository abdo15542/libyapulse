🤖 “GT13” Campaign Breaches Check Point Servers at 22 Organizations — and AI Is at the Heart of the Attack!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Threat intelligence company GreyNoise uncovered a large-scale breach campaign targeting Check Point firewall management servers at at least 22 organizations in 17 countries, in an attack that is believed to still be ongoing.

Researchers tracked the attacker under the name “GT13” and recorded its confirmed activity between 17 October and 2 November, but they lost direct visibility after that date. They believe the campaign is continuing because the attacker retained persistent access to the compromised devices.

The attacks were launched from 99 IP addresses, mostly compromised Check Point devices or exit nodes of the ExpressVPN service, and the attacker used a compromised file server as a relay station to manage the breaches and collect the stolen data.

Among what the attacker stole: firewall databases, passwords, configuration files, and internal authority certificates — it even issued a new certificate inside one victim's environment and actually disabled security policies, meaning the protection itself was switched off.

The attacker exploited known flaws in other products to move around inside networks, including an authentication-bypass flaw in Check Point's own controller that grants administrator privileges, which the company had warned about in July as actively exploited.

Notably, researchers believe the attacker used AI-powered attack tools, based on the speed of exploitation, the automation, and the custom tools they observed — although the use of AI itself remains unconfirmed.

Simply put: when the management system that writes your firewalls' policies is breached, the attacker has not hacked a single device but the keys to your entire network; and when tools move at machine speed, slow patching becomes more dangerous than the flaw itself.

Do you trust that your security management systems are as updated and protected as the devices they manage?
