🧠 A brain-computer interface restores arm movement after a stroke — a trial on 32 patients!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

A scientific journal published the results of a new clinical trial: training with a brain-computer interface helps recent stroke patients regain movement in the affected arm.

The trial involved 32 patients in the subacute stage after a stroke, divided into two equal groups of 16. Each group trained for 30 minutes per session, 5 sessions a week, for 4 weeks, in addition to the usual rehabilitation therapy.

In the interface group, the patient imagines moving their arm while playing a virtual swimming game; the device reads the brain signals, and when the signal is strong enough, it moves a robotic arm that helps the patient's arm move.

The second group received a sham training identical in form and duration, but its device does not respond to brain signals, so the researchers could measure the effect of the real interface alone.

The interface group achieved greater improvement on the Fugl-Meyer upper-limb movement scale and on the hand-function and movement-assessment tests, while the difference was not clear on the daily-independence scale.

MRI scans showed brain changes in the interface group, including increased grey-matter volume in the supplementary motor area and stronger connections with other areas in the cerebellum, and these changes were linked to improved movement.

The researchers stress that the results are promising but need larger trials and longer follow-up before the method is adopted as a routine treatment.

Simply put: the patient imagines the movement and the device helps carry it out, so the brain relearns movement step by step.
