🎨 'Etsy' Backs Down on Its New AI Policy After Sellers' Anger!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Handmade-goods platform "Etsy" has announced it will revise its new AI policy in its terms of use, after a wave of anger from sellers who feared for their original work. James Osman, an executive at the company, told the "New York Times" that the platform will share the revised terms directly with sellers soon.

Sellers had objected to the new terms because they feared their product photos and handmade works would be used to train AI models that imitate their work or replace their creativity. In an earlier update this week, Osman tried to reassure them, saying: "We don't use your content to train AI models designed to copy your original work or replace your creativity. And that won't change."

"Etsy" relies on independent sellers and craftspeople, whose authentic work is considered the platform's core and value, so any ambiguity about using their work to train AI raised widespread concern among them. The company has not yet revealed the full details of the revised terms, or when they will be published.

This step comes as many platforms tighten their policies around AI — some banning training models on user content, others allowing it — amid growing demands from creators to protect their work.

Simply put: "Etsy" sellers said their handmade works are not material for training AI, and the platform backed down and promised clearer terms protecting their creativity.
