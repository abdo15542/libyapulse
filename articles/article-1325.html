💊 Novartis CEO: AI Won't Cure Cancer Within 10 Years — and We Only Understand 5% of the Body!

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The chief executive of Swiss drugmaker Novartis, Vas Narasimhan — who is also a board member at Anthropic — has rejected the idea that artificial intelligence will cure cancer within five to ten years, insisting it will take a full decade just to cut four years from the drug-development process.

"The real problem is that we only understand about 5% of what goes on inside the human body," Narasimhan said in an interview published on 23 November, explaining that tech companies approach the problem "with an engineering mindset", while doctors and drug companies know how much we still don't know about the body — a model's ability to learn and predict is far higher in programming and language, where we understand a much larger share.

His stance clashes with the promises of AI leaders: Anthropic's chief executive Dario Amodei has said AI will cure cancer and Alzheimer's within a decade, and DeepMind founder Demis Hassabis said last year that he believes AI could solve all diseases within a single decade.

Novartis uses AI at every stage of developing its drugs, and its biggest gains have come in the early discovery stage before a drug is tested on humans, where it can cut up to two years from a stage that normally takes four — and it expects to shorten the full process from an average of 12 years to 8 within a decade, while raising a drug's chance of success from 8% to 12%.

But Narasimhan stressed that long clinical trials will remain essential, because drugs must be tested on animals and then on humans to verify how they work biologically, and major acceleration will only happen if regulators become convinced that we can predict how a drug behaves in the human body with high accuracy and ease trial requirements.

He saw AI's real impact as "somewhere in the middle" between the ambitious expectations of tech companies and the more sceptical view of drug companies, while Anthropic itself is investing in a drug-discovery programme, buying small biotech companies and building its own labs.

Simply put: AI designs better molecules and cuts years from early research, but it understands only 5% of the human body — a cure still needs long trials on people, not promises alone.
