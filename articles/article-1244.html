🎮 Xbox Helix was going to make multiplayer free… and open the door to Steam!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

A new report has revealed that the next generation of Xbox, known as Project Helix, was originally designed to break a rule more than twenty years old: online multiplayer behind a paid subscription.

According to the report, Helix was designed under the platform's previous leadership as a true hybrid between a PC and a console: an Xbox-style interface, full support for PC games through stores like Steam and the Epic Games Store, with the ability to exit to a Windows desktop and install software.

The logic was simple: if the device is a PC at heart, there is no point charging for multiplayer, because playing on PC is free anyway; the plan was to remove the subscription wall entirely, in a radical change to Xbox's business model since the days of Xbox Live.

Advertisement

But the plan may change: with new leadership for the gaming platform focused on profitability, and with component prices rising during the AI boom, Microsoft has become more cautious, and reports suggest the device's price could reach around $1,800.

In contrast, Microsoft described Helix at the gaming platform's 25th anniversary celebration as a "console that plays your PC games", and vice president Jason Ronald called it "the perfect gaming machine that removes all barriers to play".

Simply put: the next Xbox dreamed of being a gaming PC and a console in one device, with no subscriptions and no barriers; the question now is: will those dreams survive the component bill?

Would you prefer an open Xbox with Steam and free multiplayer even at a higher price, or a traditional console at a lower price?
