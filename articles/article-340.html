🕵️ Experts Warn: Everyone Working in AI Is a Target for the 'Honey Trap'!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Security experts have warned that people working at AI companies have become easy targets for an old trick known as the "honey trap," which involves getting close to someone with sensitive information to win their trust and then luring them into revealing their company's secrets. A former intelligence officer said "no one teaches company founders how to protect themselves from espionage," even though the most dangerous secrets today are no longer in government headquarters, but inside companies that make AI chips, models, and infrastructure.

Notably, the attacker doesn't need a love story to reach their goal; friendship itself is the oldest form of the trick. The attacker may approach their target as an investor, a client, a recruiter, or a consultant, and their requests look legitimate on paper — all they need is for the target to feel comfortable talking about their work. OpenAI itself has begun warning its employees about strangers trying to get close to them in search of company secrets, after the accusations that hit Nvidia's chief in a similar scandal that is still unfolding.

And most dangerously, AI itself has made these tricks easier and cheaper than ever: where planning such an operation once took months or years of research and building a fake persona, today AI can be used to research the target, generate fake accounts, images, and voices, and maintain a complete fictional backstory. Warning signs include "chance" meetings that happen more often than usual, constant curiosity about work and colleagues, requests to keep meetings secret, and requests that keep growing.

Simply put: imagine a stranger approaching you with a smile and interest, not to steal your password, but to get you to open the door for them yourself — the weakest guard in any company is the human, not the firewall.

Do you trust strangers who show a lot of interest in your work too quickly?
