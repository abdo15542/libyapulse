💼 Linear passes $100 million — and AI agents now write half the tasks!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Linear, the task-tracking tool for programming teams, announced it has passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue this year, with more than 40,000 paying customers.

Chief Operating Officer Christina Cordova revealed in an interview on November 19 that AI agents now create more than half of the tasks inside the tool, up from just 3% a year ago.

Today the agent opens the task itself, classifies it, assigns it and closes it, whereas a year ago it was merely an assistant writing side notes — and 95% of paid workspaces have now enabled agents.

Advertisement

The transformation began in May 2025, when the company launched its MCP server, a standard connector that lets the agent read and write the tool's data directly, then added a platform for building agents and integrations with Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and its own agent.

The other figures are no less striking: long-standing customers now spend 177% of what they used to spend, and employees sold shares worth $99 million in August at a company valuation of $2.5 billion.

One secret of the growth is that the company has never hired a single salesperson; it spread among developers by word of mouth, and it sells AI at cost, with no profit margin on tokens, earning only from subscriptions.

In technical support, AI drafts the reply — but a human decides what is finally sent.

Simply put: a small task-tracking tool has become a system in which agents do more work than humans, and humans have become supervisors of the agents' work.

Would you trust an AI agent to write your team's tasks and close them on its own?
