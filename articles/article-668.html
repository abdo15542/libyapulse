🛠️ Airbnb open-sources "Skipper" — a workflow engine that lives inside your service!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

Airbnb announced on November 10 the open-sourcing of its internal workflow engine "Skipper", which its teams have used in production for years, making it available now to all developers for free under the Apache 2.0 licence.

The idea is simple and clever: instead of relying on a separate central orchestration service to manage your operations, Skipper lives as a library inside your own service, using its existing database. You write a multi-step operation — such as publishing a listing or completing a booking — as an ordinary code class in Java or Kotlin, and the engine saves its progress and resumes it after any failure or restart, without losing what was done.

The built-in scheduler coordinates work between copies of the service via the database, so if one copy fails, another takes over the task. Skipper also allows long waits — such as waiting for human approval — without holding an execution thread, and restarts the operation from the beginning on resumption, relying on recorded save points, reusing the results of completed steps instead of repeating them.

The open-source release includes the core engine, adapters for MySQL and SQLite databases, testing tools, a management interface, and a special language for describing operations as states and transitions. The company plans to add PostgreSQL support and other databases later.

Simply put: instead of building a huge orchestration server for your operations, you can now put a small engine inside your service that remembers where it stopped and continues on its own. Will this suit Arab developers building their services in Java and Kotlin?
