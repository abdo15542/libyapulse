🔬 The 'Human Frontier' Mission Launches Worldwide on 24 November! AI Agents Test Science... and the Results Are for Everyone!

23 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The Human Frontier Mission committee announced on 21 November that its new research program will open worldwide on 24 November from the city of Seattle, putting independent AI agents to work on scientific and engineering questions that are still unsolved.

The idea rests on a clear division of labor: specialist experts write each challenge and its progress criteria, then AI agents develop candidate solutions and test them, and every result is published for public review — so researchers, engineers and institutions take part in suggesting questions, providing evidence and evaluation methods, and helping judge the results.

By the time of the announcement, the program had recorded 2,391 experiments across 54 challenges in 12 fields, with 23 studies underway. The questions range from global weather prediction, traffic signal coordination, and wind energy scheduling with energy storage, to steel's response to repeated loads and distinguishing lung cells.

The committee says the strongest model makes it possible to test ideas that would have competed for limited time and resources — instead of a researcher waiting for their turn in the lab, the agent tries the idea and publishes its result for everyone to review.

Yet there is a striking gap in the announcement: it names no committee member, no participating lab, and no model powering the agents, mentioning only the program's website and its account on the X platform.

Simply put: AI agents try solutions to hard scientific problems, human experts set the questions, and all the results are published for everyone... does science trust an experiment no human has run?

Would you believe a scientific result produced by an AI agent, or is only a human experiment enough?
