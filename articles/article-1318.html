🧠 Anthropic Brings AI 'Neurons' Back Into Focus — Has the Model's Mind Become Readable?

25 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Two researchers in Anthropic's fellows programme, Jasmine Shoun and Lisa Dunlap, published two papers on 23 November revisiting an old idea: can an AI model be understood by studying its neurons one by one?

Researchers had moved away from individual neurons for three reasons: a single neuron fires for unrelated inputs, a concept's meaning changes from one layer to another inside the model, and facts seem spread across many neurons rather than sitting in one.

The two papers changed the angle of view: instead of asking what makes a neuron fire, they asked what a neuron actually adds to the model's output, using a tool called 'J-Lens' to read what each neuron contributes, then testing their hypotheses by switching neurons off or moving their outputs to other layers.

The results were surprising: a neuron adds two unrelated concepts in only 1% of cases or fewer, the meaning of what it adds stays almost constant across most layers, and the researchers traced 18 facts in the 'Llama 3.1' model to specific neurons — even though retrieving a fact usually requires changing many neurons.

The second paper turned the theory into practical tools: precisely described neurons outperformed traditional methods at detecting harmful content without any prior training, and auditors using these insights reached 75% accuracy in detecting hidden behaviours inside models, compared with only 40% when analysing outputs alone.

Simply put: what looked like chaos inside the model's mind may have a system that can be read neuron by neuron — and if that holds, AI could be monitored from the inside, not just from its results.
