🔗 The AI giants finally come together! Unified protocols for agents that shop and pay on your behalf!

23 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The world's biggest AI companies are working together to set shared rules for AI agents — independent programs that complete tasks and make transactions on behalf of users — within a new foundation that includes OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft, so these agents speak one language instead of each working on an isolated island.

The companies are racing against time because the number of agents is exploding: one expert estimated that up to 100 billion AI agents could transact online within the next few years, while the CEO of Coinbase believes agents will drive more than 99.9% of transactions within a decade — so if these programs do not agree on shared rules, the internet could turn into chaos where one agent cannot understand another.

Each company has donated a core open-source technology to the new foundation: Anthropic donated the MCP protocol, which already runs on more than 10,000 public servers; OpenAI donated the Agents.md framework; Block added the Goose tool; and Coinbase contributed the x402 payment protocol, which has already processed more than 138 million transactions.

The step came after a warning issued last week by a governor on the US Federal Reserve board, who said major banks had faced AI agents trying to act inside customer accounts without the bank being able to verify their identity or the customer's authorization — showing that the absence of rules has become a real risk to people's money.

But experts warn against over-standardizing: the CEO of SingularityNET said unifying how agents communicate, prove their identities, and secure themselves is good, but trying to unify how agents work internally would be a mistake, because it could stifle innovation and stop every company from standing out in its own way.

Simply put: rival companies agreeing on one language for AI agents before those agents start shopping and paying on our behalf... will the shared rules succeed in making them safe?

Do you trust an AI agent that pays and shops on your behalf, or do you prefer transactions to stay in your hands alone?
