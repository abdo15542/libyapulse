✈️ It Finally Ignited! "Boom Supersonic" Fires Up the Core of Its "Sprint Core" Engine for the First Time — 6,500 RPM on the Second Try!

23 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Engineers at "Boom Supersonic" succeeded on 21 November in firing up the first prototype of the core of their in-house developed engine for the first time — a step the company's founder described as the key to producing the largest jet engine designed by a startup.

The test took place at the company's facility at the Colorado Air and Space Port in Watkins. The core ignited on the second attempt — founder and CEO Blake Scholl said the team had estimated the chance of success at under 50% — then the core spun up to 6,500 revolutions per minute in a short, deliberate run, while around 2,000 sensors gathered live data on everything happening inside it.

The core is the most important part of the engine: a six-stage high-pressure compressor and a single-stage high-pressure turbine. It is the shared foundation for two projects at once: the "Superpower" industrial turbine, rated at 42 megawatts to power AI data centres, and the "Symphony" engine, with 40,000 pounds of thrust for the "Overture" supersonic aircraft at Mach 1.7.

This achievement comes four years after the company's 2022 decision to develop its own engine, after it proved impossible to buy a ready-made engine for its aircraft. Its top priority now is the data-centre turbine, while the aircraft engine remains the next goal on the same path.

The team expects to expand the core's test range over the next two months, and reach a full, integrated engine test around the middle of next year, while the company's new "Superpower Superfactory" in Centennial, Colorado prepares an in-house foundry to cast single-crystal turbine blades — precision parts that are not sold ready-made on the market.

The company aims to deliver the first "Superpower" turbines to customers in mid-2027, producing 25 turbines a year in 2028 and 50 in 2029 — a total capacity of up to 2 gigawatts a year.

Simply put: an engine core designed and built inside the company ignited on the second attempt — could this be the engine that brings back supersonic flight and powers AI data centres at the same time?
