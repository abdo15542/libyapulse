🌱 Taiwan puts an 'efficiency card' on AI data centers!

20 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

Taiwan is working on an energy-efficiency labeling system for AI computing centers, to cut electricity consumption and carbon emissions as demand grows for processing units, servers, and cooling systems.

Under the new amendments, any new or expanded data center with contracted capacity of 5 megawatts or more must submit an 'energy use guide' before construction begins and adopt the best available technologies, with a 7-item checklist covering equipment and software choice, data management, cooling, the power system, monitoring, and overall performance. Power usage effectiveness has been adopted as an official standard: no more than 1.3 for hyperscale computing centers and 1.4 for colocation centers. This comes as the state power company needs about two years to build the high-voltage lines needed for new centers, which could delay their operation until 2028 at the earliest.

Simply put: imagine every data center carrying an efficiency card like the card on the fridge in your home; the lower the number, the less electricity it consumes, and the lighter AI centers become on the power grid and the environment.
