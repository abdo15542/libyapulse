🔆 Europe Bets €400 Million on Photonic Chips… to Break the Dominance of America and Asia!

24 November 2026 · Website · TechPulse

The European Union has allocated €400 million to a project aiming to make Europe a leader in manufacturing photonic chips — a technology that processes data with light instead of electricity. Supporters say it could do for photonic computing what the 1970s did for electronic chips.

The initiative, called "PIXEurope," is partly led by the Dutch research institute TNO, whose researcher said photonic chips are "on the verge of a breakthrough" after decades as a specialized laboratory technology. They are faster, use less energy, and run cooler than the electric chips that power everything today, from phones to AI data centers.

The move comes as American, Taiwanese, and Chinese companies dominate the chip industry. European officials said they do not want to repeat the same mistake with photonic chips, and that Europe needs to manufacture them within its own borders instead of relying on others as before.

Photonic chips are still made on silicon wafers only four inches in diameter, much smaller than those used in electronic chip manufacturing. So the team is building a 10,000-square-meter factory in the Dutch city of Eindhoven to produce six-inch wafers, set to open fully in 2030.

Europe is building on an existing strength in the field: the Dutch company ASML, maker of the photolithography machines needed to produce the latest electronic chips, sits near the new production line. Officials hope this expertise will give the continent a bigger share in a technology still small enough for any country to get ahead in.

Simply put: instead of electricity… light itself carries the data inside the chip… Will Europe succeed in catching up in the chip race through a new door?
