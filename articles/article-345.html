⚖️ India Demands More Than $150 Million in Taxes from Xiaomi — and the Case Reaches the Supreme Court!

21 November 2026 · Website · Libya Pulse

The Indian government told the Supreme Court that Xiaomi India and its former contract manufacturer "Flex" did not disclose the full details of their royalty licensing agreements until investigators began examining import transactions, and that it is seeking to recover unpaid customs duties and fines that could cost Xiaomi's business in India more than $150 million.

The case turns on a technical but very expensive question: should the royalty fees Xiaomi pays to technology partners, ranging from 2% to 5%, be counted as part of the value of imported components when calculating customs duties? A tax court in Chennai ruled in November 2025 that Xiaomi had underpaid duties over three years before 2020 by excluding these fees from the components' value, setting the unpaid amount at about $72 million — which could exceed $150 million with interest and fines if the company loses.

Xiaomi says its contract manufacturers import the components, and that royalty fees are separate transactions that owe no customs duties, and it appealed the ruling to the Supreme Court in January. But the government replied in a memo dated September 21 that the company "knew the licensing arrangements under which it paid the fees, and did not provide the full disclosure required." The government is also challenging a separate request by "Flextronics India" to overturn the court's view that contract manufacturers "willingly participated in arranging the transactions."

The case is a test of how India handles contract manufacturing, a sector that has attracted companies like "Foxconn", "Jabil", and "Tata Electronics" to expand in the country, and the ruling's impact could extend to other sectors such as pharmaceuticals and cars. Xiaomi, which holds about 12% of India's smartphone market, faces other regulatory challenges, including a proposal by the Serious Fraud Investigation Office to open an inquiry into its business model.

Simply put: when a company imports phone parts and pays fees to use the technology in them, where does the part's value end and the idea's value begin? The court's answer could change manufacturing rules across India.
